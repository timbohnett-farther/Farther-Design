The Quarterly Letter
Finishing the year with intention
What the quarter meant for long-term investors, and the planning decisions worth making before December 31.

Dear Alex,

Markets gave us plenty to talk about this fall. Headlines moved fast; the fundamentals of a good plan did not. This letter covers what we watched, what we did in portfolios, and a short list of year-end decisions that can make a real difference to your taxes and your family.

As always, reply to this email if anything here sparks a question.

On our minds
Three things from the quarter
01

Rates are easing, slowly

Cash yields are drifting down. If you're holding more cash than your plan calls for, it may be time to put some of it to work in high-quality bonds.

02

Leadership is broadening

Returns came from more corners of the market than last year. A reminder of why we hold a diversified mix instead of chasing last year's winners.

03

We harvested losses where it helped

During October's dip we realized losses in taxable accounts and kept you invested, banking tax savings you can use against future gains.

Planning calendar
Year-end deadlines to know
Not every item applies to you. We'll flag the ones that do at your review.
Dec 15

Charitable gifts of appreciated stock

Transfers can take time to settle. Start early to count it this year.

Dec 31

Required minimum distributions

If you're 73 or older, take this year's RMD, or give it directly to charity.

Dec 31

Roth conversions

A lower-income year can be a window to convert at a modest tax rate.

Apr 15

IRA and HSA contributions for 2026

You have until the filing deadline, but earlier dollars get more time invested.
