Tax-loss harvesting
Harvesting losses throughout the year rather than only at year-end
Most investors only think about tax-loss harvesting in December, but volatility creates harvestable losses throughout the year, and waiting until year-end means missing most of them.
How it works
Markets move constantly, which means loss opportunities appear and disappear all year. A February dip, a summer pullback, or a single stock's earnings miss can all create a harvestable loss long before December arrives, and by the time year-end comes, many of those losses may have already recovered and disappeared.
Step 1
Monitor all taxable positions continuously for unrealized losses, not just in Q4.
Step 2
Harvest a loss as soon as it's meaningful, rather than waiting to see if it recovers.
Step 3
Reinvest in a similar, not identical, holding to maintain market exposure and avoid a wash sale.
Step 4
Bank the loss to offset future gains or ordinary income, tracked across the full tax year.
The cost of waiting
Suppose a $10,000 loss opportunity appears in March but recovers by June. Harvesting it immediately versus waiting for a year-end review produces very different outcomes.

Tax-loss harvesting beyond December · fit and reference
Who this is designed for
Investors with taxable brokerage accounts holding diversified positions
Households that have historically only reviewed harvesting opportunities once a year
Anyone who has watched a loss opportunity disappear before December arrived
Key benefits at a glance
Captures losses before they disappear in a recovery
Offsets an unlimited amount of realized capital gains during the year
Reduces ordinary income up to $3,000/yr, with unlimited carry-forward
Keeps the portfolio fully invested throughout the process
Important considerations
Wash-sale risk
Repurchasing a substantially identical security within 30 days disallows the loss.
Missed windows
Losses harvested too late may have already recovered, eliminating the opportunity.
Household coordination
A spouse's account or an IRA purchase can inadvertently trigger a wash sale.
