The best days won’t wait for you
A hypothetical $10,000 invested in the S&P 500 from 1996 through 2025 grew to $192,167 if held through every trading day — but only $85,490 if the ten single best days were missed.

Hypothetical illustration. The “missed best days” scenarios assume the investor was out of the market only on those specific trading days. 48% of the 50 best days occurred during a bear market; another 28% in the first two months of a bull market.
“Far more money has been lost by investors preparing for corrections, or trying to anticipate corrections, than has been lost in corrections themselves.”
Peter Lynch
Why this matters
Thirty years holds about 7,500 trading days. Ten of them accounted for more than half of the total gain. No one has a reliable way to know which ten they will be, which is why staying invested does the heavy lifting.
