Dividends, the quiet engine of wealth
Since 1960, $10,000 in the S&P 500 grew to $7.58 million with dividends reinvested versus $1.14 million on price appreciation alone — 85% of the total gain came from dividends and the compounding they made possible.

Why this matters
A 1% or 2% dividend looks like a rounding error next to a 20% up year. But reinvested, it buys shares that pay their own dividends, which buy more shares. Over 66 years that loop produced 6.6 times more ending wealth than price gains alone.
