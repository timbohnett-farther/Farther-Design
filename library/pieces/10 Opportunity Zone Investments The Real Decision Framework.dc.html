Tax planning
Which tax break still exists, and for which vintage
A Qualified Opportunity Fund lets you defer eligible gain by investing it into a fund that holds Qualified Opportunity Zone property. The mechanics are strict, the calendar matters, and the fund still has to be worth owning on its own merits.
How it works
Under current IRS guidance, eligible gains generally must be recognized for federal income tax purposes before January 1, 2027, cannot come from a related-party transaction, and must be invested within 180 days in exchange for an equity interest rather than debt.
Step 1
Confirm the gain is eligible, including capital gain and certain Section 1231 gain, and not from a related party.
Step 2
Underwrite the sponsor, asset mix, and hold period before the tax label enters the conversation.
Step 3
Invest within 180 days for an equity interest in the QOF, never debt.
Step 4
Elect deferral on Form 8949, then file Form 8997 every year the investment is held.

A $2,000,000 realized eligible gain, taxed at the illustrated federal rate, shows the trade-off between paying tax now and keeping that capital invested.

Qualified Opportunity Funds · fit and reference
When a QOF is most defensible
You already have a realized eligible gain
You can meet the 180-day deadline
You understand the annual filing requirements
You are willing to underwrite the actual project or fund rather than chase the tax label
When it becomes less attractive
The tax deferral is small relative to the investment risk
Liquidity is needed before the strategy matures
The sponsor's compliance discipline is weak
The gain would be recognized after the legacy deferral window closes
Three buckets to keep separate
Legacy pre-2027 deferral
Gain recognized before January 1, 2027, with inclusion on the earlier of an inclusion event or December 31, 2026.
Post-2026 program
Investments made under the revised framework, with redesignation and expanded reporting following the original regime.
Sponsor-level compliance
Fund qualification, substantial improvement, and maintenance of QOF status over time under the final regulations.

How Farther approaches this
The right question is rarely whether a gain should go into a QOF. It is whether this specific fund, with this sponsor, asset mix, reporting discipline, and hold period, justifies using the Opportunity Zone rules at all. Because IRS resources now point to both the final regulations and 2026 transitional guidance, each investment is analyzed by date and structure rather than by headline.
