Same returns, opposite retirements
Three retirees start with $1 million, withdraw 4% adjusted for inflation, and earn the same 5% average annual return over 30 years — the only difference is the order the returns arrive, and it decides whether one of them runs out of money.

Why this matters
While you are saving, the order of returns barely matters. Once you are spending, it matters enormously. A weak market early forces you to sell more shares to fund the same lifestyle, and those shares never come back to recover.
