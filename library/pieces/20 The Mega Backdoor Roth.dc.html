Retirement accounts
Using after-tax employer-plan contributions and in-plan conversions where available
For employees whose 401(k) plan allows after-tax contributions and in-plan Roth conversions, a mega backdoor Roth strategy can allow significantly more Roth savings than the standard IRA or 401(k) Roth contribution limits permit.
How it works
Beyond the standard pre-tax or Roth 401(k) contribution limit, some plans allow additional after-tax contributions up to the overall plan limit. If the plan permits in-plan conversions or in-service withdrawals, those after-tax contributions can be converted to Roth relatively quickly, minimizing taxable growth before conversion.
Step 1
Confirm the employer plan allows after-tax contributions beyond the standard deferral limit.
Step 2
Confirm the plan also allows in-plan Roth conversions or in-service withdrawals.
Step 3
Contribute after-tax dollars up to the overall plan limit, after accounting for other contributions and match.
Step 4
Convert those after-tax contributions to Roth promptly, minimizing taxable growth before conversion.
