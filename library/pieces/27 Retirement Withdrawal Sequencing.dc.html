Retirement accounts
Coordinating taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, and charitable-account withdrawals
The order in which retirement income is drawn from taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, and charitable accounts can meaningfully affect lifetime tax liability, and the optimal order often isn't simply 'taxable first, tax-deferred last.'
How it works
A naive approach drains taxable accounts first, then tax-deferred, then Roth last. But this can leave large tax-deferred balances to compound into an even larger future RMD problem. A more sophisticated sequence blends withdrawals across account types each year, filling tax brackets efficiently and sometimes drawing from multiple sources simultaneously.
Step 1
Project income needs and available account balances across the retirement horizon.
Step 2
Model withdrawal sequences that fill tax brackets efficiently each year, rather than draining one account type at a time.
Step 3
Integrate Roth conversions where they reduce lifetime tax liability.
Step 4
Adjust the sequence annually as income, tax law, and account balances change.

Important considerations
Sequential draining
Draining taxable accounts first without considering the future RMD consequences on tax-deferred balances.
Ignoring conversions
Failing to consider blending Roth conversions into the withdrawal sequence.
One-time plan
Treating the sequencing decision as a one-time plan rather than an annual recalibration.
