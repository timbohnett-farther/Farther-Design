Avoiding unexpected Medicare premium surcharges after large income events
Medicare's Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA) surcharge is based on income from two years prior, meaning a large income event today, a Roth conversion, a business sale, or a big capital gain, can trigger an unexpected premium increase two years from now.
How it works
Because IRMAA looks back two years, the connection between an income decision and its Medicare premium consequence is easy to miss in the moment. A household celebrating a successful business sale or executing a large Roth conversion may not realize until two years later that their Medicare premiums have increased substantially as a direct result.
Step 1
A large income event occurs in a given tax year.
Step 2
That year's income is reported and becomes part of the IRS record.
Step 3
Two years later, Medicare uses that reported income to determine the IRMAA surcharge.
Step 4
The higher premium applies for the full year, regardless of the household's current income.

Anyone approaching or already on Medicare who is considering a Roth conversion or other large income event

Key benefits at a glance
Prevents unexpected Medicare premium increases from catching a household off guard
Allows large income events to be timed and sized more strategically
May identify appeal opportunities after certain life-changing events
Integrates seamlessly into broader multi-year tax planning
Important considerations
No forward modeling
Executing a large income event without modeling its IRMAA impact two years later.
Missed appeal
Failing to explore an IRMAA appeal after a qualifying life-changing event, such as retirement or loss of income.
Timing confusion
Assuming current income, rather than income from two years prior, determines this year's premium.
