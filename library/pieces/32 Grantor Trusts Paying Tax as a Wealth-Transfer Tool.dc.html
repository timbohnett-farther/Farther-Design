Gifting and estate planning
How the grantor's payment of trust income tax can preserve trust assets
When a grantor trust's income tax is paid personally by the grantor rather than by the trust, that tax payment is not treated as a taxable gift, effectively allowing the grantor to transfer additional wealth to the trust's beneficiaries tax-free every year.
How it works
A grantor trust is structured so its income is taxed to the grantor personally, not the trust. Because the grantor is legally obligated to pay that tax, doing so is not considered an additional gift to the trust, even though it effectively shifts additional wealth to the trust's beneficiaries each year the grantor's personal assets shrink by the tax paid, while the trust's assets compound undiminished.
Step 1
Establish a properly structured grantor trust, typically for estate planning purposes.
Step 2
The trust generates taxable income each year from its investments.
Step 3
The grantor pays that income tax personally, using funds outside the trust.
Step 4
The trust's assets continue compounding without reduction for income tax, effectively transferring additional value to beneficiaries.

Who this is designed for
Grantors of irrevocable grantor trusts, such as SLATs, IDGTs, or dynasty trusts
High-net-worth households using grantor trust structures for wealth transfer
Anyone whose estate planning includes grantor trust income tax obligations
Key benefits at a glance
Transfers additional wealth to trust beneficiaries without using any gift tax exemption
Allows the trust's assets to compound undiminished by income tax
Reduces the grantor's own taxable estate through the tax payments
Compounds meaningfully over the life of a long-term trust
Important considerations
Toggling off status
Inadvertently or unnecessarily terminating grantor trust status, ending this benefit.
Cash flow strain
Failing to plan for the grantor's ongoing personal cash flow needs to pay the trust's tax liability.
Underappreciating the benefit
Overlooking this as a deliberate, sanctioned wealth transfer tool rather than simply a tax obligation.
