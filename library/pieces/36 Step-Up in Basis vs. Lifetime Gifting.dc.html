Gifting and estate planning
Comparing the transfer-tax benefit of a gift with potential loss of basis adjustment
Gifting an appreciated asset during life transfers the donor's original cost basis to the recipient, while the same asset, if left to be inherited instead, generally receives a step-up in basis to its value at death, a critical trade-off in deciding whether to gift now or wait.
How it works
A highly appreciated asset gifted during life carries the donor's low original cost basis forward to the recipient, who will owe capital gains tax on that full appreciation when eventually sold. The same asset, left to be inherited instead, generally receives a basis step-up to its date-of-death value, erasing that embedded gain entirely, a major consideration when low-basis assets are involved in estate planning.
Step 1
Identify the asset's current cost basis and fair market value.
Step 2
Compare the potential future capital gains tax if gifted now, using carryover basis, against the outcome if inherited later, with a stepped-up basis.
Step 3
Weigh this basis consideration against the benefit of removing the asset's future appreciation from the taxable estate through an earlier gift.
Step 4
Decide gift timing asset by asset, since low-basis, highly appreciated assets favor holding, while cash or high-basis assets favor gifting freely.

Important considerations
Blanket gifting
Gifting all appreciated assets during life without considering the lost step-up benefit.
Estate size oversight
Failing to consider whether the estate is even likely to be subject to estate tax, which changes the calculus significantly.
Cash flow assumption
Assuming lifetime gifting is always superior without comparing it to the specific step-up benefit available at death.
