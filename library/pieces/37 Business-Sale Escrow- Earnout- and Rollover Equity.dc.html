Liquidity and business transitions
Understanding the difference between headline sale price and usable liquidity
A business sale's headline price rarely equals the cash a founder can actually use immediately, escrow holdbacks, earnout contingencies, and rollover equity requirements can each significantly reduce near-term liquidity.
How it works
A headline sale price might be $50 million, but after a 15% escrow holdback, a partially achieved earnout, and a 20% rollover equity requirement, the cash actually available at closing can be substantially less, a critical distinction for post-sale planning around taxes, gifting, and diversification.
Step 1
Identify the headline sale price and each deal term reducing immediate liquidity.
Step 2
Model the escrow holdback and its expected release timeline.
Step 3
Model the earnout structure and realistic probability of achieving its targets.
Step 4
Account for any required rollover equity, which remains illiquid and at risk in the new ownership structure.

Who this is designed for

Key benefits at a glance
Prevents overcommitting to spending or gifting based on the headline price alone
Enables more accurate tax and cash flow planning around the actual liquidity timeline
Clarifies which portions of proceeds remain at risk through rollover equity or earnout contingency
Supports better-informed negotiation of deal terms before signing
Important considerations
Headline anchoring
Planning finances based on the full headline price rather than realistic usable liquidity.
Earnout overconfidence
Assuming full earnout achievement without stress-testing the underlying assumptions.
Rollover risk blind spot
Treating rollover equity as equivalent to cash rather than as a new, illiquid, at-risk investment.
