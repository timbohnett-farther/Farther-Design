Liquidity and business transitions
Sequencing tax, estate, liquidity, charitable, and governance decisions before a sale
Many of the most valuable tax and estate planning strategies available to a founder, like gifting shares or establishing certain trusts, must be completed before a sale is imminent, making a structured 12-month pre-sale checklist essential.
How it works
Once a sale process is well underway, particularly after a letter of intent is signed, many planning opportunities close off or become significantly riskier from a tax perspective. Gifting shares to a trust or family members, for example, is far more defensible for valuation purposes when done well before a sale is imminent, rather than once a deal is essentially certain.
Step 1
Begin the planning process at least 12 months before an anticipated sale, ideally earlier.
Step 2
Evaluate and execute any gifting or trust funding strategies while valuation is not yet influenced by a pending deal.
Step 3
Coordinate QSBS eligibility verification, charitable planning, and entity structuring well ahead of the transaction.
Step 4
Finalize liquidity and tax payment planning as the deal approaches closing.

Pre-Sale Planning: The 12-Month Founder Checklist · fit and reference
Who this is designed for
Founders and business owners anticipating a sale within the next one to two years
Anyone wanting to preserve gifting, trust, and charitable planning opportunities before a deal is imminent
Households needing to coordinate multiple advisors ahead of a major liquidity event
Key benefits at a glance
Preserves access to planning strategies that become unavailable or risky closer to a sale
Improves the defensibility of any pre-sale gifting or trust funding valuations
Reduces the chance of a rushed, reactive planning process during the deal itself
Coordinates tax, estate, and liquidity planning into one integrated timeline
Important considerations
Waiting too long
Beginning planning only after a deal process is already underway, foreclosing many strategies.
Uncoordinated advisors
Failing to coordinate tax, legal, and wealth advisors early enough to execute a cohesive plan.
Missed QSBS verification
Neglecting to verify QSBS eligibility well before a sale, when documentation is easier to establish.
