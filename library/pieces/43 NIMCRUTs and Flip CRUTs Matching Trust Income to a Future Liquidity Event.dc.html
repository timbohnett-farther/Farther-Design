Advanced trust structures
Net-income CRUTs and "flip" provisions for illiquid assets such as private-company stock, private equity, or real estate
A NIMCRUT limits payments to the trust's actual net income, useful when the contributed asset is illiquid and can't easily be sold or distributed from, while a flip CRUT is designed to automatically convert to a standard unitrust once a specific triggering event, like a company sale, occurs.
How it works
Contributing illiquid assets like private company stock or real estate to a standard CRUT can create a problem, the trust may owe a distribution it doesn't have the liquid cash to pay. A NIMCRUT solves this by limiting payouts to actual net income until the asset generates cash or is sold, and a flip CRUT provision can convert the trust to a standard, more generous unitrust payout once that liquidity event occurs.
Step 1
Contribute an illiquid asset, such as private company stock, to a NIMCRUT or flip CRUT.
Step 2
Prior to the triggering event, the trust pays only its actual net income, which may be minimal.
Step 3
A defined triggering event occurs, such as the sale of the underlying private company.
Step 4
The trust automatically flips to a standard unitrust structure, paying the full percentage-based unitrust amount going forward.

Who this is designed for
Owners of illiquid assets like pre-IPO stock, private equity interests, or undeveloped real estate
Founders anticipating a future liquidity event who want to pre-fund a charitable income vehicle
Anyone wanting to avoid a CRUT structure that could force an untimely or unfavorable sale
Key benefits at a glance
Avoids forcing a premature or unfavorable sale of an illiquid contributed asset
Aligns the trust's payment obligations with the asset's actual liquidity timeline
Automatically transitions to a more generous payout once liquidity is achieved
Preserves the charitable and tax deferral benefits of a standard CRUT once liquid
Important considerations
Wrong vehicle for liquid assets
Using a NIMCRUT or flip CRUT for an already-liquid asset, when a standard CRUT would suffice.
Undefined flip trigger
Failing to clearly define the triggering event in the trust document, creating ambiguity.
Underestimating pre-flip income need
Assuming the net income payout will meet the beneficiary's needs before the flip occurs.
