Sale of appreciating assets to an Intentionally Defective Grantor Trust in exchange for a promissory note
An Intentionally Defective Grantor Trust (IDGT) sale lets a grantor sell an appreciating asset to the trust in exchange for a promissory note, freezing the asset's value in the grantor's estate at the note's value while all future appreciation above the note's interest rate accrues to the trust's beneficiaries.
How it works
Because an IDGT is a grantor trust, the sale between the grantor and the trust is disregarded for income tax purposes, no capital gains tax is triggered on the sale itself. The grantor receives a note bearing a modest AFR-based interest rate, and the asset's value is effectively frozen in the grantor's estate at the note's face value, while all appreciation beyond the note's interest rate benefits the trust's beneficiaries.
Step 1
Fund the IDGT with an initial 'seed' gift, typically at least 10% of the intended purchase price.
Step 2
Sell the appreciating asset to the trust in exchange for a promissory note bearing an AFR-based interest rate.
Step 3
The grantor receives note payments over the note's term, with no capital gains tax recognized on the sale.
Step 4
Any appreciation of the asset above the note's interest rate remains in the trust for beneficiaries.

IDGT Sales: Freezing an Estate With an Installment Note · fit and reference
Who this is designed for
Owners of assets expected to appreciate significantly faster than current AFR-based interest rates
UHNW households seeking to freeze estate value while transferring future growth to heirs
Anyone with sufficient other assets to fund the required seed gift and support the note obligation
Key benefits at a glance
Freezes the asset's value in the grantor's estate at the note's face value
Transfers future appreciation above the note rate to trust beneficiaries
Avoids capital gains tax on the sale itself, due to grantor trust status
Can be paired with the grantor's payment of the trust's income tax for additional wealth transfer benefit
Important considerations
Insufficient seed gift
Failing to fund an adequate seed gift before the sale, which can undermine the note's validity as a bona fide sale.
Overestimating growth
Selling an asset that fails to outperform the note's interest rate, providing little transfer benefit.
Note default risk
Structuring a note the trust cannot realistically service from the asset's cash flow or eventual sale.
