Life-insurance structure that may provide tax-deferred growth and estate-planning utility, subject to investor-control, diversification, liquidity, expense, and suitability constraints
Private Placement Life Insurance wraps an institutionally managed investment portfolio inside a life insurance policy, potentially allowing tax-deferred, and in some cases tax-free at death, growth, subject to strict diversification, investor-control, and suitability requirements.
How it works
By holding institutional-style investment strategies inside a life insurance policy structure, PPLI can defer taxation on the portfolio's growth and, if structured properly and held until death, potentially avoid income tax on that growth entirely for beneficiaries. However, strict diversification and investor-control rules must be followed, and the structure carries its own costs, liquidity constraints, and suitability requirements that must be carefully evaluated.
Step 1
Confirm investor suitability, typically requiring accredited investor or qualified purchaser status.
Step 2
Fund a PPLI policy, which invests in a diversified pool of institutional-style investment strategies.
Step 3
The underlying investments grow tax-deferred inside the policy structure.
Step 4
At death, the policy's proceeds may pass to beneficiaries income-tax-free, subject to proper structuring and compliance.

Who this is designed for
Accredited investors or qualified purchasers with a long time horizon and significant taxable investment assets
UHNW households seeking tax-efficient exposure to institutional-style investment strategies
Anyone comfortable with the cost structure and diversification requirements unique to PPLI
Key benefits at a glance
Provides tax-deferred growth on an institutionally managed investment portfolio
May allow income-tax-free proceeds to beneficiaries if held until death
Offers access to investment strategies not typically available in a standard insurance product
Can complement other estate and tax planning structures for eligible investors
Important considerations
Investor-control violations
Retaining impermissible control over specific investment decisions within the policy, jeopardizing its tax treatment.
Diversification failures
Failing to maintain the required diversification standards within the underlying investment pool.
Suitability mismatch
Pursuing PPLI without meeting the sophistication, liquidity, and cost tolerance the structure requires.
