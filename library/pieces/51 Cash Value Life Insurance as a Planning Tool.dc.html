Insurance planning
The structure has to be worth its own cost
Cash value life insurance is often discussed either as a magic solution or as something to avoid entirely. Both views are incomplete. The product may have a legitimate role when the planning objective is specific enough, and becomes problematic when used to solve a simpler problem.
How it works
A portion of premium may build value inside the policy over time, subject to charges, contract design, and carrier performance. Access may be available through withdrawals or loans, but those features should not be treated as free money.
Step 1
State the objective: lifetime coverage, estate liquidity, or trust funding.
Step 2
Test whether term plus investing solves the same problem more cheaply.
Step 3
Confirm you can fund the contract for its full intended life.
Step 4
Model loans and withdrawals before relying on policy liquidity.

The real planning question
The comparison is rarely product versus product. It is whether the household values lifetime coverage and policy-based liquidity enough to justify the cost and complexity relative to separate insurance and investment tools.

Cash value policies · fit and reference
When the structure can be justified
Lifetime coverage is genuinely required, not merely preferred
Estate liquidity or trust funding depends on a policy staying in force
Other tax-advantaged capacity is already fully used
The household can fund the contract through a bad decade without straining
What can go wrong
Early-year charges mean surrender in the first years usually destroys value
Policy loans and withdrawals reduce cash value and death benefit, and can cause a lapse
Overfunding can create a modified endowment contract and change the tax treatment
A policy abandoned partway through never delivers what the illustration promised
Three mechanics to understand before signing
Loans
Borrowed against cash value, accruing interest. Unpaid loans reduce the death benefit and can lapse the policy.
Withdrawals
Reduce cash value directly and may be taxable above basis, depending on the contract.
Lapse risk
If charges outpace funding, the contract can fail late in life, when coverage matters most.
