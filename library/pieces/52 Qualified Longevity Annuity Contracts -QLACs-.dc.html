Retirement income · client education guide
Retirement income
Insuring the years most people underestimate
A QLAC is not a broad annuity category. It is a highly specific retirement planning tool with defined tax rules. Its job is narrow but meaningful: use part of qualified retirement assets to buy income that begins later in life.
How it works
Because eligible dollars used to purchase the QLAC can reduce the IRA balance counted for required distribution purposes, subject to the applicable rules and limits, some retirees use it to reshape the timing of taxable retirement income.
Step 1
Confirm the account and contract are eligible, and check the current dollar limit.
Step 2
Decide the income start age, commonly 80 or 85 depending on the contract.
Step 3
Model the effect on required distributions in the years before income begins.
Step 4
Compare it against laddered bonds, deferred income planning, and Roth conversions.

What it does not do
A QLAC does not make taxes disappear or solve a weak retirement plan. It changes the pattern of income and may improve resilience late in life if that specific problem matters to the household.

QLACs · fit and reference
When a QLAC fits
Longevity is a genuine concern given health and family history
Required distributions are pushing taxable income higher than the plan wants
The household has enough other liquidity to give up access to the carved-out amount
A guaranteed floor under late-life spending has real value to you
What you give up
Liquidity on the amount used, which is generally not accessible before income begins
Eligibility limits: not all retirement accounts or annuity contracts qualify
Dollar caps set by the IRS, which limit how much of the balance can be carved out
Rule risk, since future legislative or regulatory changes could affect the treatment
Three problems it addresses, in order
Longevity risk
Income that begins at an advanced age insures against outliving other resources.
Distribution pressure
Carving out eligible dollars can reduce the balance the required distribution is calculated on.
Sequencing
Lower early distributions can leave room for conversions or lower-bracket years.

How Farther approaches this
Farther evaluates whether a QLAC belongs in the income plan by comparing it with laddered bonds, deferred income planning, Roth conversion strategy, and other ways to manage later-life spending and distribution pressure. The right answer depends on longevity expectations, liquidity needs, tax brackets, and how much guaranteed income the household already has.
