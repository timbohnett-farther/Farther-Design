Wealth protection
Prevention is the only reliable defense, because recovery is not one
High-net-worth households face fraud risks that go beyond typical investment scams: account takeover, wire fraud impersonating a trusted advisor or family member, identity theft leveraging publicly visible wealth, and social engineering aimed at household staff or family members with account access. These attacks are increasingly sophisticated and specifically target the complexity that comes with significant wealth.
Where the real exposure comes from
Wire fraud through impersonation
Fraudsters increasingly impersonate an advisor, attorney, or family member via a spoofed or compromised email to request an urgent wire transfer, often around real transactions like a real estate closing.
Publicly visible wealth creates a target profile
Property records, business filings, and social media can allow criminals to build a detailed profile of a household's wealth and family structure, informing more convincing, targeted scams.
Household staff and family members are common entry points
Assistants, bookkeepers, or family members with account access may have less security awareness than the principal, making them an easier target for social engineering.
The verification standard
Voice
Confirm every wire verbally, on a number you already had
Never email
Instructions arriving or changing by email are the attack
Always
No exceptions for urgency, closings, or a familiar name

Illustrative wire fraud scenario
A household closing on a property purchase receives updated wire instructions the day before funding.
Amount wired in good faith
$850,000
Typically recoverable once wired
Uncommon
Window in which recovery is possible
Hours
Once funds are wired to a fraudulent account in a scheme like this, recovery is uncommon and typically only possible if caught within hours, making prevention, not recovery, the only reliable defense. Hypothetical illustration only.
How the scheme runs
Step
What happens
1. Reconnaissance
Fraudster identifies that the household is closing on a property purchase, often via public records
2. Impersonation
Fraudster sends a spoofed email appearing to be from the title company or attorney with "updated" wire instructions
3. Execution
Household wires $850,000 in good faith to the fraudulent account
4. Result
Funds are typically unrecoverable once wired to the fraudulent account, often moved within hours
The verification checklist
Before any wire leaves: confirm the instructions verbally, on a phone number you already had rather than one supplied in the message; treat any change to previously issued instructions as an attack until proven otherwise; and never rely on email alone, however familiar the sender appears. Business email compromise schemes caused $3.04 billion in reported U.S. losses in 2025 alone (FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center), with high-net-worth households an increasingly common target.

How Farther approaches this
Farther evaluates this risk as part of the full financial plan, coordinating with tax advisors, attorneys, and other professionals where appropriate so the strategy fits the broader plan rather than standing alone. In practice that means a standing verification protocol for every transfer, defined access for staff and family, and a plan for what happens in the first hour if something goes wrong.
