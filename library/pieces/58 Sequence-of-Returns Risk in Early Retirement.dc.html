Retirement risk · client education guide
Retirement risk
The order of returns matters more than the average
Two retirees can experience the exact same average annual return over 25 years and end up with dramatically different outcomes, one running out of money and the other thriving, simply because of the order in which the returns occurred. A market decline in the first few years of retirement withdrawals does lasting damage that the same decline occurring mid-career would not.
Why the order of returns matters more than the average
Withdrawing while declining compounds the damage
During accumulation, a market decline just means buying more shares at lower prices. During retirement withdrawals, a decline combined with a required distribution means selling more shares at lower prices, permanently reducing the number of shares left to recover when the market rebounds.
The first five to ten years matter disproportionately
Once a portfolio has been depleted by early losses combined with withdrawals, even a strong market recovery later cannot fully repair the damage, because there is less capital left to participate in the recovery.
The danger zone
Years 1–5
Highest sensitivity. Losses here are compounded by withdrawals.
Years 6–10
Still elevated. The base has not yet had time to rebuild.
Year 11+
Sequence risk falls as the portfolio and horizon settle.

Both retirees experienced the same average annual return over 25 years, but Retiree B ran out of money roughly six years before the end of the period simply because the losses arrived early, while withdrawals continued on schedule. Retiree B's first five years were −15%, −8%, +3%, −10%, and +5%. Hypothetical illustration only.

What this changes about planning
The same 7% average return over 25 years can leave one retiree with nothing and another with more than $2 million, depending only on the order of returns. That is not an argument for predicting markets, which is not possible. It is an argument for building the first decade of retirement so that a bad opening does not force selling into it.
