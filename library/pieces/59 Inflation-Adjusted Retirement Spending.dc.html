Retirement risk · client education guide
Retirement risk
One inflation number is not enough
Most retirement projections apply one blended inflation assumption to all future spending. In reality, healthcare and long-term care costs have historically risen faster than general consumer inflation, while other categories like a fixed mortgage may not inflate at all. A plan that looks conservative using a flat 3% assumption can be materially underfunded once category-specific inflation is applied.
Why one inflation number is not enough
Spending categories inflate at different rates
Housing costs may be fixed or slow-growing if a mortgage is paid off, while healthcare, long-term care, and certain discretionary categories can rise faster than general inflation over a multi-decade retirement.
The gap compounds over a long retirement
A modest difference in assumed inflation rate for healthcare specifically, compounded over 20 to 30 years of retirement, can create a meaningfully larger real spending need than a single blended rate would suggest.

Why this shows up late
A flat assumption is not wrong in year one. The two projections are nearly identical early, which is exactly why the gap goes unnoticed: it opens slowly and then compounds. By the years when healthcare spending is heaviest, the plan built on a blended rate is short by the amount it needed most.

How Farther approaches this
Farther evaluates this risk as part of the full financial plan, coordinating with tax advisors, attorneys, and other professionals where appropriate so the strategy fits the broader plan rather than standing alone. In practice that means projecting each spending category at its own rate, funding the healthcare line deliberately, and revisiting the assumptions as the actual spending pattern emerges.
