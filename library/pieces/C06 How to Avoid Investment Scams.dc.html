Wealth protection
Guaranteed high returns with no risk do not exist
Investment scams run from classic Ponzi schemes to cryptocurrency fraud and pig-butchering schemes, using pressure, promises of guaranteed returns, and fabricated urgency. AI-generated voices and deepfakes impersonating trusted contacts make them harder to spot than ever.
How it works
Nearly every legitimate investment carries risk and takes time to grow. Any offer promising guaranteed high returns with little or no risk should be treated as an immediate red flag.
Step 1
Slow down. Urgency is a tactic, not a feature of good investments.
Step 2
Verify the person and the offering through BrokerCheck and SEC registration.
Step 3
Ask exactly how the investment generates its returns, in plain terms.
Step 4
Call your advisor before any funds move, especially by wire, crypto, or gift card.
Seeing it in action
A retiree receives an unsolicited call promising a guaranteed 15% annual return on a private investment, with pressure to wire funds within 24 hours.

Investment fraud · fit and reference
Warning signs to watch for
Promises of guaranteed or unusually high returns with little or no risk
Pressure to invest quickly, before you can research or consult an advisor
Requests for payment by wire transfer, cryptocurrency, or gift cards
Unregistered investments or unlicensed individuals soliciting your money
Vague or overly complex explanations of how returns are actually generated
What staying vigilant protects
Your life savings, from losses that are effectively irreversible
Your decision-making, by slowing down high-pressure situations
Your household against AI-driven scams that increasingly target older investors
Your credentials habit: verifying anyone before working with them
Three ways to verify before you act
BrokerCheck
FINRA's brokercheck.finra.org shows registration, licensing, and disciplinary history.
SEC and state regulators
investor.gov and your state securities regulator confirm whether an offering is registered.
Your own advisor
A fiduciary, fee-based advisor has no incentive to pressure you, and can sanity-check anything.
