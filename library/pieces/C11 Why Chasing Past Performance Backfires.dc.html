Investor behavior
Buying the winner usually means buying the peak
Chasing performance means buying an investment because it recently did well, assuming the trend continues, often without asking whether it still fits the strategy. Markets and sectors rotate, and the leader in one year is frequently among the laggards in the next.
How it works
Past performance is one of the weakest predictors of future returns, a lesson consistent enough that every fund prospectus is required to state it.
Step 1
Ask what changed in the investment, not what changed in its price.
Step 2
Test the idea against your existing strategy and target allocation.
Step 3
Check whether the run-up has stretched valuations rather than improved fundamentals.
Step 4
If it still fits, size it deliberately rather than at the level enthusiasm suggests.

Does it fit?
Whether the holding has a role in the target allocation, independent of its recent return.
What is priced in?
Whether the run-up reflects improved fundamentals or simply higher valuations.
What would make me sell?
