Tax-advantaged saving
The only account with three tax advantages at once
An HSA is available to those enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan. Contributions are deductible, growth is tax-free, and withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free. No other account offers all three.
How it works
HSAs have no use-it-or-lose-it rule. Unlike flexible spending accounts, unused balances roll over indefinitely and many providers allow the balance to be invested rather than held in cash.
Step 1
Confirm your health plan qualifies as a high-deductible plan.
Step 2
Contribute to the annual limit where cash flow allows.
Step 3
Invest the balance rather than leaving it in cash, if the provider permits.
Step 4
Pay current medical costs out of pocket and keep the receipts.

HSAs · fit and reference
What the triple advantage means
Contributions are tax-deductible, reducing current taxable income
Growth within the account is completely tax-free
Qualified medical withdrawals are tax-free as well
After 65, funds can be withdrawn for any purpose, taxed as ordinary income if not medical
Unused balances roll over year to year and can be invested
What the strategy requires
Enrollment in a qualifying high-deductible health plan
Cash flow to pay current medical costs out of pocket
A provider that permits investing the balance rather than holding cash
Records of qualified expenses, which support tax-free withdrawals later
Three tax breaks in one account
Going in
Contributions are deductible, lowering taxable income in the year they are made.
While invested
Growth accumulates with no annual tax on interest, dividends, or gains.
Coming out
Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are tax-free, at any age.
