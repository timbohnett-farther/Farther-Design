Cost of investing · client education guide
Cost of investing
Returns are uncertain. Fees are not
Investment costs arrive in several forms: expense ratios charged by funds, advisory fees charged on assets, and transaction costs per trade. Small percentages compound significantly over long horizons.
How it works
Lower fees do not automatically mean a better investment. Knowing exactly what you pay, and what you receive for it, is what makes the value judgment possible.
Step 1
Total the advisory fee and the underlying fund expenses, not just one of them.
Step 2
Look for embedded costs such as sales loads and 12b-1 fees.
Step 3
Compare the all-in figure against what the relationship actually delivers.
Step 4
Substitute lower-cost alternatives only where they fit the same strategy.

Fund expense ratios that never appear as a line item on a statement
Sales loads charged at purchase or redemption
12b-1 marketing fees embedded in certain share classes
Transaction costs on frequently traded strategies
Three layers of cost
Expense ratio
The annual percentage a fund charges to manage the money, deducted from returns before you see them.
Advisory fee
What you pay for advice, typically a percentage of assets, disclosed and billed directly.
Transaction costs
Charged per trade, and higher on strategies that turn the portfolio over frequently.
