Investing fundamentals
Time does the work that contributions cannot
Compounding is returns generating their own returns. You earn not only on the original contribution but on the growing balance, original money plus every prior gain.
How it works
The earlier you start, the more time compounding has. A dollar invested in your twenties has decades longer to grow than the same dollar invested in your forties.
Step 1
Start with whatever amount is sustainable, rather than waiting to start large.
Step 2
Reinvest dividends and capital gains instead of spending them.
Step 3
Keep costs low, since fees compound against you on the same schedule.
Step 4
Leave it alone; the mechanism depends on time more than on activity.

Compounding · fit and reference
What compounding rewards
Starting as early as possible, even in small amounts
Reinvesting dividends and capital gains rather than spending them
A long-term mindset over short-term gains
Consistency, which matters more than any single large contribution
Low costs, since fees compound against you the same way
Why delay is expensive
The years lost are the ones with the most compounding left in them
Larger contributions later rarely recover the gap
Even a few years of delay has an outsized cost over a full career
Time is the one input that cannot be replaced with money
Three inputs, one irreplaceable
Amount
How much you contribute. Adjustable at any point, and the least powerful of the three.
Return
What the portfolio earns. Partly outside your control, and partly a function of costs.
Time
How long it compounds. The most powerful input, and the only one that cannot be added later.
