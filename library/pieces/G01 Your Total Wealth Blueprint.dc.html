Why good decisions become fragmented
Financial complexity usually arrives one decision at a time
An old retirement plan remains at a former employer
A new account solves an immediate need
Equity compensation grows into a major exposure
A trust is created but not fully funded
Tax, investment, insurance, and estate conversations occur separately
Family priorities change faster than the documents and portfolio
Result: each decision may be reasonable alone while the full system becomes difficult to see.

Common planning gaps

Insurance reflects an earlier stage of life

The coordination model
Complex decisions tend to improve when professionals work from the same facts
The household defines
Goals, values, tradeoffs, and final decisions
The advisor coordinates
Planning, investments, scenarios, implementation, and follow-through
Other professionals contribute
Tax advice, legal documents, insurance analysis, business valuation, and specialized expertise
The shared requirement
Clear data, defined roles, documented decisions, and timely communication
