Begin with purpose
Before asking “What should I buy?” ask “What must this money do?”
Which goals are non-negotiable?
When will capital be needed?
How much decline can the financial plan absorb?
Which tax, liquidity, ownership, or personal constraints matter?
Risk is not only volatility. Risk is being unable to fund what matters.

Diversification has layers
Owning many investments does not always mean owning many different risks
Companies and issuers
Sectors and industries
Geography and currency
Stocks, bonds, real assets, and other exposures
Interest-rate and credit sensitivity
Investment styles and factors
Public and private liquidity
Household exposures: employer stock, business, and real estate
Diversification spreads money among investments to reduce risk. It cannot guarantee against loss.

Risk has more than one form
The risks investors feel are not always the risks that matter most
Visibility versus consequence
Conceptual positioning. Relative importance varies by household.
Volatility is the risk investors notice most
Inflation and longevity are quieter but can do more harm
Sequence and concentration risk can permanently impair a plan
Design for the risks that matter, not only the ones that are felt

Illustrative household
The right allocation depends on the household, not only age
The situation
A 58-year-old household expects retirement in seven years, has substantial employer stock, a paid-off home, future tuition support, and a high tolerance for market volatility.
Key distinction
High emotional risk tolerance does not automatically create high capacity for concentration or illiquidity.
Questions to coordinate
01Which goals require capital before retirement?
02How much employer exposure exists outside the portfolio?
03What tax cost accompanies diversification?
04Which assets can remain invested for 10+ years?

Measure what matters
Performance matters, but it is not the only measure of success
Are priority goals still funded?
Has concentration or illiquidity increased?
Is near-term spending protected?
What was retained after taxes and fees?
Does the allocation still match the plan?
Did the investor follow the agreed process?

Three things to remember
Portfolio resilience begins before the market test
1Assign capital according to purpose and timing
2Diversify across household risks, not merely account holdings
3Establish decision rules for rebalancing, liquidity, taxes, and review
