Equity compensation · the three grant types
Managing your equity means being mindful of taxes
The same share count, three different tax outcomes
Farther offers a helping hand when you need to make big decisions around your hard-earned equity. Each grant type moves through the same three stages, but the tax consequence at each stage differs.
Restricted stock units (RSUs)
Stage
What happens
Initial grant
Grant from the company, including share amount, grant date, vesting schedule, vesting conditions, and restrictions on transfer
Conversion
Exchange of RSUs for shares of the company. No payment required by the employee, shares received upon vesting, and the current value of shares is taxable as current income
Sale
Immediate: value of shares treated as taxable income. Within a year: value at conversion plus gains after conversion taxed as current income. One year or longer: gains taxed at the capital gains rate
Non-qualified stock options (NSOs)
Stage
What happens
Initial grant
Grant from the company, including share amount, grant date, strike price, vesting schedule, vesting conditions, and restrictions on transfer
Conversion
Shares purchased with payment from the employee to the company. Ordinary income tax payable on the difference between the fair market value of the stock and the exercise price
Sale
Immediate or within a year: taxed on the difference between current fair market value and the market price when you exercised, with gains treated as taxable income. One year or longer: gains taxed at the capital gains rate

Incentive stock options and reference
Incentive stock options (ISOs)
Stage
What happens
Initial grant
Grant from the company, including share amount, grant date, strike price, vesting schedule, vesting conditions, and restrictions on transfer
Conversion
Shares purchased with payment from the employee to the company. The current value of shares becomes the taxable value for computing alternative minimum tax (AMT), but no additional taxes are due beyond what you owe for AMT
Sale
Immediate: value of shares less amount paid treated as taxable income. Within a year: may be eligible for AMT in the year of exercise, with gains treated as taxable income. One year or longer: gains taxed at the capital gains rate, with a tax credit for AMT paid

How Farther approaches this
Managing your equity means being mindful of taxes at every stage: grant, conversion, and sale. Your advisor coordinates the exercise and sale timing with the rest of your plan and with your tax professional, so a single decision does not undo the value of the grant.
