Insurance and structure are different tools
Risk transfer
Insurance pays claims from someone else's balance sheet. It cannot cover excluded claims or exceed the limit.
Segregation
Entities and titling confine a claim to the asset that produced it. They do not protect against your own negligence.
Exemption and trusts
State law places some assets beyond reach, and trusts remove ownership, but only before a claim is foreseeable.
Most families treat these as alternatives. They are layers, and each one covers what the others miss.

What the table does not solve
An entity around a rental property confines claims arising at that property. It does not protect that property from a claim arising elsewhere, and it does not shield you from liability for your own negligence. If you were driving, the entity is irrelevant. Capacity above $25 million is genuinely tight, and a household with a teenage driver may find several carriers unwilling to write past $1 million regardless of assets. Structures also have to precede the problem: a transfer made after a claim exists or is reasonably foreseeable can be unwound.

Three points carry the piece
Verdict severity has risen far faster than the liability limits most families carry, and a judgment can reach earnings and assets you do not yet have. Insurance and legal structure do different jobs, and a complete program uses both rather than choosing between them. And availability is now the binding constraint at high limits, which makes reviewing coverage during a quiet year the whole strategy.
How Farther approaches this
Property and casualty coverage usually sits outside the wealth management conversation entirely, reviewed by a broker who has never seen the balance sheet it is meant to protect. Farther earns no commission on any policy, so a recommendation to increase coverage is a recommendation and not a sale.
What that looks like in practice
Sizing liability limits against your current balance sheet including future earning capacity
Identifying the exposures your household creates, from drivers to staff to board seats to short-term rentals
Coordinating with your broker on tower structure and with your attorney on titling and entities
Confirming what your state already exempts, since some protection exists without any action
Reviewing coverage annually, because both your balance sheet and the market move
