Wealth structure
Leverage turns a protection tool into a rate bet
Premium financing can help a household preserve liquidity while implementing a sizable life insurance policy. It can also add an entirely new layer of risk to what many families assume is a conservative protection tool.
How it works
A lender advances funds used to pay policy premiums. The client generally posts collateral, pays interest, and expects the policy or other assets to support eventual repayment depending on the design. The strategy only works when policy design, collateral capacity, interest-rate assumptions, and lender behavior all remain inside a range the household can tolerate.
Step 1
Size the collateral requirement, and confirm it can be posted through a drawdown.
Step 2
Stress-test the interest carry at rates well above the inception assumption.
Step 3
Identify the exit path, and what it costs at each point in the financing period.
Step 4
Compare the whole structure against a simpler cash-funded design.

The stress case
If rates rise from 5.5% to 8.0%, annual interest carry increases from $275,000 to $400,000. The strategy remains viable only if the household can comfortably absorb that change, in a year when collateral values may also have fallen.

Premium financing · fit and reference
What the structure is meant to deliver
A sizable death benefit without liquidating income-producing assets
Preserved liquidity for the operating business or investment portfolio
Estate liquidity funded outside the taxable estate, typically through trust ownership
Capacity beyond what current cash flow alone would support
The risks that come with it
Interest-rate risk, since a rising rate raises the carry on the whole financed balance
Collateral risk, since lenders can demand more precisely when asset values fall
Lender renewal risk, since facilities are not guaranteed to roll on the same terms
Policy performance risk, since crediting below assumptions leaves less to repay the loan
Exit risk, since unwinding partway through can be expensive or forced
Three scenarios to model before committing
Base case
Rates near inception, policy performing to illustration, collateral stable. The version usually presented.
Moderate stress
Rates a point or two higher and crediting below assumption. Additional cash contributions likely.
Severe stress
Rates sharply higher, collateral values down, lender tightening at renewal. The case that decides suitability.

Farther evaluates whether premium financing improves the balance sheet or simply makes the strategy look easier in the short run.
