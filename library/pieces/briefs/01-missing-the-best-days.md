## Primary Chart/Table Data

[Chart type]: Vertical bar chart, four bars, descending left to right, with dollar value labels above each bar and the "% less than fully invested" figure called out on bars 2–4.

Hypothetical growth of $10,000 invested in the S&P 500 Index, 1996–2025 (30 calendar years).

| Scenario | Ending value of $10,000 | Difference vs. fully invested (%) | Implied annualized return (%) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fully invested — all days | $192,167 | — | 10.35% |
| Missed 10 best days | $85,490 | −56% (Hartford: "56% Less") | 7.41% |
| Missed 20 best days | $49,551 | −74% (Hartford: "74% Less") | 5.48% |
| Missed 30 best days | $31,123 | −84% (Hartford: "84% Less") | 3.86% |

Notes on the table: ending values and the −56% / −74% / −84% figures are printed on the Hartford Funds chart. The "Difference vs. fully invested" column restated precisely from the ending values is −55.5%, −74.2% and −83.8%; Hartford rounds these to 56%, 74% and 84%. The "Implied annualized return" column is a Farther calculation from Hartford's ending values over the 30-year period (1996–2025) — Hartford does not print annualized percentages on the chart. Designer should label that column as calculated, or omit it.

## Insight Callouts

INSIGHT 01 — TEN DAYS DECIDED HALF THE OUTCOME
Thirty years holds about 7,500 trading days. Ten of them accounted for more than half of the total gain. No one has a reliable way to know which ten they will be, which is why staying invested does the heavy lifting.

INSIGHT 02 — THE BEST DAYS FEEL THE WORST
Roughly three quarters of the market's strongest days arrived during bear markets or in the first weeks of a recovery. The moment selling feels most sensible is usually the moment the rebound begins. Discipline in those weeks is worth more than any forecast.
