# The Wash-Sale Rule Across Your Whole Household

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Coordinating taxable accounts, IRAs, spouses, and employer-plan activity

The wash-sale rule doesn't just apply to your own taxable account, it applies across every account you or your spouse control, including IRAs and employer retirement plans, which makes coordination essential.

---

### Headline Numbers

**61 days**
Total wash-sale window: 30 days before, day of, and 30 days after a sale

---

**100%**
Of a household's accounts, including a spouse's IRA, are aggregated under the rule

---

**$0**
Loss allowed if a substantially identical security is repurchased anywhere in the household

## The Core Idea

Selling a security at a loss and repurchasing a substantially identical security within 30 days before or after disallows the loss for tax purposes. Critically, this rule applies in aggregate across a household, a spouse's IRA purchase or an automatic 401(k) reinvestment can trigger a wash sale even if the original sale happened in a completely separate account.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** A security is sold at a loss in a taxable account.

**STEP 2.** The 61-day window opens, spanning 30 days before and after the sale date.

**STEP 3.** Any purchase of a substantially identical security within that window, in any account the household controls, triggers the rule.

**STEP 4.** The loss is disallowed and added to the cost basis of the replacement shares instead.

## How a Wash Sale Can Slip Through

A $20,000 loss is harvested in a taxable account, but a spouse's IRA has automatic dividend reinvestment in the same fund.

| Scenario | Loss Intended | Loss Actually Allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Coordinated household (tracked) | $20,000 | $20,000 |
| Uncoordinated household (spouse IRA reinvests) | $20,000 | $0 |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual outcomes depend on the specific securities and account activity involved.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households with taxable accounts alongside IRAs or 401(k)s holding similar securities
- Married couples with separately managed accounts that aren't coordinated
- Anyone using automatic dividend reinvestment on a position being harvested

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Preserves the full intended tax benefit of a harvested loss
- Avoids unpleasant surprises discovered only at tax filing
- Extends coordination across spousal accounts and employer plans
- Supports confident, larger-scale harvesting without hidden risk

## Important Considerations

**Siloed accounts.** Assuming the rule only applies within a single account.

**Spousal blind spot.** A spouse unknowingly repurchasing the same security in their own IRA.

**Automatic reinvestment.** Dividend reinvestment plans can inadvertently trigger a wash sale.
