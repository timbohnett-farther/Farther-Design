## The Core Idea

Bonds and REITs generate ordinary income annually and are best sheltered in tax-deferred accounts. Broad index funds and municipal bonds are already tax-efficient and fit well in taxable accounts. Getting this placement right, across every account a household holds, can add measurable after-tax return every year.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify the tax character of each holding, ordinary income, qualified dividends, or tax-free.

**STEP 2.** Place high-tax-cost assets in tax-deferred accounts.

**STEP 3.** Place tax-efficient assets in taxable accounts.

**STEP 4.** Maintain the same total allocation across accounts, only the location changes.

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households with assets spread across taxable and tax-advantaged accounts
- Anyone holding bonds, REITs, or high-turnover funds
- Investors seeking incremental after-tax return without changing their allocation

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Improves after-tax return without adding investment risk
- Requires no change to the household's overall target allocation
- Compounds meaningfully over long horizons
- Applies across every account type a household holds simultaneously

## Important Considerations

**Reversed placement.** Holding bonds primarily in a taxable account while an IRA sits mostly in cash.

**Set-and-forget.** Never revisiting asset location as account balances shift over time.

**One-time mindset.** Treating asset location as a single exercise rather than an ongoing discipline.
