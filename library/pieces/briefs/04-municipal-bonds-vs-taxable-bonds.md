# Municipal Bonds vs. Taxable Bonds

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Comparing tax-equivalent yield rather than stated yield

Comparing a municipal bond's yield directly to a taxable bond's yield is misleading, the correct comparison is the taxable bond's tax-equivalent yield, which rises with your marginal tax bracket.

## The Core Idea

A municipal bond yielding 3.5% may look lower than a taxable bond yielding 4.5%, but for a household in a high tax bracket, the municipal bond's tax-equivalent yield can exceed the taxable bond's actual yield once taxes are factored in.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify the municipal bond's stated yield.

**STEP 2.** Identify your marginal federal (and applicable state) tax bracket.

**STEP 3.** Divide the muni yield by (1 minus your tax bracket) to calculate the tax-equivalent yield.

**STEP 4.** Compare that tax-equivalent yield directly against taxable bond alternatives.

## Tax-Equivalent Yield by Bracket

A 3.5% municipal bond's tax-equivalent yield rises sharply as the investor's marginal tax bracket increases.

| Marginal Tax Bracket | Muni Stated Yield | Tax-Equivalent Yield |
|---|---|---|
| 24% | 3.5% | 4.61% |
| 32% | 3.5% | 5.15% |
| 37% | 3.5% | 5.56% |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Excludes potential state tax benefits for in-state municipal bonds, which would further increase the tax-equivalent yield.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households in higher marginal tax brackets
- Investors holding bonds in taxable accounts
- Anyone comparing municipal and taxable bond options without adjusting for tax treatment

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Reveals the true, comparable value of tax-free income
- Can outperform taxable bonds on an after-tax basis for high earners
- Often carries additional state tax benefits for in-state bonds
- Fits naturally within a broader asset location strategy

## Important Considerations

**Apples-to-oranges.** Comparing stated yields directly without adjusting for tax treatment.

**Ignoring state tax.** Overlooking the added benefit of in-state municipal bonds.

**Wrong account.** Holding municipal bonds in tax-deferred accounts, where their benefit is wasted.
