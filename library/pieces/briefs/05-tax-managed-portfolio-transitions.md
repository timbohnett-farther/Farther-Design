# Tax-Managed Portfolio Transitions

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Moving legacy holdings without triggering an unnecessary tax bill

Inheriting or acquiring a new advisory relationship often means inheriting a legacy portfolio full of embedded gains, and liquidating it all at once can trigger an unnecessarily large single-year tax bill.

## The Core Idea

Rather than selling a legacy portfolio in one transaction, a tax-managed transition spreads the realized gains across multiple years, using a deliberate annual gains budget that stays within a targeted tax bracket, paired with tax-loss harvesting elsewhere to offset the impact.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Inventory the legacy portfolio's embedded gains, position by position.

**STEP 2.** Set an annual gains budget calibrated to avoid pushing into a higher tax bracket.

**STEP 3.** Sell the highest-priority repositioning targets first, within that annual budget.

**STEP 4.** Pair sales with tax-loss harvesting elsewhere in the portfolio to offset realized gains.

## One-Time Sale vs. Staged Transition

A legacy portfolio with $500,000 in embedded gains can be liquidated all at once, or transitioned over three years.

| Approach | Year 1 Tax Bill | Total Tax Bill (3-Yr) |
|---|---|---|
| Full liquidation in Year 1 | $185,000 | $185,000 |
| Staged transition over 3 years | ~$62,000 | ~$150,000* |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Staged total assumes some offsetting tax-loss harvesting; actual bracket and harvesting benefits vary by household.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Anyone transitioning a legacy portfolio from a prior advisor or inheritance
- Households with significant embedded gains in outdated or concentrated holdings
- Investors wanting a new allocation without an immediate, unmanageable tax bill

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Avoids an unnecessarily large single-year tax event
- Allows repositioning toward a better allocation over a manageable timeline
- Pairs naturally with ongoing tax-loss harvesting
- Preserves flexibility to adjust as tax law or income changes

## Important Considerations

**All at once.** Liquidating an entire legacy portfolio in a single year without a tax budget.

**Ignoring offsets.** Ignoring available losses elsewhere that could offset the transition's gains.

**No prioritization.** Failing to prioritize which legacy positions most urgently need to be sold first.
