# Charitable Gifts of Appreciated Securities

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Donating long-term appreciated assets instead of cash

Donating appreciated securities held long-term, rather than cash, allows a donor to claim a full fair-market-value deduction while avoiding capital gains tax on the built-in appreciation entirely.

## The Core Idea

If you sell an appreciated stock and donate the cash proceeds, you pay capital gains tax on the sale first. If you donate the stock directly, the charity receives the same value, you still get the full deduction, and the embedded gain is never taxed to you.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify a long-term appreciated security suitable for donation.

**STEP 2.** Transfer the shares directly to the charity or a donor-advised fund, rather than selling first.

**STEP 3.** Claim a deduction for the full fair market value at the time of the gift.

**STEP 4.** The embedded capital gain is never recognized or taxed to the donor.

## Cash Gift vs. Stock Gift

A donor wants to give $25,000 to charity using stock with a $10,000 cost basis, held long-term, versus selling first and donating cash.

| Approach | Capital Gains Tax Paid | Deduction | Net Value to Charity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sell stock, donate cash | ~$5,950 | $25,000 | $25,000 |
| Donate stock directly | $0 | $25,000 | $25,000 |
| Donor's tax savings | +$5,950 | — | — |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a 23.8% combined long-term capital gains and net investment income tax rate on a $15,000 gain.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Charitably inclined households holding long-term appreciated securities
- Anyone currently donating cash who holds appreciated positions instead
- Investors wanting to reduce a concentrated position while supporting charity

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Avoids capital gains tax entirely on the donated appreciation
- Provides a full fair-market-value income tax deduction
- Can help diversify a concentrated position while giving
- Charity receives the same total value either way

## Important Considerations

**Selling first.** Selling the stock and donating the cash proceeds, triggering unnecessary capital gains tax.

**Short holding period.** Donating a security held one year or less, which limits the deduction to cost basis.

**Wrong lot.** Choosing the wrong lot to donate when multiple purchase lots exist.
