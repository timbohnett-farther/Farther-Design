- **Headline (page title, 3-6 words)**: The Rule Of 72
- **Subhead (one sentence, states exactly what the chart shows)**: Divide 72 by your annual return and you get the approximate number of years it takes money to double — a shortcut that shows how much of long-term wealth is created by time, not timing.

## Headline Stat Boxes (4 boxes, top of page)
1. THE SHORTCUT — **72 ÷ return = years to double** — Divide 72 by the annual rate of return to estimate an investment's doubling time (Investopedia, https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/ruleof72.asp)
2. S&P 500 LONG-RUN RETURN — **10.02%** — Annualized total return of the S&P 500, with dividends reinvested, 1928–2025 (Farther calculation from NYU Stern / Aswath Damodaran annual return series, https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/datasets/histretSP.xls)
3. YEARS TO DOUBLE AT THAT RATE — **7.2 years** — 72 ÷ 10.02 = 7.2 years; the precise math gives 7.26 years, so the shortcut is off by less than four weeks per double (Farther calculation; formula per Investopedia, https://www.investopedia.com/terms/r/ruleof72.asp)
4. INFLATION CUTS BOTH WAYS — **23.8 years** — At the 3.04% average U.S. inflation rate from 1928–2025, 72 ÷ 3.04 means a dollar's purchasing power halves in roughly 24 years (Farther calculation from CPI-U series, FRED via NYU Stern dataset, https://pages.stern.nyu.edu/~adamodar/pc/datasets/histretSP.xls; https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/CPIAUCNS)

## Insight Callouts

INSIGHT 01 — TWO POINTS OF RETURN, HALF THE WAIT
At 6% a year, money takes about 12 years to double. At 12%, it takes about 6. Two doublings instead of one, over the same stretch of life, is the whole argument for owning growth assets and paying attention to costs.

INSIGHT 02 — INFLATION IS RUNNING THE SAME MATH
Prices have compounded at roughly 3% a year since 1928, which halves a dollar's buying power in about 24 years. Cash does not sit still; it quietly shrinks. Any plan built to last 30 years has to out-compound that.
