# Donor-Advised Fund "Bunching"

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Front-loading charitable deductions while granting over time

Bunching charitable contributions into a donor-advised fund in a single high-income year, then granting to charities over subsequent years, can maximize the tax benefit of itemizing deductions.

## The Core Idea

Rather than giving the same amount to charity every year, which may not exceed the standard deduction threshold, bunching combines several years of intended giving into one large DAF contribution, itemizing that year, then distributing grants to charities on the donor's normal timeline afterward.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Estimate several years of intended annual charitable giving.

**STEP 2.** Contribute that combined amount to a donor-advised fund in a single high-income year.

**STEP 3.** Claim the full deduction in that contribution year, exceeding the standard deduction.

**STEP 4.** Grant to specific charities from the DAF over the following years, on the same timeline as before.

## Bunching vs. Annual Giving

A household giving $10,000/year compares taking the standard deduction each year versus bunching 3 years into one DAF contribution.

| Approach | Itemized Deductions Claimed | Years Itemizing |
|---|---|---|
| Annual $10k giving (standard deduction used) | $0 above standard | 0 of 3 years |
| Bunched $30k DAF contribution | ~$15,000 above standard | 1 of 3 years |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a single filer with a $15,000 standard deduction and no other itemizable deductions.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households whose annual giving falls near the standard deduction threshold
- Anyone with a high-income year, from a bonus, sale, or other event, suited to a larger deduction
- Charitably inclined donors wanting to maximize tax benefit without changing their giving pattern

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Maximizes the value of itemizing in a single, well-timed year
- Preserves the donor's normal multi-year granting schedule
- Pairs well with donating appreciated securities instead of cash
- Can be timed to coincide with an unusually high-income year

## Important Considerations

**Never itemizing.** Continuing to give the same modest annual amount without itemizing, missing the deduction benefit entirely.

**Cash instead of stock.** Funding a DAF with cash instead of appreciated securities when both are available.

**Poor timing.** Failing to coordinate the bunching year with other income and deduction planning.

| | Do Nothing (Give $15,000/yr, Take Standard Deduction) | Bunch 3 Years Into One DAF Gift | Difference |
|---|---|---|---|
| Itemized benefit over 3 years | $0 (below std. deduction each year) | $30,000 excess itemized | +$30,000 deductible |
| Tax savings at 32% bracket | $0 | $9,600 | **+$9,600 over 3 years** |
| 10-yr cumulative savings (3 bunching cycles) | $0 | ~$32,000 | **+$32,000** |

## Itemizing Benefit by Bunching Horizon (Single Filer, $15,000 Std. Deduction)
