# Qualified Charitable Distributions

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Using IRA distributions for charitable gifts after age 70½

A Qualified Charitable Distribution (QCD) allows IRA owners age 70½ or older to direct IRA funds straight to a qualified charity, satisfying part or all of an RMD without the distribution counting as taxable income.

## The Core Idea

Unlike a typical charitable deduction, which requires itemizing, a QCD reduces taxable income directly at the source, the distribution never appears as income in the first place. This can be more valuable than a deduction, especially for those who take the standard deduction or face income-based thresholds like IRMAA.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Confirm eligibility at age 70½ or older.

**STEP 2.** Direct the IRA custodian to send funds directly to a qualified charity.

**STEP 3.** The distribution counts toward satisfying the year's RMD, if applicable.

**STEP 4.** The amount is excluded from taxable income entirely, rather than deducted after the fact.

## QCD vs. Standard RMD Plus Cash Gift

An IRA owner with a $30,000 RMD wants to give $20,000 to charity this year.

| Approach | Taxable Income Added | Charitable Deduction Needed |
|---|---|---|
| Take RMD, then donate cash | $30,000 | Requires itemizing to benefit |
| Direct $20,000 QCD, remaining $10k as RMD | $10,000 | Not needed; income excluded |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual RMD and QCD amounts depend on account balance, life expectancy factors, and individual circumstances.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- IRA owners age 70½ or older who are charitably inclined
- Households taking the standard deduction who wouldn't otherwise benefit from a charitable deduction
- Anyone concerned about RMDs pushing them into a higher tax bracket or triggering IRMAA surcharges

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Excludes the distribution from taxable income, not merely deducts it
- Satisfies some or all of the year's RMD requirement
- Helps manage IRMAA and other income-based thresholds
- Requires no itemizing to receive the tax benefit

## Important Considerations

**Wrong order.** Taking the RMD as a normal distribution first, then donating the cash, which forfeits the QCD's tax exclusion.

**Wrong vehicle.** Directing the distribution to a donor-advised fund, which does not qualify for QCD treatment.

**Exceeding the limit.** Missing the annual QCD limit and inadvertently creating taxable income on the excess.
