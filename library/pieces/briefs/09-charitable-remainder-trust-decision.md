# The Charitable Remainder Trust Decision

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## When a CRT may convert a highly appreciated asset into income and charitable value

A Charitable Remainder Trust (CRT) allows a donor to contribute a highly appreciated asset, have it sold inside the trust without immediate capital gains tax, and receive an income stream for life or a term of years, with the remainder passing to charity.

---

### Headline Numbers

**0%**
Immediate capital gains tax owed at the trust level when a CRT sells the contributed asset

---

**10%+**
Minimum actuarial remainder value generally required to pass to charity

---

**5%+**
Typical minimum annual payout rate to the income beneficiary

## The Core Idea

Selling a highly appreciated asset outright triggers capital gains tax immediately, reducing the amount available to reinvest for income. Contributing that same asset to a CRT allows the trust to sell it without immediate capital gains tax, generating an income stream from the full pre-tax value, with the remainder ultimately benefiting charity.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Contribute a highly appreciated, low-basis asset to the CRT.

**STEP 2.** The trust sells the asset without immediate capital gains tax at the trust level.

**STEP 3.** The donor receives an income stream, either a fixed annuity or a percentage-based unitrust payment.

**STEP 4.** At the end of the trust term, the remainder passes to the named charity.

## Outright Sale vs. CRT-Funded Income

A $2,000,000 asset with a $200,000 basis is sold outright versus contributed to a CRT paying 5% annually.

| Approach | Immediate Tax Due | Year 1 Investable/Income Amount |
|---|---|---|
| Sell outright, reinvest after-tax | ~$428,400 | ~$1,571,600 |
| Contribute to CRT, 5% payout | $0 | $100,000 income stream on full $2M |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a 23.8% combined capital gains/NIIT rate on outright sale. CRT income is taxable to the recipient under tiered ordering rules.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Owners of highly appreciated, low-basis assets such as concentrated stock or real estate
- Charitably inclined households wanting to convert an illiquid or concentrated asset into income
- Anyone seeking to diversify a concentrated position while deferring immediate capital gains tax

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Defers capital gains tax at the trust level entirely
- Converts a concentrated, illiquid asset into a diversified income stream
- Provides a partial charitable income tax deduction at funding
- Supports a philanthropic legacy through the eventual remainder

## Important Considerations

**Assuming tax-free income.** Income received from the trust is still taxable to the recipient under specific ordering rules.

**Underestimating complexity.** Underestimating the complexity and irrevocability of establishing a CRT.

**Skipping the comparison.** Failing to model the actual cash-flow comparison against a simple outright sale before deciding.
