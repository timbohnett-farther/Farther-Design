## Secondary chart or data point
**Frequency panel** — how often equities lose money, by holding period, from Capital Group's study through 12/31/2025: the S&P 500 had negative results in 26% of years over the past 98 years; one-year investors who held two more years experienced nearly half as many negative periods; and 94% of 10-year periods over the past 89 years were positive (https://www.capitalgroup.com/individual/planning/investing-fundamentals/time-not-timing-is-what-matters.html).

**Why the range narrows, not the risk of a bad start** — Crestmont Research's work shows 20-year outcomes are strongly related to the price/earnings ratio at the start of the period: the weakest decile of 20-year stretches averaged 5.1% a year and began at an average P/E10 of 23.6, while the strongest decile averaged 15.4% a year and began at an average P/E10 of 10.1 (https://www.crestmontresearch.com/docs/Stock-20-Yr-Returns.pdf).

## Insight Callouts

INSIGHT 01 — TIME DOES NOT REMOVE RISK, IT NARROWS IT
A single year in stocks has run from −44% to +53%. Stretch the holding period to 20 years and the whole range sits between roughly +2% and +18% a year. The asset did not get safer. The horizon did the work.

INSIGHT 02 — YOUR HORIZON SHOULD SET YOUR ALLOCATION
Money you need in three years and money you need in thirty are not the same money. Match each goal to the holding period its dollars actually have, and short-term volatility stops being a threat to the plan.
