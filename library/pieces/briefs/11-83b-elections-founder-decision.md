# 83(b) Elections: The 30-Day Founder Decision

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Addressing the election window for restricted stock

An 83(b) election lets a founder or early employee pay tax on restricted stock's value at grant, rather than as it vests, but the election must be filed within 30 days of the grant, with no extensions or exceptions.

## The Core Idea

Without an 83(b) election, restricted stock is taxed as ordinary income on its value each time it vests, which can be substantial if the company's value has grown significantly. Filing the election at grant, when the stock's value is typically low or nominal, locks in that low value for tax purposes and starts the capital gains holding period immediately.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Restricted stock is granted, subject to a vesting schedule.

**STEP 2.** Within 30 days, the recipient files an 83(b) election with the IRS.

**STEP 3.** Tax is paid on the stock's value at grant, typically low for an early-stage company.

**STEP 4.** Future appreciation is taxed at capital gains rates upon eventual sale, not as ordinary income at vesting.

## With Election vs. Without

A founder receives stock worth $1,000 at grant that grows to $1,000,000 by the time it fully vests four years later.

| Approach | Ordinary Income Recognized | Tax Character of Future Growth |
|---|---|---|
| 83(b) election filed at grant | $1,000 | Capital gains |
| No election filed | $1,000,000 (at vesting) | Ordinary income |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes the stock's value grows as described and the election is properly filed within 30 days.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Founders and early employees receiving restricted stock grants
- Anyone granted equity in a company with low current value but high growth expectations
- Advisors and attorneys coordinating founder equity structuring

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Locks in a low taxable value at grant
- Converts future appreciation from ordinary income to capital gains
- Starts the capital gains holding period immediately
- Can meaningfully reduce lifetime tax on founder equity

## Important Considerations

**Missed deadline.** Missing the strict 30-day deadline, which cannot be extended or corrected after the fact.

**No proof.** Failing to send a copy of the election to the employer and retain proof of timely filing.

**Forfeiture risk.** Filing the election without considering the risk that the stock could later be forfeited.
