# RSUs: Sell-to-Cover Is Not a Strategy

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Managing concentrated employer exposure after vesting

Sell-to-cover, where just enough shares are sold at vesting to cover the tax withholding, is a default mechanism, not a deliberate strategy, and relying on it alone often leaves employees increasingly concentrated in employer stock over time.

## The Core Idea

Each RSU vesting event is an opportunity to make an active decision about how much employer stock to hold versus sell, based on the household's total concentration, tax situation, and diversification goals, rather than defaulting to the minimum withholding sale.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** RSUs vest and are taxed as ordinary income at that day's fair market value.

**STEP 2.** Sell-to-cover automatically sells enough shares to satisfy tax withholding.

**STEP 3.** Absent a deliberate decision, the remaining shares are simply held, growing the concentrated position.

**STEP 4.** A deliberate plan instead sets a target sale percentage at each vest, based on total concentration.

## Default Withholding vs. Deliberate Plan

An employee vesting $100,000 of RSUs each year compares sell-to-cover alone against a deliberate 75% sale target.

| Approach | Shares Sold at Vest | Employer Stock Retained per Vest |
|---|---|---|
| Sell-to-cover only | ~30% | ~$70,000 |
| Deliberate 75% sale plan | 75% | $25,000 |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual withholding percentages and sale decisions vary by employer, jurisdiction, and household circumstances.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Employees receiving regular RSU vesting from their employer
- Anyone whose employer stock concentration has grown without an active decision
- Households wanting a systematic plan rather than a default withholding mechanism

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Establishes a deliberate, repeatable diversification process
- Reduces single-stock concentration risk over time
- Keeps tax withholding and diversification decisions separate
- Can be coordinated with blackout periods and trading policies

## Important Considerations

**False sense of diversification.** Treating sell-to-cover as sufficient diversification, when it typically only covers taxes, not concentration risk.

**No target.** Failing to set a target allocation to employer stock and rebalance toward it at each vest.

**Compliance gaps.** Ignoring blackout periods or insider trading policies when planning sales beyond sell-to-cover.
