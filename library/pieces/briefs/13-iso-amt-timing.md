# Incentive Stock Options and AMT

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Timing ISO exercise while managing alternative minimum tax exposure

Exercising Incentive Stock Options (ISOs) can trigger the Alternative Minimum Tax (AMT) on the spread between the exercise price and fair market value, even though no shares have been sold, making exercise timing a critical planning decision.

## The Core Idea

Unlike regular income tax, AMT is calculated on the ISO exercise spread whether or not the shares are sold. Exercising a large number of ISOs in a single year, especially when the spread is significant, can trigger a substantial AMT liability, sometimes on stock that later declines in value before it can be sold.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** ISOs are exercised, creating a spread between exercise price and current fair market value.

**STEP 2.** That spread is added back for AMT calculation purposes, regardless of whether shares are sold.

**STEP 3.** If AMT liability results, it may be partially recoverable in future years via an AMT credit.

**STEP 4.** Spreading exercises across multiple years can help manage the AMT impact of any single year.

## Single-Year Exercise vs. Spread Exercise

An employee with $500,000 of ISO exercise spread compares exercising it all in one year versus spreading it across two years.

| Approach | Spread Recognized Year 1 | Estimated AMT Exposure |
|---|---|---|
| Exercise all in Year 1 | $500,000 | ~$140,000 |
| Exercise $250k in Year 1, $250k in Year 2 | $250,000 per year | ~$70,000 per year |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual AMT liability depends on total income, other AMT preference items, and available exemptions.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Employees holding Incentive Stock Options with a significant exercise spread
- Anyone considering exercising a large number of ISOs in a single year
- Households wanting to model AMT exposure before an exercise decision

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Preserves potential capital gains tax treatment on future ISO stock sales
- Spreads AMT exposure across multiple, more manageable tax years
- Builds a plan for tracking and later claiming any AMT credit
- Reduces the risk of overpaying tax on stock that later declines

## Important Considerations

**No modeling.** Exercising all vested ISOs in a single year without modeling the AMT impact.

**Value risk.** Exercising ISOs on stock that subsequently declines, resulting in AMT owed on a paper gain that evaporated.

**Lost credits.** Failing to track and claim AMT credits available in future years.
