## Headline Stat Boxes (3-4 boxes, top of page)
1. INVESTED IMMEDIATELY — **$170,555** — Ending value for the investor who put each year's $2,000 into the S&P 500 on the first trading day of the year, 2005–2024. (Charles Schwab, "Does Market Timing Work?" — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)
2. NEVER INVESTED — **$47,357** — Ending value for the investor who kept waiting for a better entry point and left the money in Treasury bills instead. (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)
3. COST OF PERFECT TIMING — **$15,522** — The entire advantage that flawless, buy-at-the-annual-low timing produced over simply investing right away across the same 20 years. (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)
4. ODDS AGAINST WAITING — **75.6%** — Share of typical 12-month periods in which the market has risen, which is why delaying tends to cost more than it saves. (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)

## Secondary chart or data point
**Data point 1 — bad timing still beat no timing.** The worst-possible-timing investor ended with about **three times** what she would have had if she had never invested in stocks at all ($151,343 vs. $47,357; the "about three times" characterization is Schwab's). (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)

**Data point 2 — this is not one lucky stretch.** Schwab ran the same five strategies across **80 separate 20-year periods** and found similar results across almost all of them. (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)

**Data point 3 — the ranking is stable.** Across the 20 years studied, the order was always the same: perfect timing first, invest-immediately second, monthly dollar-cost averaging third, worst-possible timing fourth, and cash last. (Charles Schwab — https://www.schwab.com/learn/story/does-market-timing-work)

## Insight Callouts

INSIGHT 01 — PERFECT TIMING IS WORTH LESS THAN YOU THINK
Twenty years of flawless, buy-at-the-bottom decisions beat simply investing on day one by $15,522. That is the prize for being right every single time — and nobody is. The realistic version of the winning strategy is just showing up early.

INSIGHT 02 — THE REAL RISK IS THE SIDELINES
The investor with the worst timing in the study still finished with more than three times the cash investor. Waiting for clarity cost $123,198 over 20 years. Bad timing is survivable; not investing is what compounds against you.
