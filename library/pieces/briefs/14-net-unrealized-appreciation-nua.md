# Net Unrealized Appreciation (NUA)

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Evaluating employer stock held inside a retirement plan

Employees holding significant employer stock inside a 401(k) may be able to use the Net Unrealized Appreciation (NUA) strategy at retirement or separation, potentially converting what would be ordinary income tax into long-term capital gains treatment.

## The Core Idea

Rather than rolling employer stock into an IRA, where all future withdrawals are taxed as ordinary income, the NUA strategy distributes the stock directly, taxing only the original cost basis as ordinary income immediately, while the appreciation is taxed at capital gains rates only when the stock is eventually sold.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Confirm eligibility, typically requiring a qualifying triggering event and a full lump-sum distribution.

**STEP 2.** Distribute employer stock directly out of the plan, rather than rolling it to an IRA.

**STEP 3.** Pay ordinary income tax immediately on the stock's original cost basis only.

**STEP 4.** Pay capital gains tax on the appreciation only when the stock is eventually sold.

## NUA vs. Standard IRA Rollover

An employee holds employer stock in a 401(k) with a $100,000 cost basis and $500,000 of embedded appreciation.

| Approach | Tax on Basis (Ordinary) | Tax on Appreciation |
|---|---|---|
| Standard IRA rollover, later withdrawn | Ordinary income on full $600k | N/A, all ordinary income |
| NUA strategy at distribution | Ordinary income on $100k basis only | Capital gains on $500k appreciation |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Employees with significant embedded appreciation in employer stock within a qualified plan
- Anyone with substantial employer stock inside a 401(k) approaching retirement or separation
- Households comparing NUA against a standard IRA rollover

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Converts a portion of what would be ordinary income into capital gains
- Can meaningfully reduce lifetime tax on embedded employer stock appreciation
- Allows earlier access to the appreciation without RMD-driven ordinary income
- Fits naturally alongside a broader retirement distribution plan

## Important Considerations

**Default rollover.** Rolling employer stock into an IRA by default without evaluating the NUA alternative.

**Eligibility gaps.** Failing to meet the strict eligibility requirements, such as taking a full lump-sum distribution.

**Cash flow surprise.** Not planning for the immediate ordinary income tax due on the cost basis portion.
