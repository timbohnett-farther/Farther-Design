# Concentrated Stock: A Staged Sale Plan

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Reducing a single-stock position while setting a tax and risk budget

Reducing a large concentrated stock position all at once can trigger a substantial single-year tax bill, while a staged sale plan spreads the reduction over multiple years within a defined tax and risk budget.

## The Core Idea

A staged sale plan sets both an annual tax budget, limiting realized gains to avoid unnecessary bracket creep, and a risk reduction target, gradually moving the position toward an acceptable concentration level, balancing the urgency of risk reduction against the cost of taxation.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Establish the target ending allocation to the concentrated position.

**STEP 2.** Set an annual gains budget calibrated to the household's tax situation.

**STEP 3.** Sell a planned amount each year according to the glidepath, adjusting for tax-loss harvesting opportunities.

**STEP 4.** Reassess the plan annually as tax law, income, and the stock's performance evolve.

## Full Liquidation vs. 5-Year Glidepath

A $5,000,000 low-basis concentrated position is reduced all at once versus over a 5-year staged plan.

| Approach | Combined Tax Cost | Risk Reduction Timeline |
|---|---|---|
| Full liquidation, Year 1 | Highest marginal bracket on full gain | Immediate |
| 5-year staged glidepath | Spread across lower annual brackets | Gradual, over 5 years |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Executives, founders, or heirs holding a large single-stock position
- Households wanting to reduce concentration risk without an unmanageable tax event
- Anyone whose concentrated position significantly exceeds a prudent allocation target

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Balances risk reduction urgency against tax efficiency
- Avoids unnecessary bracket creep from a single large sale
- Can be paired with ongoing tax-loss harvesting
- Provides a disciplined, revisited annual plan rather than an ad hoc decision

## Important Considerations

**All at once.** Selling the entire position at once, absorbing the maximum possible tax cost in a single year.

**Missed offsets.** Failing to pair the sale plan with available tax-loss harvesting opportunities.

**No target.** Setting no target allocation, resulting in an indefinite delay in risk reduction.
