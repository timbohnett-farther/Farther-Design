# Concentrated Stock: Collars and Protective Puts

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Protecting downside while preserving some upside

A collar, combining a purchased put option with a sold call option, allows a concentrated stock holder to limit downside risk without an immediate sale, in exchange for capping some potential upside.

## The Core Idea

Buying a protective put alone provides downside protection but costs a premium. Selling a call against the position helps offset that premium cost, but caps the position's upside above the call's strike price, together forming a collar that trades some upside potential for meaningful downside protection.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Purchase a put option establishing a floor price for the stock.

**STEP 2.** Sell a call option at a higher strike price, generating premium to offset the put's cost.

**STEP 3.** The position is now protected below the put strike, capped above the call strike.

**STEP 4.** The collar typically has a defined term, after which the strategy may be renewed or the position sold.

## Unhedged Position vs. Collared Position

A $1,000,000 concentrated position is compared unhedged versus collared with a 10% downside floor and 12% upside cap.

| Market Move | Unhedged Value | Collared Value |
|---|---|---|
| Stock falls 25% | $750,000 | ~$900,000 (floor protected) |
| Stock rises 25% | $1,250,000 | ~$1,120,000 (capped) |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual collar terms, costs, and outcomes vary based on strikes chosen, premiums, and market conditions.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Holders of a large, low-basis concentrated stock position wanting protection without an immediate sale
- Executives subject to blackout periods or restrictions limiting outright sales
- Anyone wanting to bridge to a later diversification event while managing downside risk

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Provides meaningful downside protection without triggering an immediate sale
- Can be structured at little or no net premium cost
- Bridges the gap until a planned sale or diversification event
- Compatible with insider trading restrictions in many structures

## Important Considerations

**Upside surprise.** Failing to understand that a collar caps upside as well as limiting downside.

**Tax and margin complexity.** Overlooking the tax and margin implications of options strategies on a concentrated position.

**Permanent mindset.** Treating a collar as a permanent solution rather than a bridge to eventual diversification.
