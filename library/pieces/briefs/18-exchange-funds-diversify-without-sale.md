# Exchange Funds: Diversify Without an Immediate Sale

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Trading concentrated stock into pooled exposure, subject to restrictions and risks

An exchange fund allows a holder of concentrated stock to contribute shares to a pooled fund alongside other concentrated holders, receiving diversified exposure in return without triggering an immediate taxable sale.

## The Core Idea

By pooling concentrated positions from multiple investors, an exchange fund achieves diversification for each participant without any individual sale occurring, deferring the capital gains tax that would otherwise be due, though participants must meet minimum position size requirements and accept a multi-year lock-up period.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Confirm the concentrated position meets the exchange fund's minimum size and eligibility requirements.

**STEP 2.** Contribute the concentrated shares to the pooled fund.

**STEP 3.** Receive an interest in the diversified pool, achieving immediate diversification without a taxable sale.

**STEP 4.** Remain subject to the fund's lock-up period before redemption is available.

## Outright Sale vs. Exchange Fund Contribution

A $1,000,000 concentrated position with a $200,000 basis is sold outright versus contributed to an exchange fund.

| Approach | Immediate Tax Due | Diversification Achieved |
|---|---|---|
| Sell outright, reinvest after-tax | ~$190,400 | Immediate, on reduced after-tax amount |
| Contribute to exchange fund | $0 | Immediate, on full pre-tax amount |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a 23.8% combined capital gains/NIIT rate; gain is deferred, not eliminated, until eventual redemption.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Holders of a large, low-basis concentrated position seeking diversification without a sale
- Investors comfortable with a multi-year lock-up period
- Anyone whose position size meets the exchange fund's minimum eligibility requirements

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Achieves diversification without an immediate taxable event
- Defers the capital gains tax on the contributed position
- Provides exposure to a broader, pooled portfolio immediately
- Can complement other concentration-reduction strategies over time

## Important Considerations

**Lock-up surprise.** Underestimating the length and illiquidity of the fund's lock-up period.

**Deferral misunderstanding.** Assuming the gain is eliminated rather than merely deferred.

**Fee blind spot.** Failing to compare exchange fund fees and terms against alternative concentration-reduction strategies.
