# Rule 10b5-1 Trading Plans

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Creating a compliant selling framework for insiders

Corporate insiders holding significant equity face trading restrictions and legal risk around ad hoc selling decisions, and a properly structured Rule 10b5-1 trading plan provides a pre-planned, systematic approach that can offer an affirmative defense against insider trading claims.

## The Core Idea

A 10b5-1 plan is established during an open trading window, when the insider does not possess material non-public information, and specifies in advance the dates, prices, or formulas governing future sales. Once adopted, the plan executes automatically, removing the need for real-time discretionary decisions that could raise compliance concerns.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** The plan is adopted during an open trading window, without material non-public information.

**STEP 2.** Specific selling parameters, dates, prices, or formulas, are defined in advance.

**STEP 3.** The plan executes automatically according to its terms, regardless of subsequent information.

**STEP 4.** The plan is coordinated with the household's broader tax and concentration reduction strategy.

## Who This Is Designed For

- Corporate insiders and executives subject to trading restrictions and blackout periods
- Anyone with vesting equity compensation creating ongoing concentration
- Households wanting a systematic, compliant approach to reducing employer stock exposure

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Provides a potential affirmative defense against insider trading claims
- Removes emotion and timing risk from ongoing sale decisions
- Enables systematic diversification despite blackout periods
- Integrates cleanly with a broader tax and concentration strategy

## Important Considerations

**Bad timing at adoption.** Establishing a plan while in possession of material non-public information, undermining its legal protection.

**Frequent changes.** Frequently modifying or canceling the plan, which can raise compliance concerns.

**Standalone thinking.** Treating the plan purely as a compliance exercise without integrating it into a broader tax and concentration strategy.
