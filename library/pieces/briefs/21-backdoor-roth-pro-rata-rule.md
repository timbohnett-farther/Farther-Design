# Backdoor Roth IRAs and the Pro-Rata Rule

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Why pre-tax IRA balances can change the tax result

A backdoor Roth IRA, contributing after-tax dollars to a traditional IRA and converting to Roth, works cleanly only if the contributor has no other pre-tax IRA balances; otherwise, the pro-rata rule can make part of the conversion unexpectedly taxable.

## The Core Idea

The IRS treats all of a person's traditional IRAs as one aggregate account for conversion purposes. If pre-tax IRA balances exist alongside the new after-tax contribution, the pro-rata rule requires that the conversion be treated as proportionally pre-tax and after-tax, making a portion of the conversion taxable even though the intent was a tax-free backdoor Roth.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify all existing traditional IRA balances, including old rollover IRAs.

**STEP 2.** Contribute after-tax dollars to a traditional IRA, intending a backdoor Roth conversion.

**STEP 3.** Calculate the pro-rata taxable portion based on the ratio of pre-tax to total IRA balance.

**STEP 4.** Consider rolling existing pre-tax IRA balances into an employer plan beforehand, if eligible, to clear the way for a clean backdoor Roth.

## Clean Backdoor Roth vs. Pro-Rata Impacted

A $7,000 after-tax contribution is converted to Roth, compared with and without a pre-existing $93,000 pre-tax IRA balance.

| Scenario | Total IRA Balance | Taxable Portion of Conversion |
|---|---|---|
| No existing pre-tax IRA balance | $7,000 | $0 |
| $93,000 existing pre-tax IRA balance | $100,000 | ~$6,510 (93%) |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Pro-rata calculations use year-end IRA balances and are recalculated annually.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- High earners exceeding direct Roth IRA contribution income limits
- Anyone with an existing rollover or traditional IRA balance considering a backdoor Roth
- Households wanting to understand the tax impact before attempting this strategy

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Provides a legal pathway to Roth savings for high earners above income limits
- Can be executed cleanly with proper pre-planning around existing IRA balances
- Adds meaningfully to lifetime tax-free growth when done correctly
- Can be repeated annually as part of an ongoing savings strategy

## Important Considerations

**Ignoring existing balances.** Attempting a backdoor Roth without checking for existing pre-tax IRA balances first.

**Spousal oversight.** Overlooking a spouse's IRA balances, which are evaluated separately for pro-rata purposes.

**Skipped workaround.** Failing to consider rolling pre-tax IRA funds into an employer plan before executing the conversion.
