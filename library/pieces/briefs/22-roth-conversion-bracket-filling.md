# Roth Conversion Bracket Filling

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Converting only enough each year to use a targeted tax bracket

Rather than converting a large lump sum in a single year, bracket-filling converts just enough each year to use up a targeted, favorable tax bracket without spilling into a higher one.

## The Core Idea

Every taxpayer has room within their current bracket before crossing into the next, higher bracket. Bracket filling calculates that remaining room precisely and converts exactly that amount, repeating the process annually, often across several lower-income years, to convert as much as possible at the lowest sustainable tax cost.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Project total taxable income for the current year before any conversion.

**STEP 2.** Calculate the remaining room to the top of the current, targeted tax bracket.

**STEP 3.** Convert exactly that amount from a traditional account to Roth.

**STEP 4.** Repeat the process annually, adjusting for changes in income and tax law.

## Lump-Sum Conversion vs. Bracket Filling

A retiree converts $200,000 from a traditional IRA either as a single lump sum or via 5 years of bracket-filling conversions.

| Approach | Peak Marginal Bracket Reached | Approx. Total Tax Paid |
|---|---|---|
| Single lump-sum conversion | 32%–35% | Highest |
| 5-year bracket-filling conversion | 24% | Lower, spread across years |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Early retirees with a gap in income before Social Security or RMDs begin
- Households in a temporarily lower tax bracket for any reason
- Anyone wanting a disciplined, repeatable approach to Roth conversions over multiple years

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Minimizes the marginal tax rate paid on each dollar converted
- Builds tax-free Roth growth systematically over several years
- Can reduce future RMD amounts and their tax impact
- Fits naturally into a broader multi-year tax planning process

## Important Considerations

**Round numbers.** Converting a round number without calculating the actual remaining bracket room.

**Ignoring other thresholds.** Ignoring the effect of the conversion on other income-based thresholds, like IRMAA.

**Poor projection.** Failing to project the full year's income accurately before executing the conversion.
