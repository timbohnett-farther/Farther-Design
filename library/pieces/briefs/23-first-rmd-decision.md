# The First RMD Decision

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Taking the first RMD in the current year versus delaying to April 1

An account holder's first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the following year, but doing so means two RMDs are due in that following year, which can push total taxable income meaningfully higher than spreading the distributions across two separate years.

## The Core Idea

Delaying the first RMD seems appealing, but it results in two required distributions landing in the same tax year, potentially pushing income into a higher bracket or triggering other income-based thresholds. Taking the first RMD in the year it's actually due, rather than delaying, often produces a smoother, more manageable tax outcome.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Determine the year the first RMD is due, based on the account holder's age.

**STEP 2.** Compare current-year projected income against next year's projected income.

**STEP 3.** Decide whether to take the RMD by December 31 of the current year or delay to April 1 of the following year.

**STEP 4.** If delaying, plan explicitly for two RMDs landing in the same subsequent tax year.

## Taking the RMD On Time vs. Delaying

An account holder with a $60,000 first RMD and a $60,000 second RMD compares taking them in separate years versus both in one year.

| Approach | Year 1 RMD Income | Year 2 RMD Income |
|---|---|---|
| Take first RMD on time | $60,000 | $60,000 |
| Delay first RMD to April 1 | $0 | $120,000 (both RMDs) |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual RMD amounts depend on account balance and applicable life expectancy factors.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Individuals approaching their first required RMD
- Households comparing current-year versus next-year income projections
- Anyone considering delaying their first RMD without modeling the resulting two-RMD year

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Avoids an unnecessary doubling of taxable income in a single year
- Provides a smoother, more predictable tax trajectory
- Reduces the risk of triggering higher IRMAA tiers
- Preserves flexibility to model both options before deciding

## Important Considerations

**Automatic delay.** Automatically delaying the first RMD without modeling the tax impact of two distributions in one year.

**Ignoring IRMAA.** Failing to consider how the combined RMDs might affect IRMAA or other income-based thresholds.

**Missed deadline.** Missing the April 1 deadline if delaying was the chosen strategy.
