# RMD Aggregation Rules

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Which retirement accounts can be combined for RMD purposes—and which cannot

RMD aggregation rules allow certain account types, like multiple traditional IRAs, to have their combined RMD satisfied from just one of those accounts, while other account types, like 401(k)s, generally require separate distributions from each.

---

### Headline Numbers

**Yes**
Traditional IRAs can generally be aggregated together for RMD purposes

---

**No**
401(k) and other employer plans generally cannot be aggregated with each other or IRAs

---

**Separate**
Inherited accounts generally must be tracked apart from the account holder's own accounts

## The Core Idea

Understanding which accounts can be aggregated simplifies RMD management and can improve tax efficiency, for example, taking a combined IRA RMD from the account holding the least tax-efficient assets, while recognizing that employer plan accounts generally cannot be aggregated with IRAs or with each other.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Categorize every retirement account by type: traditional IRA, 401(k), 403(b), inherited, and so on.

**STEP 2.** Identify which accounts within each category can be aggregated for RMD purposes.

**STEP 3.** Calculate the combined RMD for aggregatable accounts.

**STEP 4.** Satisfy that combined RMD from any one or a combination of the aggregatable accounts, while satisfying non-aggregatable accounts separately.

## Aggregation by Account Type

A household with 3 traditional IRAs and 2 former-employer 401(k)s illustrates which RMDs can be combined.

| Account Group | Can Be Aggregated? | RMDs Required |
|---|---|---|
| 3 traditional IRAs | Yes | 1 combined RMD, from any IRA |
| 2 separate 401(k)s | No | 2 separate RMDs, one per plan |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Aggregation rules can vary by account type and specific plan provisions; verify with plan administrators.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Anyone with multiple retirement accounts across different account types
- Households managing RMDs across IRAs, 401(k)s, and inherited accounts simultaneously
- Individuals wanting to simplify RMD administration where aggregation is permitted

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Simplifies RMD administration by consolidating eligible distributions
- Allows strategic selection of which account to draw from within an aggregated group
- Reduces the risk of missing a required distribution from a non-aggregatable account
- Supports better overall tax and cash flow planning

## Important Considerations

**Over-aggregating.** Assuming all retirement accounts can be aggregated together, when employer plans generally cannot.

**Missed accounts.** Failing to satisfy each non-aggregatable account's RMD separately.

**Inherited account confusion.** Overlooking special aggregation rules for inherited accounts, which differ from the account holder's own accounts.
