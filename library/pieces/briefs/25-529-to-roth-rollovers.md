# 529-to-Roth IRA Rollovers

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## The 15-year, beneficiary, annual-limit, and lifetime-limit framework

Unused 529 plan funds can potentially be rolled into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary, but the rules involve a 15-year account age requirement, annual contribution limits, and a lifetime cap that must all be satisfied together.

---

### Headline Numbers

**15 yrs**
Minimum account age required before any 529-to-Roth rollover is permitted

---

**$35,000**
Lifetime rollover limit per beneficiary across all 529-to-Roth transfers

---

**5 yrs**
Number of prior years' contributions generally excluded from rollover eligibility

## The Core Idea

This relatively new flexibility helps address the concern that 529 funds might otherwise be trapped or subject to penalty if a beneficiary doesn't use them all for education. But it is not unlimited, the account must have existed for at least 15 years, contributions from the last five years generally don't qualify, and both annual and lifetime caps apply.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Confirm the 529 account has been open for at least 15 years.

**STEP 2.** Identify the amount eligible for rollover, excluding contributions from the preceding five years.

**STEP 3.** Roll over up to the annual Roth IRA contribution limit each year.

**STEP 4.** Continue until reaching the $35,000 lifetime limit per beneficiary, if applicable.

## Reaching the Lifetime Limit

A beneficiary with $35,000 of eligible 529 funds uses the annual Roth IRA contribution limit to roll the funds over gradually.

| Year | Annual Rollover Amount | Cumulative Rollover |
|---|---|---|
| Year 1 | $7,000 | $7,000 |
| Year 2 | $7,000 | $14,000 |
| Year 5 | $7,000 | $35,000 (lifetime limit reached) |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes the annual Roth IRA contribution limit remains constant; actual limits are subject to adjustment.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Families with unused 529 plan balances after a beneficiary's education is complete
- Beneficiaries wanting to start Roth IRA savings using leftover 529 funds
- Anyone evaluating whether their specific 529 account meets the 15-year age requirement

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Provides a productive use for unused education savings
- Avoids the early withdrawal penalty that would otherwise apply
- Jump-starts a beneficiary's own retirement savings
- Requires no new out-of-pocket contribution from the beneficiary

## Important Considerations

**Assuming full transfer.** Assuming the full 529 balance can be rolled over regardless of recent contributions.

**Account age miss.** Overlooking the 15-year account age requirement, which can disqualify newer accounts.

**Single-year assumption.** Failing to account for the annual limit, requiring the rollover to be spread across multiple years to use the full lifetime cap.
