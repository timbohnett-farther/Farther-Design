# HSA as a Long-Term Retirement Asset

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Paying current medical costs from cash while preserving HSA receipts and investments

Rather than using an HSA to pay medical bills as they arise, paying those bills from other cash and leaving the HSA invested lets the account's unique triple tax advantage compound for decades, with saved receipts allowing tax-free reimbursement anytime in the future.

## The Core Idea

Every dollar withdrawn from an HSA to pay a current medical bill is a dollar that stops compounding tax-free. By instead paying medical costs out of pocket and simply retaining the receipts, the HSA balance can be invested and grown for years or decades, then reimbursed tax-free whenever the account holder chooses, even in retirement.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Pay current qualified medical expenses using other available cash, not the HSA.

**STEP 2.** Retain receipts and documentation for those expenses indefinitely.

**STEP 3.** Invest the HSA balance for long-term growth rather than leaving it in cash.

**STEP 4.** Reimburse yourself tax-free from the HSA at any future point, using the saved receipts.

## Spend-Now vs. Invest-and-Grow

A $5,000 annual HSA contribution is either spent on current medical costs or invested and grown over 20 years at 7%.

| Approach | HSA Balance After 20 Years |
|---|---|
| Spent on current medical costs each year | Near $0 |
| Invested, medical costs paid from other cash | ~$205,000 |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes consistent $5,000 annual contributions growing at a 7% annual return over 20 years.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- HSA holders who can afford to pay current medical costs from other cash flow
- Anyone currently leaving HSA funds in cash rather than investing them
- Households wanting to maximize a uniquely tax-advantaged long-term savings vehicle

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Preserves the HSA's unique triple tax advantage for maximum long-term growth
- Builds a tax-free reserve for medical costs, including in retirement
- Provides flexibility, since non-medical withdrawals after 65 avoid penalty
- Complements other retirement accounts in a broader withdrawal strategy

## Important Considerations

**Spending it down.** Spending the HSA balance immediately rather than letting it compound.

**Leaving it in cash.** Leaving the HSA balance in cash instead of investing it for growth.

**Losing receipts.** Failing to retain receipts that could later be used for tax-free reimbursement.
