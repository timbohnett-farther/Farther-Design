## Coordinating taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, and charitable-account withdrawals

The order in which retirement income is drawn from taxable, tax-deferred, Roth, and charitable accounts can meaningfully affect lifetime tax liability, and the optimal order often isn't simply 'taxable first, tax-deferred last.'

## The Core Idea

A naive approach drains taxable accounts first, then tax-deferred, then Roth last. But this can leave large tax-deferred balances to compound into an even larger future RMD problem. A more sophisticated sequence blends withdrawals across account types each year, filling tax brackets efficiently and sometimes drawing from multiple sources simultaneously.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Project income needs and available account balances across the retirement horizon.

**STEP 2.** Model withdrawal sequences that fill tax brackets efficiently each year, rather than draining one account type at a time.

**STEP 3.** Integrate Roth conversions where they reduce lifetime tax liability.

**STEP 4.** Adjust the sequence annually as income, tax law, and account balances change.

## Naive Sequencing vs. Blended Sequencing

A retiree with taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth balances compares a simple sequential drawdown against a blended, bracket-aware approach.

| Approach | Future RMD Size | Lifetime Tax Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Naive: taxable, then tax-deferred, then Roth | Larger, compounded balance | Higher, concentrated in later years |
| Blended, bracket-aware sequencing | Smaller, actively managed | Lower, spread more evenly |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Retirees or near-retirees with balances across taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts
- Households wanting to minimize lifetime tax liability, not just current-year tax
- Anyone currently following a simple, sequential withdrawal approach without modeling alternatives

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Reduces lifetime tax liability rather than optimizing for just one year
- Manages future RMD size proactively through earlier planning
- Coordinates naturally with Roth conversion opportunities
- Adapts annually as circumstances and tax law evolve

## Important Considerations

**Sequential draining.** Draining taxable accounts first without considering the future RMD consequences on tax-deferred balances.

**Ignoring conversions.** Failing to consider blending Roth conversions into the withdrawal sequence.

**One-time plan.** Treating the sequencing decision as a one-time plan rather than an annual recalibration.
