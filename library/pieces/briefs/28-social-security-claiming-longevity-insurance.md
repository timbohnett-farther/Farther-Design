# Social Security Claiming as Longevity Insurance

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Evaluating early versus delayed claiming in the context of survivor and longevity risk

Deciding when to claim Social Security is often framed as a simple break-even calculation, but it's better understood as a longevity insurance decision, delayed claiming provides a larger, guaranteed, inflation-adjusted benefit for as long as you or a surviving spouse live.

## The Core Idea

Claiming early provides smaller monthly checks starting sooner; delaying, up to age 70, provides a permanently larger monthly benefit. Because Social Security is one of the few sources of guaranteed, inflation-adjusted lifetime income, delaying can function as insurance against the risk of a long life, and it also affects the benefit a surviving spouse may receive.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Estimate benefits at multiple claiming ages, from as early as 62 to as late as 70.

**STEP 2.** Calculate the break-even age at which cumulative delayed benefits surpass early claiming.

**STEP 3.** Weigh that break-even age against family longevity history and health considerations.

**STEP 4.** Consider the impact of the claiming decision on a surviving spouse's future benefit.

## Who This Is Designed For

- Individuals and couples approaching Social Security claiming age
- Households with family longevity history suggesting an above-average life expectancy
- Couples wanting to optimize their combined claiming strategy, including survivor benefit considerations

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Provides guaranteed, inflation-adjusted income for as long as you live
- Functions as insurance against the risk of outliving other assets
- Can increase the benefit available to a surviving spouse
- Complements a broader retirement withdrawal sequencing strategy

## Important Considerations

**Claiming reflexively early.** Claiming as early as possible without considering the longevity insurance value of delaying.

**Ignoring survivor impact.** Ignoring the impact of the claiming decision on a surviving spouse's future benefit.

**Break-even tunnel vision.** Treating the break-even calculation as the only factor, without considering health and family longevity.
