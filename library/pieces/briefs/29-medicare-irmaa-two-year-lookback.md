# Medicare IRMAA: The Two-Year Lookback

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Avoiding unexpected Medicare premium surcharges after large income events

Medicare's Income-Related Monthly Adjustment Amount (IRMAA) surcharge is based on income from two years prior, meaning a large income event today, a Roth conversion, a business sale, or a big capital gain, can trigger an unexpected premium increase two years from now.

## The Core Idea

Because IRMAA looks back two years, the connection between an income decision and its Medicare premium consequence is easy to miss in the moment. A household celebrating a successful business sale or executing a large Roth conversion may not realize until two years later that their Medicare premiums have increased substantially as a direct result.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** A large income event occurs in a given tax year.

**STEP 2.** That year's income is reported and becomes part of the IRS record.

**STEP 3.** Two years later, Medicare uses that reported income to determine the IRMAA surcharge.

**STEP 4.** The higher premium applies for the full year, regardless of the household's current income.

## Crossing an IRMAA Threshold

A couple's income crosses a key IRMAA threshold by a small margin due to a Roth conversion two years earlier.

## Who This Is Designed For

- Anyone approaching or already on Medicare who is considering a Roth conversion or other large income event
- Households who have experienced a business sale, large capital gain, or bonus in recent years
- Individuals surprised by a Medicare premium increase without understanding its two-year-old cause

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Prevents unexpected Medicare premium increases from catching a household off guard
- Allows large income events to be timed and sized more strategically
- May identify appeal opportunities after certain life-changing events
- Integrates seamlessly into broader multi-year tax planning

## Important Considerations

**No forward modeling.** Executing a large income event without modeling its IRMAA impact two years later.

**Missed appeal.** Failing to explore an IRMAA appeal after a qualifying life-changing event, such as retirement or loss of income.

**Timing confusion.** Assuming current income, rather than income from two years prior, determines this year's premium.
