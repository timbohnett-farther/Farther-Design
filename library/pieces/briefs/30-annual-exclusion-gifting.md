# Annual Exclusion Gifting

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Using annual gifts, gift-splitting, and documentation correctly

The annual gift tax exclusion allows individuals to gift a set amount per recipient each year without using any lifetime exemption, but maximizing this benefit across a family requires careful tracking, documentation, and, for married couples, proper gift-splitting.

---

### Headline Numbers

**$19,000**
2025 annual exclusion per recipient, per person, subject to annual adjustment

---

**$38,000**
Combined annual exclusion per recipient for a gift-splitting married couple

---

**$0**
Lifetime exemption used for gifts that stay within the annual exclusion

## The Core Idea

Each person can gift the annual exclusion amount to as many recipients as they choose every year, entirely gift-tax-free and without touching their lifetime exemption. Married couples can double this amount per recipient through gift-splitting, and consistent, well-documented annual gifting compounds meaningfully over years for larger families.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify all intended gift recipients for the year, children, grandchildren, or others.

**STEP 2.** Confirm each gift stays within the annual exclusion amount per recipient.

**STEP 3.** For married couples, elect gift-splitting to double the per-recipient amount, if desired.

**STEP 4.** Document each gift's date, amount, and recipient for the family's records.

## Family Gifting at Scale

## Who This Is Designed For

- Families wanting to transfer wealth gradually and gift-tax-free over time
- Married couples wanting to maximize gifting through gift-splitting
- Anyone with multiple children, grandchildren, or other intended gift recipients

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Transfers wealth outside the estate without using lifetime exemption
- Can be repeated every year for meaningful cumulative impact
- Doubles per-recipient capacity for married couples through gift-splitting
- Simple to execute compared to more complex trust structures

## Important Considerations

**Inconsistent tracking.** Failing to track annual gifts consistently across multiple recipients and years.

**Missed gift-splitting.** Overlooking the gift-splitting election for married couples, missing the doubled amount.

**Basis blind spot.** Gifting appreciated assets without considering the recipient's resulting cost basis.
