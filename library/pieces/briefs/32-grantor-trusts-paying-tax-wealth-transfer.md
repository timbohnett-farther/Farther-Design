# Grantor Trusts: Paying Tax as a Wealth-Transfer Tool

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## How the grantor's payment of trust income tax can preserve trust assets

When a grantor trust's income tax is paid personally by the grantor rather than by the trust, that tax payment is not treated as a taxable gift, effectively allowing the grantor to transfer additional wealth to the trust's beneficiaries tax-free every year.

**Ongoing**
Duration of this benefit, repeating every year the trust remains a grantor trust

## The Core Idea

A grantor trust is structured so its income is taxed to the grantor personally, not the trust. Because the grantor is legally obligated to pay that tax, doing so is not considered an additional gift to the trust, even though it effectively shifts additional wealth to the trust's beneficiaries each year the grantor's personal assets shrink by the tax paid, while the trust's assets compound undiminished.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Establish a properly structured grantor trust, typically for estate planning purposes.

**STEP 2.** The trust generates taxable income each year from its investments.

**STEP 3.** The grantor pays that income tax personally, using funds outside the trust.

**STEP 4.** The trust's assets continue compounding without reduction for income tax, effectively transferring additional value to beneficiaries.

## Grantor-Paid Tax vs. Trust-Paid Tax

A trust generating $200,000 of annual taxable income compares having the grantor pay the tax personally versus having the trust pay it.

| Approach | Trust Assets Reduced By Tax? | Effective Additional Transfer to Trust |
|---|---|---|
| Grantor pays tax personally | No | ~$60,000+ per year (tax-free) |
| Trust pays its own tax | Yes | $0 additional transfer |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a combined effective tax rate of roughly 30% on trust income; actual rates vary by jurisdiction and income type.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Grantors of irrevocable grantor trusts, such as SLATs, IDGTs, or dynasty trusts
- High-net-worth households using grantor trust structures for wealth transfer
- Anyone whose estate planning includes grantor trust income tax obligations

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Transfers additional wealth to trust beneficiaries without using any gift tax exemption
- Allows the trust's assets to compound undiminished by income tax
- Reduces the grantor's own taxable estate through the tax payments
- Compounds meaningfully over the life of a long-term trust

## Important Considerations

**Toggling off status.** Inadvertently or unnecessarily terminating grantor trust status, ending this benefit.

**Cash flow strain.** Failing to plan for the grantor's ongoing personal cash flow needs to pay the trust's tax liability.

**Underappreciating the benefit.** Overlooking this as a deliberate, sanctioned wealth transfer tool rather than simply a tax obligation.
