# Spousal Lifetime Access Trusts (SLATs)

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Transferring assets outside an estate while preserving indirect household access

A Spousal Lifetime Access Trust (SLAT) allows one spouse to gift assets out of their taxable estate into an irrevocable trust for the benefit of the other spouse, potentially preserving indirect household access to the assets even after the gift is made.

## The Core Idea

By naming the non-donor spouse as a trust beneficiary, a SLAT allows a household to remove assets from the donor's estate for estate tax purposes, while the family may still benefit indirectly if the trust makes distributions to the non-donor spouse, who in turn can use those funds for the household's benefit.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** One spouse establishes an irrevocable trust naming the other spouse as a lifetime beneficiary.

**STEP 2.** The donor spouse gifts assets into the trust, using gift tax exemption.

**STEP 3.** The trust may make discretionary distributions to the non-donor spouse over their lifetime.

**STEP 4.** Assets in the trust, and their future growth, are removed from the donor spouse's taxable estate.

## Assets Inside the Estate vs. Inside a SLAT

A $3,000,000 gift is compared remaining in the donor's estate versus transferred into a SLAT for the benefit of the other spouse.

| Approach | In Donor's Taxable Estate? | Household Access Preserved? |
|---|---|---|
| Assets remain with donor spouse | Yes | Direct |
| Assets transferred to SLAT | No | Indirect, via non-donor spouse |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Married couples wanting to use gift tax exemption while preserving some household access
- Households with taxable estates seeking to remove future appreciation from the estate
- Couples comfortable with the structural risks tied to divorce or the non-donor spouse's death

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Removes assets and their future growth from the donor spouse's taxable estate
- Preserves potential indirect access to trust assets for the household
- Uses gift tax exemption efficiently while retaining some family flexibility
- Can be structured by both spouses reciprocally, with careful attention to reciprocal trust doctrine

## Important Considerations

**Reciprocal trust doctrine.** Creating mirror-image SLATs for each other in a way that could be unwound by the IRS.

**Divorce risk.** Failing to plan for the loss of indirect access if the couple later divorces.

**Death of non-donor spouse.** Overlooking that the donor spouse loses indirect access entirely if the non-donor spouse predeceases them.
