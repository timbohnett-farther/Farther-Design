# Irrevocable Life Insurance Trusts (ILITs)

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Managing life-insurance ownership, estate inclusion, and Crummey administration

An Irrevocable Life Insurance Trust (ILIT) owns a life insurance policy on the insured's life, keeping the death benefit outside the insured's taxable estate, but proper administration, including annual Crummey notices, is essential to preserve the intended tax treatment.

## The Core Idea

If the insured personally owns a life insurance policy, its death benefit is generally included in their taxable estate. By having an ILIT own the policy from the start, and funding premiums through properly noticed annual exclusion gifts, the death benefit can pass to beneficiaries entirely outside the insured's estate.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Establish an irrevocable trust and have it apply for and own a new life insurance policy on the insured.

**STEP 2.** The insured gifts cash to the trust each year to cover premium payments.

**STEP 3.** The trustee sends Crummey notices to beneficiaries, giving them a withdrawal right that qualifies the gift for the annual exclusion.

**STEP 4.** At the insured's death, the policy proceeds pass to the trust, outside the taxable estate.

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households with taxable estates who hold or are considering life insurance
- Anyone wanting life insurance proceeds to pass to heirs outside the taxable estate
- Families needing liquidity at death, for estate taxes or business succession, without estate inclusion

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Keeps life insurance proceeds outside the insured's taxable estate
- Provides liquidity to heirs or the estate without adding to estate tax exposure
- Can be funded efficiently using annual exclusion gifts
- Supports broader estate and business succession liquidity planning

## Important Considerations

**Existing policy transfer.** Transferring an already-owned policy into the ILIT, triggering the 3-year look-back rule if death occurs too soon after.

**Missed Crummey notices.** Failing to send proper annual Crummey notices, jeopardizing the annual exclusion treatment of premium gifts.

**Underfunded premiums.** Failing to adequately gift funds to the trust to cover ongoing premium payments.
