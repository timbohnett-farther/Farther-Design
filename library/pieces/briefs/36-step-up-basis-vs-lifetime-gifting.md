# Step-Up in Basis vs. Lifetime Gifting

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Comparing the transfer-tax benefit of a gift with potential loss of basis adjustment

Gifting an appreciated asset during life transfers the donor's original cost basis to the recipient, while the same asset, if left to be inherited instead, generally receives a step-up in basis to its value at death, a critical trade-off in deciding whether to gift now or wait.

---

### Headline Numbers

**Carryover**
Basis treatment for a gifted asset during the donor's lifetime

---

**Stepped-up**
Basis treatment for most inherited assets, adjusted to fair market value at death

---

**0%**
Capital gains tax owed on appreciation that occurred before death, thanks to the step-up

## The Core Idea

A highly appreciated asset gifted during life carries the donor's low original cost basis forward to the recipient, who will owe capital gains tax on that full appreciation when eventually sold. The same asset, left to be inherited instead, generally receives a basis step-up to its date-of-death value, erasing that embedded gain entirely, a major consideration when low-basis assets are involved in estate planning.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify the asset's current cost basis and fair market value.

**STEP 2.** Compare the potential future capital gains tax if gifted now, using carryover basis, against the outcome if inherited later, with a stepped-up basis.

**STEP 3.** Weigh this basis consideration against the benefit of removing the asset's future appreciation from the taxable estate through an earlier gift.

**STEP 4.** Decide gift timing asset by asset, since low-basis, highly appreciated assets favor holding, while cash or high-basis assets favor gifting freely.

## Gift Now vs. Hold Until Death

A $1,000,000 asset with a $100,000 cost basis is gifted during life versus held and inherited at death.

| Approach | Recipient's Basis | Capital Gains Tax If Sold Immediately |
|---|---|---|
| Gifted during life | $100,000 (carryover) | ~$214,200 on $900k gain |
| Inherited at death | $1,000,000 (stepped-up) | $0 |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Assumes a 23.8% combined capital gains/NIIT rate; actual step-up rules and outcomes depend on current tax law and estate size.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Households holding significantly appreciated, low-basis assets
- Anyone weighing lifetime gifting against holding assets until death for estate planning purposes
- Families with estates below the estate tax exemption threshold, where step-up may outweigh gifting benefits

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Highlights when holding a low-basis asset until death may outperform gifting it during life
- Clarifies which assets are best candidates for lifetime gifting versus retention
- Supports more precise, asset-by-asset estate planning decisions
- Can meaningfully reduce a family's overall lifetime and estate tax burden when applied correctly

## Important Considerations

**Blanket gifting.** Gifting all appreciated assets during life without considering the lost step-up benefit.

**Estate size oversight.** Failing to consider whether the estate is even likely to be subject to estate tax, which changes the calculus significantly.

**Cash flow assumption.** Assuming lifetime gifting is always superior without comparing it to the specific step-up benefit available at death.
