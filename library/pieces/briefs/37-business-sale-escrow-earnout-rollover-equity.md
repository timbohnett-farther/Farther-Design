# Business-Sale Escrow, Earnout, and Rollover Equity

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Understanding the difference between headline sale price and usable liquidity

A business sale's headline price rarely equals the cash a founder can actually use immediately, escrow holdbacks, earnout contingencies, and rollover equity requirements can each significantly reduce near-term liquidity.

---

### Headline Numbers

**10-20%**
Typical escrow holdback percentage, held back for a defined period after closing

---

**1-3 yrs**
Common earnout period during which contingent payments may or may not materialize

---

**10-30%**
Typical rollover equity percentage required by many private equity buyers

## The Core Idea

A headline sale price might be $50 million, but after a 15% escrow holdback, a partially achieved earnout, and a 20% rollover equity requirement, the cash actually available at closing can be substantially less, a critical distinction for post-sale planning around taxes, gifting, and diversification.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Identify the headline sale price and each deal term reducing immediate liquidity.

**STEP 2.** Model the escrow holdback and its expected release timeline.

**STEP 3.** Model the earnout structure and realistic probability of achieving its targets.

**STEP 4.** Account for any required rollover equity, which remains illiquid and at risk in the new ownership structure.

## Headline Price vs. Usable Liquidity at Closing

A $50,000,000 headline sale includes a 15% escrow, a $5,000,000 earnout (50% probability), and a 20% rollover requirement.

| Component | Amount | Available at Closing? |
|---|---|---|
| Headline price | $50,000,000 | — |
| Escrow holdback (15%) | $7,500,000 | No, held 12-24 months |
| Rollover equity (20%) | $10,000,000 | No, illiquid |
| Cash at closing | ~$32,500,000 | Yes |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual deal terms vary significantly by transaction and negotiation; earnout amounts are separately contingent.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Founders and business owners negotiating or approaching a sale
- Anyone planning immediate post-sale liquidity needs, taxes, gifting, or diversification
- Households needing to understand true usable cash before making other financial commitments

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Prevents overcommitting to spending or gifting based on the headline price alone
- Enables more accurate tax and cash flow planning around the actual liquidity timeline
- Clarifies which portions of proceeds remain at risk through rollover equity or earnout contingency
- Supports better-informed negotiation of deal terms before signing

## Important Considerations

**Headline anchoring.** Planning finances based on the full headline price rather than realistic usable liquidity.

**Earnout overconfidence.** Assuming full earnout achievement without stress-testing the underlying assumptions.

**Rollover risk blind spot.** Treating rollover equity as equivalent to cash rather than as a new, illiquid, at-risk investment.
