# Pre-Sale Planning: The 12-Month Founder Checklist

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Sequencing tax, estate, liquidity, charitable, and governance decisions before a sale

Many of the most valuable tax and estate planning strategies available to a founder, like gifting shares or establishing certain trusts, must be completed before a sale is imminent, making a structured 12-month pre-sale checklist essential.

## The Core Idea

Once a sale process is well underway, particularly after a letter of intent is signed, many planning opportunities close off or become significantly riskier from a tax perspective. Gifting shares to a trust or family members, for example, is far more defensible for valuation purposes when done well before a sale is imminent, rather than once a deal is essentially certain.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Begin the planning process at least 12 months before an anticipated sale, ideally earlier.

**STEP 2.** Evaluate and execute any gifting or trust funding strategies while valuation is not yet influenced by a pending deal.

**STEP 3.** Coordinate QSBS eligibility verification, charitable planning, and entity structuring well ahead of the transaction.

**STEP 4.** Finalize liquidity and tax payment planning as the deal approaches closing.

## Early Planning vs. Planning After a Signed LOI

A founder considers gifting shares to a trust either 12+ months before a sale or after a letter of intent has been signed.

| Timing | Valuation Defensibility | Planning Flexibility |
|---|---|---|
| 12+ months before sale | Strong, pre-deal valuation | High, most strategies available |
| After LOI signed | Weak, deal terms may control valuation | Low, many strategies foreclosed |


*Hypothetical illustration only. Actual planning windows and outcomes depend on deal specifics, applicable law, and professional guidance.*

## Who This Is Designed For

- Founders and business owners anticipating a sale within the next one to two years
- Anyone wanting to preserve gifting, trust, and charitable planning opportunities before a deal is imminent
- Households needing to coordinate multiple advisors ahead of a major liquidity event

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Preserves access to planning strategies that become unavailable or risky closer to a sale
- Improves the defensibility of any pre-sale gifting or trust funding valuations
- Reduces the chance of a rushed, reactive planning process during the deal itself
- Coordinates tax, estate, and liquidity planning into one integrated timeline

## Important Considerations

**Waiting too long.** Beginning planning only after a deal process is already underway, foreclosing many strategies.

**Uncoordinated advisors.** Failing to coordinate tax, legal, and wealth advisors early enough to execute a cohesive plan.

**Missed QSBS verification.** Neglecting to verify QSBS eligibility well before a sale, when documentation is easier to establish.
