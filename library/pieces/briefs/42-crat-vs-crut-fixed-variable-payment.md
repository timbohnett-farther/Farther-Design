# CRAT vs. CRUT: Choosing a Fixed Payment or a Variable Payment

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Charitable Remainder Annuity Trust versus Charitable Remainder Unitrust payout structures

A CRAT pays a fixed dollar amount every year regardless of the trust's investment performance, while a CRUT pays a percentage of the trust's revalued balance annually, meaning the choice between them is fundamentally a choice between payment certainty and payment growth potential.

---

### Headline Numbers

**Fixed**
CRAT payment structure: a set dollar amount, unchanged for the life of the trust

---

**Variable**
CRUT payment structure: a percentage of the trust's value, revalued annually

---

**No additional gifts**
Key CRAT limitation: additional contributions to a CRAT are not permitted after initial funding

## The Core Idea

A CRAT offers predictability, useful for income beneficiaries who need a stable, known cash flow, but its fixed payment doesn't adjust for inflation or strong investment performance, and no additional contributions can ever be made. A CRUT's payment can rise in good markets and fall in poor ones, offering growth potential and inflation sensitivity, but less certainty year to year.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Determine whether the income beneficiary prioritizes payment certainty or payment growth potential.

**STEP 2.** For a CRAT, fix the annual dollar payment amount at funding, based on a chosen percentage of the initial contribution.

**STEP 3.** For a CRUT, set the annual payout percentage, to be applied against the trust's revalued balance each year.

**STEP 4.** Monitor the trust's performance annually, understanding that only the CRUT's payment will adjust accordingly.

## CRAT vs. CRUT Under Different Market Conditions

A $3,000,000 trust with a 5% payout rate is modeled as a CRAT and a CRUT across a strong market year and a weak market year.

|---|---|---|
| CRAT (fixed) | $150,000 | $150,000 |
| CRUT (variable, 5% of revalued balance) | ~$165,000 (if balance grows) | ~$135,000 (if balance falls) |

## Who This Is Designed For

- Income beneficiaries prioritizing either payment stability or growth potential
- Donors deciding between a CRAT and a CRUT for a specific charitable and income planning goal
- Anyone contributing a single asset upfront without plans for additional future contributions

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- CRATs provide payment certainty, useful for predictable budgeting needs
- CRUTs provide payment growth potential, useful for inflation-sensitive income needs
- Both defer capital gains tax on the contributed asset's sale inside the trust
- Both provide a partial charitable income tax deduction at funding

## Important Considerations

**Choosing CRAT for growth needs.** Selecting a CRAT when the beneficiary actually needs income that keeps pace with inflation.

**Choosing CRUT for stability needs.** Selecting a CRUT when the beneficiary needs a guaranteed, unchanging income stream.

**Additional contribution assumption.** Assuming additional assets can be added to a CRAT later, which is generally not permitted.
