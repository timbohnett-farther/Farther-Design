# Dynasty Trusts: Designing Wealth to Last Beyond One Generation

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## Long-duration trust planning, generation-skipping transfer tax allocation, trustee selection, distribution standards, situs selection, and governance

A dynasty trust is designed to hold and grow family wealth across multiple generations, often for as long as applicable state law permits, using generation-skipping transfer tax exemption allocation, careful trustee selection, and defined governance to preserve the family's wealth and values over time.

## The Core Idea

Without specific planning, wealth transferred across multiple generations can be taxed at each transition, once at the parents' level and again at the grandchildren's level. A dynasty trust, properly funded with generation-skipping transfer tax exemption, can hold assets for the benefit of multiple future generations without repeated transfer taxation at each generational transition, provided it is established in a jurisdiction and structure that supports long duration.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Select a trust situs jurisdiction that supports long or indefinite trust duration.

**STEP 2.** Fund the trust and allocate available generation-skipping transfer tax exemption to shield it from GST tax.

**STEP 3.** Establish clear distribution standards and governance provisions to guide trustees across multiple generations.

**STEP 4.** Select trustees, and often a trust protector, capable of administering the trust responsibly over a very long time horizon.

## Who This Is Designed For

- UHNW families wanting to preserve wealth across multiple future generations
- Households with generation-skipping transfer tax exemption available to allocate
- Families seeking to establish long-term governance and values alongside financial assets

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Shields trust assets from repeated transfer taxation at each generational transition
- Preserves and can grow family wealth over a very long time horizon
- Establishes clear governance and distribution standards for future generations
- Can incorporate family values and philanthropic intentions into its structure

## Important Considerations

**Rigid distribution standards.** Drafting overly rigid standards that fail to adapt to future generations' differing circumstances.

**Poor trustee succession planning.** Failing to plan for trustee succession across a multi-generational time horizon.

**Situs oversight.** Selecting a trust situs jurisdiction that doesn't support the desired long duration or governance flexibility.
