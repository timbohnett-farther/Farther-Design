# The Rockefeller Waterfall Method: Creating a Family Capital System

**CLIENT EDUCATION GUIDE**

## A structured generational-wealth framework using trusts, life insurance, governance, and controlled distributions—not a single statutory trust type

The Rockefeller Waterfall method is a structured, systems-based approach to family capital, using a combination of trusts, life insurance, disciplined reinvestment, and family governance to sustain and grow wealth across generations, rather than relying on any single legal structure alone.

## The Core Idea

Rather than treating each generation's inheritance as a one-time event, the waterfall method treats family capital as an ongoing system, income generated by family assets funds insurance premiums and reinvestment, protected structures shield the capital from creditors, taxes, and poor decision-making, and disciplined governance guides how and when capital flows to the next generation, sustaining the cycle indefinitely.

## How It Works

**STEP 1.** Establish trust and insurance structures designed to protect and grow the family's core capital base.

**STEP 2.** Direct income generated by the family's assets toward funding insurance premiums and reinvestment, rather than direct consumption.

**STEP 3.** Establish family governance, meetings, education, and defined distribution standards, to guide how capital flows to the next generation.

**STEP 4.** Repeat the cycle each generation, with reinvested and protected capital continuing to compound and support the family's long-term goals.

## Who This Is Designed For

- UHNW families wanting a systematic, multi-generational approach to family capital rather than a single trust or event
- Households seeking to combine trusts, insurance, and governance into one cohesive family capital strategy
- Families prioritizing education and governance alongside legal and financial structures

## Key Benefits at a Glance

- Treats family wealth as an ongoing system rather than a series of one-time transfers
- Combines multiple protective structures, trusts and insurance, for durability
- Emphasizes family governance and education alongside financial planning
- Designed to sustain and compound capital across many future generations

## Important Considerations

**Structure without governance.** Implementing trusts and insurance without the accompanying family governance and education component.

**One-and-done mindset.** Treating the waterfall as a single implementation rather than an ongoing, actively managed system.

**Underestimating complexity.** Failing to coordinate the many moving pieces, trusts, insurance, investments, and governance, across a unified plan.
