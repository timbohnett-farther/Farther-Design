## Move the Growth, Not Just the Money

**For families above the exemption who want every dollar of transfer capacity working, and who would rather decide the timing than leave it to an estate.**

For nearly a decade estate planning ran against a clock. The exemption was scheduled to fall by half at the end of 2025, and families raced to use it. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act ended that. The exemption is now $15 million per person, $30 million per couple, permanent and indexed for inflation from 2027.

The deadline is gone. The arithmetic is not. A dollar gifted today takes all of its future growth out of your estate with it, and at 40% that growth is the entire game. The families who do best from here are not the ones who moved fastest. They are the ones who use the transfer capacity that does not consume exemption at all, and then spend exemption where it buys the most.

## The Capacity You Already Have

### What costs exemption and what does not

| Transfer | Uses annual exclusion | Uses lifetime exemption | 2026 limit |
|---|---|---|---|
| Tuition paid directly to the institution | No | No | Unlimited |
| Medical expenses paid directly to the provider | No | No | Unlimited |
| Annual exclusion gift | Yes | No | $19,000 per recipient |
| Same gift split by a married couple | Yes | No | $38,000 per recipient |
| Gift to a non-citizen spouse | Yes | No | $194,000 |
| Larger outright gift | To the first $19,000 | Yes, above that | — |
| Zeroed-out GRAT remainder | No | Minimal | — |

The first two rows are the most underused provisions in the code. Paying a grandchild's tuition directly to the school costs nothing against either limit, in any amount, every year.

### Three exemptions, and one that behaves differently

| Exemption | 2026 amount | Portable to a surviving spouse |
|---|---|---|
| Estate and gift, unified | $15,000,000 | Yes, by election on a timely return |
| Generation-skipping transfer | $15,000,000 | No |
| Annual gift exclusion | $19,000 per recipient | Not applicable, expires yearly |

The generation-skipping exemption is not portable. A surviving spouse cannot inherit the unused portion the way they can with the estate exemption. Allocation is also not automatic for most transfers to trusts that could benefit grandchildren, only for direct skips. Unallocated exemption is exemption lost.

### What the rate environment favors

| Technique | Works when | Condition at a 5.20% Section 7520 rate |
|---|---|---|
| Grantor retained annuity trust | Assets outgrow the 7520 rate | Higher hurdle than the 2020 to 2022 window |
| Intrafamily loan | Assets outgrow the applicable federal rate | Mid-term 4.35%, long-term 4.98% |
| Sale to a grantor trust | Assets outgrow the note rate | Same hurdle as the loan |
| Charitable lead annuity trust | Rates are higher | Improved |
| Qualified personal residence trust | Rates are higher | Improved |

The rate resets monthly and locks at funding. A zeroed-out GRAT consumes almost no exemption, which means it stacks on top of other planning rather than competing with it.

### What annual exclusion gifting compounds into

| Recipients | Annual gift by a married couple | Value at year 20 growing at 6% | Exemption used |
|---|---|---|---|
| Three children | $114,000 | $4,194,000 | None |
| Three children and six grandchildren | $342,000 | $12,581,000 | None |

### What a coordinated transfer plan gives you

- Transfer capacity used before exemption is touched, through exclusions, tuition, and medical payments
- Future appreciation removed from the estate rather than only the current value
- Generation-skipping exemption deliberately allocated rather than left to default rules
- Techniques matched to the current rate environment rather than to a template
- Gifts made while you can see how the next generation handles them
- No clawback risk on gifts already completed under the prior exemption

### Why this matters now

Permanence removed the pressure and raised the stakes. A $40 million estate growing at 6% becomes $128 million in twenty years, and the exemption grows only with inflation. The gap between those two curves is where the entire estate tax lives.

Two other things changed. Plans built to capture a disappearing exemption may now be structured for a world that no longer exists and deserve review. And with a permanently higher federal threshold, state estate taxes have become the binding constraint for many families, including in states with cliff provisions where exceeding the threshold by a few percent can forfeit the exemption entirely.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite couple holds a $40 million estate. They used $10 million of combined exemption in an earlier transfer, leaving $20 million. They have three children and six grandchildren, and a $10 million concentrated position they expect to grow faster than the market.

### The cost of no action

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Estate today | $40,000,000 |
| Value at year 20, growing at 6% | $128,300,000 |
| Remaining combined exemption | $20,000,000 |
| Taxable estate | $108,300,000 |
| Federal estate tax at 40% | $43,320,000 |

Nothing went wrong in that table. The assets simply grew where they were.

### The benefit of acting now

Four layers, applied together.

| Layer | Commitment | Exemption used | Value outside the estate at year 20 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Annual exclusion gifts to nine recipients | $342,000 per year | $0 | $12,581,000 |
| Tuition and medical paid directly | $150,000 per year | $0 | $5,518,000 |
| Ten-year zeroed-out GRAT on the $10M position | One transfer | Minimal | $9,147,000 |
| Dynasty trust with GST exemption allocated | $20,000,000 | $20,000,000 | $64,140,000 |
| **Total** | | **$20,000,000** | **$91,386,000** |

### Side by side

| Measure | No action | Layered plan |
|---|---|---|
| Exemption consumed | $0 today, $20M at death | $20,000,000 today |
| Value outside the taxable estate at year 20 | $0 | $91,386,000 |
| Value transferred per dollar of exemption used | — | $4.57 |
| Federal estate tax avoided at 40% | $0 | $28,554,000 |
| Generations covered | One | Multiple, through GST allocation |
| Control during life | Full | Retained over the GRAT annuity and the entity interests |

The first two layers cost no exemption whatsoever. They account for more than $18 million of the total, funded entirely from cash flow the family was going to spend or leave behind anyway.

### What the table does not show

Gifted assets carry over basis rather than receiving an adjustment at death. For this family, well above the exemption with two decades of compounding ahead, the transfer tax saved dwarfs the capital gains cost. For a family near the threshold, the calculation can reverse. Gifting also means the assets are gone, which is a decision about your own security before it is a decision about tax.

*Assumptions: 6% annual growth on all assets, the $10 million position growing at 10% inside a ten-year GRAT funded at a 5.20% Section 7520 rate, a 40% federal estate tax rate, a twenty-year horizon, and gifting sustained annually without interruption. Exemption is assumed flat rather than indexed, which understates future capacity. No state estate tax, income tax on gifted assets, or administration costs are modeled. GRAT results depend entirely on actual asset performance and the grantor surviving the term.*

Three points carry the piece. Substantial transfer capacity exists before exemption is touched at all, and direct tuition and medical payments are the most overlooked provisions in the code. The generation-skipping exemption is separate, is not portable, and is not automatically allocated to most trusts, which makes it the easiest exemption to waste. And what you are really transferring is not the asset but its future growth, which is why the timing of a gift matters more than its size.
