## The Order of Operations

### Location first, then harvesting, then selection

Most tax conversations start with which investment to buy. That is the last and least important decision. The order that produces the most value runs the other way: decide which account holds which asset, then harvest losses where they can be used, then select the vehicle.

### The rates that drive every decision

| Type of income | Top federal rate | Net investment income tax | Combined |
|---|---|---|---|
| Ordinary income and short-term gains | 37% | 3.8% | 40.8% |
| Long-term gains and qualified dividends | 20% | 3.8% | 23.8% |
| QSBS gain, five-year hold | 0% | None | 0% |
| QSBS unexcluded portion, three or four-year hold | 28% | May apply | 28% or higher |
| Municipal bond interest | 0% | None | 0% |
| Unrealized appreciation held until death | 0% | None | 0% |

The spread between the first row and the second is 17 percentage points. Every asset location decision is really a decision about which of those two rows applies.

### Where things belong

| Asset | Best held in | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Taxable bonds and credit funds | Tax-deferred | Interest is ordinary income |
| Absolute return and hedge strategies | Tax-deferred | Gains are largely short-term |
| REITs and high-turnover strategies | Tax-deferred | Distributions are ordinary income |
| Broad equity index exposure | Taxable | Low turnover, qualified dividends, basis step-up |
| Direct indexing | Taxable | Harvesting only works where there are gains to offset |
| Municipal bonds | Taxable | Tax-exempt interest is wasted inside a deferred account |
| Highest expected growth | Roth | Growth is never taxed |

### What changed for business owners

| Feature | Stock issued on or before July 4, 2025 | Stock issued after July 4, 2025 |
|---|---|---|
| Holding period for any exclusion | 5 years | 3 years |
| Exclusion at three years | None | 50% |
| Exclusion at four years | None | 75% |
| Exclusion at five years | 100% | 100% |
| Per-issuer cap | $10M or 10x basis | $15M or 10x basis, indexed from 2027 |
| Company gross asset ceiling | $50M | $75M, indexed from 2027 |

Two cautions. Gain that is not excluded under the three and four-year tiers is taxed at 28% rather than the standard long-term rate, which makes the partial tiers less generous than they appear. And pre-OBBBA stock is grandfathered under the old rules, so founders with layered issuances are governed batch by batch.

### The tool almost nobody uses

| Strategy | Adoption among high-net-worth investors |
|---|---|
| Passive index funds | 91% |
| Direct indexing | 18% |

Direct indexing holds the underlying securities rather than a pooled fund, which allows individual positions to be sold at a loss while the overall exposure stays intact. Those losses offset gains elsewhere, including gains from reducing a concentrated position. Nine in ten investors at this level own index exposure. Fewer than one in five own it in the form that generates usable losses.

### What disciplined tax structure gives you

- The 17-point spread between ordinary and long-term rates working for you rather than against you
- Harvested losses available to fund concentration reduction without writing a check
- QSBS exits timed to the tier that actually maximizes what you keep
- Municipal exposure sized against your real marginal rate rather than a rule of thumb
- Appreciation positioned to receive a basis adjustment rather than be realized
- A coordinated plan your CPA can execute rather than react to in April

### Why this matters now

The sunset provisions are gone. That sounds like less urgency and is actually the opposite. When a deadline drives planning, everyone acts at once and often badly. When the rules are permanent, the advantage accrues to whoever structures first and compounds it longest. QSBS in particular now rewards founders who know their issuance dates and their tier boundaries, because the difference between a year-three exit and a year-five exit can be measured in millions.

*Rates reflect federal law as of the publication date and exclude state and local tax, which varies materially. Several states do not conform to the federal QSBS rules. Consult your tax advisor.*

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite founder holds qualified small business stock issued after July 4, 2025, with a $15 million built-in gain. An acquirer has made an offer. She is three years in.

### The cost of no action

| Exit timing | Gain excluded | Gain taxable | Federal tax | Proceeds kept |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| No QSBS treatment | $0 | $15.00M | $3.57M | $11.43M |
| Year three, 50% exclusion | $7.50M | $7.50M | $2.10M | $12.90M |
| Year four, 75% exclusion | $11.25M | $3.75M | $1.05M | $13.95M |
| Year five, 100% exclusion | $15.00M | $0 | $0 | $15.00M |

Selling twenty-four months earlier costs $2.10 million. That is the entire question, and it is answerable with a calendar.

### The second decision

After the sale she holds $15 million alongside $2 million of existing tax-deferred capacity. Today the tax-deferred account holds equity index funds and the taxable account holds a bond fund. That arrangement is backwards.

| Position | Current location | Annual tax cost |
|---|---|---|
| $2M bond fund at 5% yield | Taxable account | $40,800 |
| $2M equity index at 2% dividend | Tax-deferred account | $0 today, but no step-up ever |

| Position | Corrected location | Annual tax cost |
|---|---|---|
| $2M bond fund at 5% yield | Tax-deferred account | $0 |
| $2M equity index at 2% dividend | Taxable account | $9,520 |

### The benefit of acting now

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| Wait until the five-year mark before closing | $2,100,000 of federal tax eliminated |
| Swap the bond fund and the equity index between accounts | $31,280 of annual drag removed |
| Hold the equity sleeve through direct indexing | Generates harvestable losses the pooled fund cannot |
| Position appreciation for basis adjustment rather than realization | Preserves the zero-rate row in the table above |

### Side by side

| Measure | Before | After |
|---|---|---|
| QSBS exit timing | Year three | Year five |
| Federal tax on the sale | $2.10M | $0 |
| Bond fund located in | Taxable | Tax-deferred |
| Annual tax drag from location | $40,800 | $9,520 |
| Annual saving from location | — | $31,280 |
| Equity sleeve structure | Pooled index fund | Direct indexing |
| Losses available to offset future gains | None | Yes |
| **Identified in year one** | | **$2,131,280** |

Nothing in the second column required a different investment. It required a different calendar and a different account.

*Assumptions: $15 million eligible gain within the per-issuer cap, a 37% top ordinary rate plus 3.8% net investment income tax, a 20% long-term rate plus 3.8%, 28% on the unexcluded QSBS portion, a 5% bond yield, and a 2% equity dividend yield. State tax is excluded and several states do not conform to federal QSBS treatment, which can change the result substantially. QSBS eligibility depends on satisfying every statutory requirement across the full holding period at both the shareholder and company level. Waiting to sell carries business and market risk that this illustration does not model.*
