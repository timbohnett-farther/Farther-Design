## The Multiple Is Set Years Before the Sale

**For owners whose largest asset is the company, and who would rather choose the terms of their exit than accept them.**

Three quarters of business owners intend to exit within ten years. Seventy-nine percent have nothing written down, and roughly half have done no formal exit planning at all. The owners closest to the exit are the least prepared: 19% of baby boomer owners have started planning against 39% of Gen X owners.

Meanwhile the market is telling anyone who will listen exactly what it rewards. Across 312 closed lower middle market transactions, companies with a documented succession plan sold at a median 6.8 times EBITDA. Matched peers without one sold at 5.9 times. On $4 million of earnings that difference is $3.6 million, and it was decided years before either company received an offer.

## What Buyers Actually Pay For

### The readiness gap

Roughly half of all exits are not chosen. Health, partnership disputes, and unsolicited offers set the timing more often than owners do, which is exactly why the preparation cannot wait for the decision.

### What the market is paying

| Segment | Typical 2026 multiple |
|---|---|
| Main Street, under roughly $2M EBITDA | 2x to 4x |
| Lower middle market, $2M to $10M EBITDA | 4x to 7x |
| Home services | 4x to 6x |
| Manufacturing | 5x to 7x |
| Professional services | 4x to 7x |
| Healthcare services | 5x to 9x |

Lower middle market pricing has expanded meaningfully, with $5 million to $50 million deals moving from about 5.0 times EBITDA in 2022 to roughly 6.5 times more recently. Main Street multiples have been flat over the same period. Quality is being paid for. Size alone is not.

### The succession premium, measured

| Across 312 closed lower middle market transactions | Figure |
|---|---|
| Median multiple with a documented succession plan | 6.8x EBITDA |
| Median multiple for matched peers without one | 5.9x EBITDA |
| Difference | 0.9x |
| Value of that difference on $4 million of EBITDA | $3,600,000 |

The documented plan in that study meant three specific things: a named chief executive successor, a tested second in command for each of the top three revenue-bearing roles, and a development calendar reviewed at board level. Buyers price key-person risk as a deduction from the multiple. Reduce the risk and the deduction shrinks.

Family businesses with active succession plans also closed roughly 23% faster.

### The sequencing point that decides the tax

Transfer planning has to happen before a letter of intent exists. Once a price is negotiated, valuation discounts largely disappear and the window for moving equity at a favorable value closes. The same gift made eighteen months earlier and eighteen months later can differ by millions in exemption consumed.

### What genuine exit preparation gives you

- A higher multiple, earned by removing the key-person deduction buyers apply
- Equity transferred to the next generation before a price makes the transfer expensive
- Qualified small business stock eligibility confirmed rather than assumed
- Liquidity for estate tax that does not depend on the sale closing
- The ability to decline the first offer, which is where most negotiating leverage lives
- A business that runs without you, which is valuable whether you sell it or not

### Why this matters now

The demographic wave is arriving. Millions of owner-operated businesses will transact or close over the next fifteen years, and they will compete for the same buyers. Capital is abundant, which is favorable, and supply is about to be abundant too, which is not.

The differentiator is preparation, and preparation takes years rather than months. An owner who begins three years out gets a process. An owner who begins when the offer arrives gets whatever the offer says.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite owner runs a business generating $4 million of EBITDA. She is 58, intends to exit within five years, has no named successor, and has done no transfer planning.

### The cost of no action

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| EBITDA | $4,000,000 |
| Multiple achieved without a documented succession plan | 5.9x |
| Enterprise value at sale | $23,600,000 |
| Equity moved outside the estate before the sale | $0 |
| Valuation discount available after a letter of intent | Effectively none |
| Negotiating position | Whatever the first credible offer says |

### The benefit of acting now

Three years of deliberate preparation.

| Action | Effect |
|---|---|
| Name a successor and develop the second layer of leadership | Moves the multiple from 5.9x toward 6.8x |
| Gift 25% of non-voting equity to a dynasty trust before any letter of intent | Transfers value at a discounted appraisal |
| Confirm QSBS issuance dates and eligibility batch by batch | May eliminate federal tax on a substantial share of the gain |
| Arrange estate tax liquidity independent of the transaction | Removes forced-sale pressure from the timeline |

### The transfer, sequenced correctly

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Pre-marketing appraised enterprise value | $23,600,000 |
| Pro rata value of a 25% non-voting interest | $5,900,000 |
| After a combined 30% lack of control and marketability discount | $4,130,000 |
| Exemption consumed by the gift | $4,130,000 |
| Value of that same interest at closing | $6,800,000 |
| Value moved outside the estate per dollar of exemption | $1.65 |

### Side by side

| Measure | Unprepared | Prepared over three years |
|---|---|---|
| Enterprise value at sale | $23,600,000 | $27,200,000 |
| Value gained from succession readiness | $0 | $3,600,000 |
| Equity outside the estate at closing | $0 | $6,800,000 |
| Exemption consumed to move it | $0 | $4,130,000 |
| Estate tax avoided on the differential at 40% | $0 | $1,068,000 |
| Time to close | Baseline | Roughly 23% faster |
| **Quantified difference** | | **$4,668,000** |

The business did not change. The earnings were the same in both columns. What changed was that one version could be handed to someone else, and the other could not.
