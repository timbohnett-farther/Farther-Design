## The Position That Built Your Wealth Can Also Undo It

**For founders, executives, and long-term holders sitting on a large low-basis position who want options other than sell or hold.**

Concentration is how most large fortunes are made. It is also how a meaningful share of them are unmade. Analysis of every company in the Russell 3000 since 1980 found that roughly 40% suffered a catastrophic loss, defined as a decline of 70% or more from peak with essentially no recovery.

The uncomfortable detail is who those companies were. More than half were profitable at their peak, and nearly two-thirds carried modest debt. Financial health at the top was not protection.

You do not have to choose between paying a large tax bill today and carrying that risk indefinitely. There is a set of tools between those two answers, and each one trades something specific for something specific.

### The toolkit between sell and hold

| Strategy | What it does | Tax effect | Main constraint |
|---|---|---|---|
| Staged sales | Reduces the position over time | Gain recognized as realized | Tax cost and timing risk |
| Harvested losses from a direct-indexed sleeve | Offsets gains from those sales | Reduces or removes the tax on sales | Requires a taxable portfolio to harvest from |
| Exchange fund | Swaps shares for a diversified partnership interest | Defers the gain | Long lock-up, basis carries over |
| Protective put | Places a floor under the position | Premium cost | Cost, and holding period effects |
| Collar | Floor funded by selling away upside | Generally no immediate gain if properly structured | Constructive sale rules limit how tight it can be |
| Charitable remainder trust | Sells inside a tax-exempt trust and pays you an income stream | Defers, and part is never taxed | Irrevocable, remainder goes to charity |
| Gift to a donor advised fund | Removes the embedded gain entirely | No capital gain, plus a deduction | The shares are gone |
| Borrowing against the position | Provides liquidity without a sale | No gain recognized | Leaves the risk entirely in place |

### The rule every hedge runs into

Under the constructive sale rules, entering an offsetting position that substantially eliminates both your risk of loss and your opportunity for gain is treated as if you sold the stock. This is why a collar has to leave real distance between the floor and the cap. A hedge that removes all the risk also removes the deferral.

### What a structured approach gives you

- A defined floor under a position that historically has a meaningful chance of falling by more than half
- Tax paid deliberately, in the years you choose, offset by losses you harvested on purpose
- Deferral where deferral is appropriate and recognition where the offset makes it cheap
- Charitable capacity funded with the shares carrying the largest embedded gain
- Liquidity without a forced sale at a price you did not choose
- Upside retained on the portion you still want to own

### Why this matters now

Index concentration is at a multi-decade high, which means a household holding both employer stock and index funds may own the same exposure twice. Founders and owners hold the majority of their private and alternative wealth in their own company, and employees with equity hold more still.

None of that requires selling everything. It requires knowing what the position is doing to the rest of the balance sheet, and choosing the tool that trades away the thing you care about least.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite executive holds $12 million of employer stock with a $1.5 million cost basis. The position is 60% of her net worth. She does not want to sell it all, and she does not want to write a $2.5 million check.

### The cost of no action

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Position value | $12,000,000 |
| Cost basis | $1,500,000 |
| Embedded gain | $10,500,000 |
| Tax to sell the entire position at 23.8% | $2,499,000 |
| Share of net worth in one company | 60% |
| Historical probability of an unrecovered 70% decline | About 40% |
| Position value if that occurs | $3,600,000 |
| **Wealth removed from the balance sheet** | **$8,400,000** |

The tax bill she is avoiding is $2.5 million. The exposure she is carrying is $8.4 million.

### The benefit of acting now

A three-year program, using four tools against four different portions.

| Action | Amount addressed | Federal tax |
|---|---|---|
| Staged sales offset by $800,000 of harvested losses | $2,000,000 | $226,100 |
| Contribution to an exchange fund | $3,000,000 | Deferred |
| Gift of the lowest-basis shares to a donor advised fund | $1,000,000 | None, plus a deduction |
| Collar on the remaining position | $6,000,000 | None |
| **Total** | **$12,000,000** | **$226,100** |

### Side by side

| Measure | Sell everything today | Structured over three years |
|---|---|---|
| Position addressed | $12.0M | $12.0M |
| Federal capital gains tax paid | $2,499,000 | $226,100 |
| Charitable deduction generated | $0 | $1,000,000 |
| Downside below the collar floor | None | None |
| Upside retained | None | Retained on $6M to the cap |
| Concentration remaining | Zero | Materially reduced and hedged |
| Liquidity available | Full, immediately | Substantial, staged |

She addressed the entire position for roughly 9% of what an outright sale would have cost in tax, kept meaningful upside on half of it, and put a floor under the rest.

### What the table does not show

The exchange fund carries a lock-up measured in years and her original basis travels with her, so the gain is deferred rather than forgiven. The collar caps her upside on that portion, and if the stock doubles she will not participate fully. The gifted shares are gone permanently, and the deduction is subject to the 30% of income limit for appreciated property and the 0.5% floor that took effect in 2026. Every one of these tools trades something. The work is deciding which trade you are willing to make.

Three points carry the piece. The historical failure rate of individual stocks is far higher than most holders assume, and profitability at the peak did not protect the companies that failed. The choice is not binary, because there is a toolkit between selling everything and doing nothing. And every tool in that kit trades one thing for another, so the right question is which trade fits your situation rather than which strategy is best.
