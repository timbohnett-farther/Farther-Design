## 401(k) and Employer Plan Basics

### 💰 What It Is
A 401(k) is an employer-sponsored retirement plan that allows you to contribute a portion of your paycheck, often pre-tax, with many employers offering a matching contribution as an added incentive to participate.

### 💰 Why It Matters
Employer matching is essentially free money — failing to contribute enough to capture the full match leaves compensation on the table. Contribution limits are also significantly higher than IRAs, making 401(k)s a major wealth-building tool.

### 💰 The Key Insight
Beyond the employee deferral limit, many plans allow additional after-tax contributions up to a much higher combined limit, an underused strategy sometimes called a "mega backdoor Roth" for those with access to it.

## Benefits of Understanding Your Plan
- Employer match capture is effectively an immediate, guaranteed return on your contribution
- Pre-tax contributions reduce your current taxable income
- Automatic payroll deduction makes saving consistent and effortless
- Vesting schedules matter — understanding yours avoids leaving employer contributions behind if you change jobs
- Some plans offer a Roth 401(k) option, allowing after-tax contributions with tax-free growth

## Current Limits (2026)
For 2026, the employee deferral limit for 401(k), 403(b), and most 457 plans is $24,500, with an additional catch-up contribution of $8,000 for those age 50 and older — bringing the total to $32,500 for those eligible.[1] The combined employee and employer contribution limit is $72,000 for 2026.[2]

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Bar chart comparing total retirement savings at 3% vs. 6% contribution rate, highlighting the employer match portion

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Contributing 6% instead of 3% captured an extra $1,500/year in free employer money


Consider an employee earning $100,000 whose employer offers a 50% match on contributions up to 6% of pay.

| Contribution Level | Employee Contribution | Employer Match | Total Retirement Savings |
|---|---|---|---|
| Contributes 3% of pay | $3,000 | $1,500 (half of full match) | $4,500 |
| Contributes 6% of pay (full match) | $6,000 | $3,000 (full match) | $9,000 |

By increasing contributions from 3% to 6%, this employee captures an additional $1,500 in "free" employer money annually.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your plan administrator or financial advisor for guidance specific to your plan.
