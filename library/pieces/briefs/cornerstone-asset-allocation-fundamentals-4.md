## Asset Allocation Fundamentals

### 🧭 What It Is
Asset allocation is how you divide your investment portfolio among different categories — stocks, bonds, cash, and sometimes alternatives — based on your goals, timeline, and risk tolerance.

### 🧭 Why It Matters
Research consistently shows that the mix of asset classes you hold has a far greater impact on your long-term returns and volatility than which specific stocks or funds you pick within each category.

### 🧭 The Key Insight
Your ideal allocation isn't static — it should evolve as your goals, timeline, income needs, and risk tolerance change over your lifetime.

## Benefits of a Thoughtful Asset Allocation
- Aligns your portfolio's risk level with your actual ability and willingness to handle volatility
- Provides a disciplined framework, reducing reactive, emotion-driven decisions
- Balances growth needs against near-term cash flow or liquidity requirements
- Creates a foundation that guides rebalancing and future contributions
- Adapts naturally as life stages change (career growth, retirement, legacy planning)

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Two donut/pie charts side-by-side: Household A allocation vs. Household B allocation

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Asset mix drives more of your outcome than any single stock pick


Consider two households with different time horizons and needs.

| Household | Time Horizon | Illustrative Allocation |
|---|---|---|
| Household A — 30 years from retirement | Long | 85% stocks / 10% bonds / 5% cash |
| Household B — 3 years from retirement | Short | 40% stocks / 50% bonds / 10% cash |

Household A can afford more growth-oriented risk because time allows recovery from downturns. Household B needs more stability since a major drawdown close to retirement is harder to recover from.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your financial advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
