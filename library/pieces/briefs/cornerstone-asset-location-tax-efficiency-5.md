## Asset Location and Tax Efficiency

### 🗂️ What It Is
Asset location is the strategy of placing specific types of investments in the account type where they're taxed most favorably — for example, holding tax-inefficient investments (like bonds generating ordinary income) in tax-deferred accounts, while holding tax-efficient investments (like broad stock index funds) in taxable accounts.

### 🗂️ Why It Matters
The same investment portfolio can generate meaningfully different after-tax returns depending purely on which account type holds which asset — without changing your actual investment strategy at all.

### 🗂️ The Key Insight
Asset location is about "where," while asset allocation is about "what." Both work together, but asset location is often overlooked because it doesn't change your overall investment mix, only its tax efficiency.

## Benefits of Thoughtful Asset Location
- Reduces the total tax drag on your overall portfolio without changing your allocation
- Makes better use of tax-deferred and tax-free account "space"
- Can meaningfully increase after-tax returns over long time horizons
- Complements other tax strategies like tax-loss harvesting and Roth conversions
- Particularly valuable for households with a mix of taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Two stacked bar charts showing identical allocation split across account types: 'No Strategy' vs. 'Asset Location Applied'

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Same allocation, different tax bill — just by choosing which account holds what


Consider a household with $500,000 split evenly between a taxable brokerage account and a Traditional IRA, wanting a 50% stocks / 50% bonds overall allocation.

| Approach | Structure | Tax Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| No asset location strategy | Both accounts hold an identical 50/50 stock-bond mix | Bond interest in the taxable account is taxed annually at ordinary income rates |
| Asset location applied | Bonds held in the IRA; stocks held in the taxable account | Bond interest grows tax-deferred; stock gains in the taxable account may qualify for lower long-term capital gains rates |

The overall portfolio allocation is identical in both cases — only the tax efficiency differs.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your tax advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
