## Beneficiary Designations and Why They Matter

### 👪 What They Are
Beneficiary designations are forms attached to accounts like retirement plans, IRAs, life insurance policies, and some brokerage accounts that name who receives the assets when you pass away.

### 👪 Why It Matters
For accounts with a valid beneficiary designation, that designation legally controls who inherits the asset — it overrides instructions in your will. An outdated beneficiary form can unintentionally send assets to an ex-spouse, an estranged relative, or exclude a new spouse or child entirely.

### 👪 The Key Insight
Beneficiary designations should be reviewed after every major life event: marriage, divorce, birth of a child, or death of a previously named beneficiary.

## Benefits of Keeping Designations Current
- Ensures assets go to the people you actually intend, regardless of what your will says
- Allows many assets to bypass probate entirely, transferring quickly and privately
- Prevents outdated designations from unintentionally benefiting an ex-spouse
- Supports coordinated planning between retirement accounts, insurance, and your overall estate plan
- Allows for contingent beneficiaries in case a primary beneficiary predeceases you

## A Note on State Variation
Some states have specific rules automatically revoking an ex-spouse's beneficiary status upon divorce for certain account types, while others do not — meaning an ex-spouse could still inherit unless the designation is actively updated. Because these rules vary by state and account type (and federal law can override state law for certain retirement plans), it's important to actively update designations rather than rely on assumptions about automatic revocation.

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** An 8-year-old form could send $400,000 to the wrong person


Consider a man named James who divorced eight years ago but never updated the beneficiary on his 401(k), which has grown to $400,000.

| Scenario | Outcome |
|---|---|
| Beneficiary form never updated | Ex-spouse may still legally receive the full $400,000, regardless of the will or current relationship |
| Beneficiary form updated after divorce | Current spouse or children receive the funds as intended |

A five-minute form update can prevent a life-changing, unintended outcome for James's current family.
