## Brokerage vs. Advisory Accounts

### ⚖️ What They Are
A brokerage account is typically used for self-directed or transaction-based investing, where you (or a broker) execute individual trades, often paying per-transaction or embedded commissions. An advisory account involves an ongoing relationship with an advisor who manages your investments for an asset-based fee, typically under a fiduciary standard.

### ⚖️ Why It Matters
The standard of care differs significantly. Advisors acting in a fiduciary capacity are legally required to act in your best interest at all times, while brokers historically have only needed to recommend "suitable" investments — a materially lower bar.

### ⚖️ The Key Insight
Understanding which type of account and relationship you have clarifies exactly what level of ongoing advice, monitoring, and legal obligation you're entitled to.

## Benefits of Understanding the Distinction
- Clarifies what standard of care legally applies to the advice you're receiving
- Helps you evaluate whether fees are transaction-based or ongoing advisory fees
- Supports better questions when interviewing or evaluating a financial professional
- Reduces the risk of assuming ongoing monitoring is included when it may not be
- Encourages verifying credentials and regulatory status of anyone managing your money

| Household B | Advisory account, fiduciary fee-based relationship | Ongoing portfolio management and planning, with the advisor legally required to act in their best interest |
