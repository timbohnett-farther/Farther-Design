## Capital Gains vs. Ordinary Income

### ⏱️ What They Are
Ordinary income includes wages, interest, and short-term investment gains, taxed at your regular income tax rate. Long-term capital gains — profit from assets held longer than one year — are taxed at preferential rates, generally lower than ordinary income tax rates.

### ⏱️ Why It Matters
Selling an investment just one day before vs. after the one-year mark can mean the difference between paying your top ordinary income rate versus a substantially lower long-term capital gains rate.

### ⏱️ The Key Insight
This distinction rewards patience — the tax code is structured to encourage longer holding periods rather than frequent, short-term trading.

## Benefits of Understanding This Distinction
- Encourages a longer-term investing approach, which is generally aligned with better outcomes anyway
- Helps you plan sale timing around the one-year holding period threshold
- Clarifies why dividend type (qualified vs. non-qualified) also affects your tax rate
- Supports better after-tax return comparisons between different investment strategies
- Informs decisions around inherited assets, which often receive a "step-up" in cost basis

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Bar chart comparing tax owed: Short-Term (32%) vs. Long-Term (15%) on the same $50,000 gain

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Waiting to cross the 1-year mark saved $8,500 in this example


Consider an investor in the 32% ordinary income tax bracket who has a $50,000 gain on a stock position.

| Holding Period | Tax Treatment | Illustrative Tax Owed |
|---|---|---|
| Held less than 1 year (short-term) | Taxed as ordinary income at 32% | $16,000 |
| Held more than 1 year (long-term) | Taxed at long-term capital gains rate (commonly 15% for this income level) | $7,500 |

Simply waiting to cross the one-year holding threshold could save this investor $8,500 in this illustrative example.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your tax advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
