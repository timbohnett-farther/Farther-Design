## Dollar-Cost Averaging

### 🔁 What It Is
Dollar-cost averaging (DCA) means investing a fixed amount of money at regular intervals — such as monthly — regardless of whether the market is up or down, rather than trying to invest a lump sum at the "perfect" moment.

### 🔁 Why It Matters
Because DCA buys more shares when prices are low and fewer shares when prices are high, it naturally smooths out your average purchase price over time and removes the pressure of guessing short-term market direction.

### 🔁 The Key Insight
DCA is less about maximizing returns in any single scenario and more about building a sustainable, low-stress habit that keeps you investing consistently through both up and down markets.

## Benefits of Dollar-Cost Averaging
- Removes the emotional burden of trying to "pick the right moment" to invest
- Naturally lowers average cost per share during volatile periods
- Builds a consistent savings habit, especially useful for retirement plan contributions
- Reduces the risk of investing a large lump sum right before a downturn
- Works automatically through payroll deductions or scheduled transfers

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Bar chart of monthly share price vs. shares purchased over 6 months

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Average cost per share: $49.30 vs. $50.00 market average


Imagine an investor contributing $500 per month into a fund over a volatile six-month period where the price fluctuates between $40 and $60 per share.

| Month | Price per Share | Shares Purchased with $500 |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | $50 | 10.0 |
| 2 | $40 | 12.5 |
| 3 | $45 | 11.1 |
| 4 | $60 | 8.3 |
| 5 | $55 | 9.1 |
| 6 | $50 | 10.0 |

Average share price paid: approximately $49.30, versus the simple average market price of $50 across the period — a small but real benefit from buying more shares when prices dipped.
