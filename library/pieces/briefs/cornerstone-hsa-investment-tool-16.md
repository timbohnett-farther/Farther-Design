## Health Savings Accounts (HSAs) as an Investment Tool

### 🏥 What It Is
A Health Savings Account (HSA) is available to those enrolled in a qualifying high-deductible health plan (HDHP), allowing tax-deductible contributions, tax-free growth, and tax-free withdrawals when used for qualified medical expenses.

### 🏥 Why It Matters
No other account offers this specific "triple tax advantage" — not Traditional IRAs, not Roth IRAs. Many HSA providers also allow you to invest the balance, similar to a retirement account, rather than leaving it in cash.

### 🏥 The Key Insight
HSAs have no "use it or lose it" rule — unlike Flexible Spending Accounts, unused HSA balances roll over indefinitely and can be invested for long-term growth, making them a powerful supplemental retirement savings vehicle when used strategically.

## Benefits of Using an HSA Strategically
- Contributions are tax-deductible, reducing your current taxable income
- Growth within the account is completely tax-free
- Withdrawals for qualified medical expenses are also tax-free — a triple benefit no other account offers
- After age 65, funds can be withdrawn for any purpose (taxed as ordinary income if not medical, similar to a Traditional IRA)
- Unused balances roll over year to year and can be invested for long-term growth

## Current Limits (2026)
For 2026, HSA contribution limits are $4,400 for self-only coverage and $8,750 for family coverage, with an additional $1,000 catch-up contribution available for those age 55 and older.[1]

Consider a household maximizing family HSA contributions of $8,750 per year for 20 years, investing the balance rather than spending it on current medical expenses (paid out of pocket instead), assuming a 7% average annual return.

| Approach | Strategy | Illustrative Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Spend HSA funds as medical costs arise | No investment growth | Balance stays relatively flat, used up as expenses occur |
| Pay medical costs out-of-pocket; invest the HSA | Long-term tax-free growth | Meaningfully larger balance after 20 years, available tax-free for medical costs or taxed as ordinary income after 65 for other uses |

Treating the HSA as a long-term investment account, rather than a pass-through for current expenses, can significantly increase its long-term value.
