## The Power of Compounding

### ⏳ What It Is
Compounding is the process by which your investment returns generate their own returns. Instead of earning interest or growth only on your original contribution, you earn on the growing balance — original money plus all prior gains.

### ⏳ Why It Matters
The earlier you start, the more time compounding has to work. A dollar invested in your 20s has decades longer to grow than a dollar invested in your 40s, even if the contribution amount is identical.

### ⏳ The Key Insight
Compounding rewards patience and consistency far more than it rewards large, one-time contributions made later in life. Time is the single most valuable — and irreplaceable — ingredient.

## Benefits of Understanding Compounding
- Motivates starting to invest as early as possible, even in small amounts
- Reinforces the value of reinvesting dividends and capital gains rather than spending them
- Explains why delaying investing by even a few years can have an outsized cost
- Encourages a long-term mindset over chasing short-term gains
- Highlights why fees matter — even small annual costs compound against you over decades

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Line chart showing two growth curves over 40 years: 'Started at 25' vs. 'Started at 45', same annual contribution

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Starting 20 years earlier can more than double your ending balance


Consider two savers who each eventually invest $200,000 total, assuming a 7% average annual return.

| Saver | Contribution Pattern | Illustrative Value at Age 65 |
|---|---|---|
| Saver A | Invests $10,000/year from age 25–45 (20 years), then stops | Higher ending balance due to more compounding time |
| Saver B | Invests $10,000/year from age 45–65 (20 years) | Meaningfully lower ending balance despite investing the same total |

Saver A's money simply had more years to compound — even though both savers contributed the same total amount.
