## Rebalancing Your Portfolio

### ⚖️ What It Is
Rebalancing means periodically buying or selling investments to bring your portfolio back to its target allocation after market movements have shifted it out of balance.

### ⚖️ Why It Matters
Over time, strong-performing assets (like stocks during a bull market) can grow to represent a larger share of your portfolio than intended, quietly increasing your risk beyond your comfort level without you realizing it.

### ⚖️ The Key Insight
Rebalancing enforces a disciplined "sell high, buy low" behavior — trimming what's grown large and adding to what's lagged — which is difficult to do based on instinct alone.

## Benefits of Regular Rebalancing
- Keeps your actual risk level aligned with your intended risk tolerance
- Removes emotion from buy/sell decisions by following a rules-based process
- Can create opportunities for tax-loss harvesting when done thoughtfully
- Prevents any single holding or asset class from dominating your portfolio
- Provides a natural checkpoint to reassess your goals and circumstances

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Stacked bar chart showing allocation drift: target vs. drifted vs. rebalanced

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Unchecked drift pushed this portfolio from 70/30 to 85/15


Consider a portfolio that started at a 70% stocks / 30% bonds target allocation. After a strong multi-year stock rally, it drifts to 85% stocks / 15% bonds.

| Scenario | Allocation | Risk Level |
|---|---|---|
| Original target | 70% stocks / 30% bonds | Matches household's risk tolerance |
| Drifted (unrebalanced) | 85% stocks / 15% bonds | Meaningfully higher risk than intended |
| After rebalancing | Back to 70% stocks / 30% bonds | Realigned with household's actual comfort level |

Without rebalancing, this household could be far more exposed to a market downturn than they ever intended.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your financial advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
