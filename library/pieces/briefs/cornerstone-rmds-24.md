## Required Minimum Distributions (RMDs)

### ⏰ What They Are
RMDs are minimum amounts the IRS requires you to withdraw annually from most tax-deferred retirement accounts (Traditional IRAs, 401(k)s, 403(b)s) starting at a certain age, ensuring taxes are eventually paid on those funds.

### ⏰ Why It Matters
Missing an RMD deadline carries a steep penalty — an excise tax of 25% of the amount that should have been withdrawn (potentially reduced to 10% if corrected promptly).[1] Understanding your specific required beginning date is essential to avoid this.

### ⏰ The Key Insight
Your RMD starting age depends on your birth year: age 73 for those born 1951–1959, and age 75 for those born in 1960 or later, under SECURE Act 2.0 rules.[2] Roth IRAs are not subject to RMDs during the original owner's lifetime.

## Benefits of Planning Ahead for RMDs
- Avoids costly penalties from missed or incorrect distributions
- Allows time to plan strategies (like Roth conversions) that can reduce future RMD amounts
- Helps manage the tax impact of RMDs alongside other income sources
- Supports charitable giving strategies, such as Qualified Charitable Distributions (QCDs), which can satisfy RMDs tax-free
- Provides clarity for multi-account households on aggregation rules

## Key Rules to Know
- Your first RMD can be delayed until April 1 of the year after you reach your RMD age, but this creates two RMDs in that same calendar year (increasing that year's taxable income)[1]
- IRA RMDs can be aggregated and taken from any combination of your IRAs, but 401(k) RMDs generally must be taken separately from each employer plan[1]
- RMD amount is calculated by dividing your prior year-end account balance by an IRS life expectancy factor specific to your age[1]

Consider someone turning 73 in 2026 with a $1,000,000 Traditional IRA balance as of December 31, 2025.

| Step | Calculation | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Prior year-end balance | — | $1,000,000 |
| IRS distribution period at age 73 | 26.5 | — |
| Required RMD | $1,000,000 ÷ 26.5 | Approximately $37,700 |

Missing this distribution could trigger a penalty as high as $9,425 (25% of the missed amount) before any corrective reduction.
