## Roth Conversions

### 🔄 What It Is
A Roth conversion involves moving money from a Traditional IRA or 401(k) into a Roth account. You pay ordinary income tax on the converted amount in the year of conversion, but the funds then grow and can be withdrawn tax-free in retirement.

### 🔄 Why It Matters
Roth conversions can be a powerful way to "prepay" taxes at a known, potentially lower rate today rather than risk paying higher rates later — particularly valuable during lower-income years, such as early retirement before Social Security or required distributions begin.

### 🔄 The Key Insight
Conversions work best when done thoughtfully and often gradually over several years ("bracket filling") to avoid pushing yourself into a much higher tax bracket in a single year.

## Benefits of Strategic Roth Conversions
- Locks in today's tax rate on converted funds, which can be valuable if rates rise later
- Reduces future required minimum distributions, since Roth IRAs have none during the owner's lifetime
- Can reduce the tax burden passed on to heirs, who often prefer inheriting Roth assets
- Creates more flexibility to manage taxable income in retirement
- Particularly useful during temporary low-income years (e.g., between retirement and Social Security)

## Important Considerations
- Conversions are permanent and cannot be reversed once completed
- The converted amount is added to your taxable income for that year, potentially affecting Medicare premiums (IRMAA) or other income-based thresholds
- A five-year holding period generally applies to each conversion before earnings can be withdrawn tax- and penalty-free

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Bar chart showing 5 years of partial Roth conversions filling up a tax bracket, versus one large conversion spiking into a higher bracket

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Spreading a $200,000 conversion over 5 years can avoid bracket spikes


Consider a retiree who has stopped working but hasn't yet started Social Security, temporarily sitting in a lower tax bracket.

| Scenario | Approach | Illustrative Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| No conversion | Leaves $200,000 in Traditional IRA | Future RMDs and withdrawals taxed at whatever bracket applies later, potentially higher |
| Strategic partial conversion | Converts $40,000/year over 5 years during low-income years | Fills up lower tax brackets at today's known rates, reducing future RMD-driven tax spikes |

By spreading the conversion over several years, the retiree avoids pushing the entire $200,000 into higher brackets in a single year.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your tax advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
