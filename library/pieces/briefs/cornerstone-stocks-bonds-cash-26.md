## Understanding Stocks, Bonds, and Cash

### 🧱 What They Are
Stocks represent partial ownership in a company and offer growth potential with higher volatility. Bonds are loans you make to a government or company in exchange for interest payments, generally offering more stability and income. Cash and cash equivalents (savings accounts, money market funds, T-bills) offer safety and liquidity but minimal growth.

### 🧱 Why It Matters
Each asset class behaves differently under different economic conditions. Understanding their distinct roles helps you build a portfolio that matches your goals, timeline, and comfort with risk — rather than one that's simply reactive to headlines.

### 🧱 The Key Insight
No single asset class is "best." The right portfolio blends all three based on your specific time horizon and need for growth, income, or stability at any given point in life.

## Benefits of Understanding Each Asset Class
- Clarifies why your portfolio holds a mix, rather than just "the best-performing" investment
- Helps you understand why bonds may lose value too, especially when interest rates rise
- Explains the tradeoff between cash's safety and its inability to keep pace with inflation
- Supports more informed conversations with your advisor about risk and expected returns
- Reduces panic-driven decisions during market swings, since you understand each asset's role

Consider a simplified illustration of how $10,000 might behave differently across asset classes during a single volatile year.

| Asset Class | Typical Role | Illustrative One-Year Range |
|---|---|---|
| Stocks | Long-term growth | -20% to +25% |
| Bonds | Income and stability | -5% to +8% |
| Cash | Safety and liquidity | +2% to +5% (interest), no loss of principal |

A portfolio blending all three can smooth out the extremes of any single asset class, rather than exposing a household to the full swing of stocks alone.
