## Tax-Loss Harvesting Basics

### ✂️ What It Is
Tax-loss harvesting involves selling an investment that has declined in value to realize a capital loss, which can offset capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio and potentially reduce your taxable income.

### ✂️ Why It Matters
Losses aren't just a downside of investing — when managed strategically, they can directly reduce your tax bill, effectively turning a paper loss into a real financial benefit.

### ✂️ The Key Insight
After offsetting any capital gains, up to $3,000 of excess losses can be deducted against ordinary income each year, with any remaining losses carried forward indefinitely to future tax years.

## Benefits of Tax-Loss Harvesting
- Offsets realized capital gains elsewhere in your portfolio, reducing current tax liability
- Allows up to $3,000 per year of excess losses to offset ordinary income
- Unused losses carry forward indefinitely to future tax years
- Can be done while maintaining your overall market exposure by reinvesting in a similar (not identical) investment
- Most valuable in taxable accounts, since retirement accounts don't generate taxable gains or losses annually

## Important Rule to Know
The IRS "wash sale rule" disallows the loss if you buy the same or a "substantially identical" security within 30 days before or after the sale — this applies across all your accounts, including IRAs, so coordination matters.

## Seeing It in Action

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Simple before/after bar: Taxable Gain Before Harvesting ($15,000) vs. After Harvesting ($7,000)

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** Harvesting one loss cut this investor's taxable gain in half


Consider an investor who has a $15,000 realized long-term capital gain from selling one stock, and a separate holding down $8,000 from its purchase price.

| Action | Outcome |
|---|---|
| No tax-loss harvesting | Pays capital gains tax on the full $15,000 gain |
| Harvests the $8,000 loss | Net taxable gain reduced to $7,000, lowering the tax bill accordingly |

By realizing the loss, this investor reduces their taxable gain by more than half, without necessarily changing their overall market exposure if reinvested appropriately.
