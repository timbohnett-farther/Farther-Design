## Time in the Market vs. Timing the Market

### 📈 What It Means
"Timing the market" means trying to predict short-term ups and downs — buying right before a rally and selling right before a drop. "Time in the market" means staying invested through cycles and letting your portfolio compound over years and decades.

### 📈 Why It Matters
Missing just a handful of the market's best days can meaningfully reduce long-term returns, and those best days often cluster right after the worst days — precisely when nervous investors are most likely to sell. Trying to dodge volatility by jumping in and out usually means missing the recovery.

### 📈 What to Watch Out For
No one — not even professional traders — can reliably and consistently predict short-term market moves. Studies consistently show that most attempts to time the market underperform a simple buy-and-hold approach over the long run.

## Benefits of Staying Invested
- Captures the market's long-term historical upward trend rather than betting on short-term noise
- Avoids the "double mistake" of selling low and buying back in high
- Reduces stress and decision fatigue tied to constantly watching headlines
- Keeps compounding uninterrupted, which is the single biggest driver of long-term wealth
- Aligns behavior with a written plan instead of emotion
