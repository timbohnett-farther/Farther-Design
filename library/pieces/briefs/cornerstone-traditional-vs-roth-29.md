## Traditional vs. Roth Accounts

### 🔀 What They Are
Traditional retirement accounts (like a Traditional IRA or 401(k)) are typically funded with pre-tax dollars, reducing your taxable income today, with withdrawals taxed as ordinary income in retirement. Roth accounts are funded with after-tax dollars — no upfront deduction — but qualified withdrawals in retirement are entirely tax-free.

### 🔀 Why It Matters
Choosing between the two is essentially a bet on whether your tax rate will be higher or lower in retirement compared to today. Many households benefit from holding both types, creating flexibility to manage taxable income in retirement.

### 🔀 The Key Insight
There's no universally "better" choice — the right answer depends on your current tax bracket, expected future tax bracket, and how many years your money has to grow.

## Benefits of Understanding Both Account Types
- Provides tax diversification, giving you flexibility to manage taxable income in retirement
- Roth accounts offer tax-free growth, which can be especially valuable over long time horizons
- Traditional accounts reduce your current taxable income, which can be valuable during high-earning years
- Roth IRAs have no required minimum distributions during the original owner's lifetime
- Supports more strategic decisions around Roth conversions later in life

## Current Contribution Limits (2026)
For 2026, the IRA contribution limit is $7,500 (with an additional catch-up contribution for those 50 and older), and the 401(k) employee deferral limit is $24,500, with a $8,000 catch-up contribution for those 50 and older.[1] These limits apply whether you contribute to Traditional or Roth versions of these accounts, though Roth IRA eligibility phases out at higher income levels.

> 📊 **CHART PLACEHOLDER:** Side-by-side bar chart: Traditional IRA ending balance (pre-tax) vs. Roth IRA ending balance (after-tax, tax-free)

> 💡 **KEY STAT CALLOUT:** 2026 IRA limit: $7,500 ($8,600 if 50+)


Consider two hypothetical individuals in the 24% tax bracket today, each contributing $7,500 annually for 20 years and growing at an assumed 7% average return.

| Account Type | Tax Treatment | Illustrative Outcome |
|---|---|---|
| Traditional IRA | Tax deduction now; withdrawals taxed later | Larger pre-tax balance, but a tax bill is owed on every withdrawal in retirement |
| Roth IRA | No deduction now; withdrawals tax-free later | Smaller current tax benefit, but the entire retirement balance is available tax-free |

If tax rates in retirement are similar to today, the after-tax outcomes are often comparable — but if rates rise, the Roth account becomes more valuable, and vice versa.

This is a hypothetical example for illustrative purposes only and does not represent an actual client. Consult your tax advisor for guidance specific to your situation.
