## Trusts vs. Individual Ownership Basics

### 🏛️ What They Are
Individual ownership means assets are titled directly in your name and typically pass through probate at death according to your will (or state law if there's no will). A revocable living trust is a legal structure you create during your lifetime that holds assets on your behalf, allowing them to pass to beneficiaries without probate.

### 🏛️ Why It Matters
Probate can be time-consuming, public, and costly, and it varies significantly by state. Assets held in a properly funded trust generally avoid probate entirely, transferring privately and often more quickly.

### 🏛️ The Key Insight
A revocable trust doesn't reduce estate taxes or protect assets from your own creditors during your lifetime — its primary benefits are probate avoidance, privacy, and control over how and when beneficiaries receive assets.

## Benefits of Trust-Based Ownership (Where Appropriate)
- Avoids probate, which can take months and become part of the public record
- Allows for detailed instructions on how and when beneficiaries receive assets (e.g., staggered distributions for young heirs)
- Provides continuity of management if you become incapacitated, without court involvement
- Can simplify management of property held in multiple states
- Maintains privacy, since trust terms generally aren't part of the public record like a will

## A Note on State Variation
Probate timelines, costs, and complexity vary considerably by state — some states offer simplified or "small estate" probate processes for smaller estates, while others have more involved, costly processes regardless of estate size. This variation is one of the key reasons a trust may make more sense in some states than others, and it's worth discussing your specific state's probate process with your estate attorney.

Consider a couple with a vacation home in a second state and a primary residence in their home state.

| Structure | Outcome at Death |
|---|---|
| Individual ownership, no trust | Potential probate proceedings in two separate states, extending time and cost |
| Assets held in a properly funded revocable trust | Both properties can pass to heirs without probate in either state |

The trust structure can meaningfully simplify what would otherwise be a multi-state probate process.
