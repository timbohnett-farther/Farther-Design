# Intelligent Wealth Series: Family Limited Partnerships and Entity Planning

## Give Away the Economics, Keep the Control

**For families holding real estate or operating businesses who want to transfer value without transferring the steering wheel.**

A family partnership does something no other structure does quite as cleanly. It separates control from economics, which lets the senior generation hand down the value of an asset while continuing to run it.

It also produces a valuation discount, because an interest that carries no control and cannot be sold is worth less than its share of the underlying assets. That discount is legitimate, well established, and aggressively examined. In 2024 the Tax Court disallowed discounts entirely on a partnership holding roughly $10.8 million of reported value, pulling the full undiscounted amount back into the estate. The technique works. The execution is what decides it.

## How the Structure Works

### Two roles, two very different rights

| Role | Typically held by | What it carries |
|---|---|---|
| General partner or manager | Senior generation, often through a separate entity | Control of operations, investments, and distributions |
| Limited or non-managing interest | Children, grandchildren, or trusts for them | Economic value, no control, restricted transfer rights |

A senior family member contributes real estate, securities, or business interests to the partnership, receives both classes of interest in return, and then gifts the non-managing interests over time. Control stays where it was.

### Where the discount comes from

| Discount | Typical range | What it reflects |
|---|---|---|
| Lack of control | 15% to 30% | A minority holder cannot force distributions, sales, or liquidation |
| Lack of marketability | 10% to 35% | No willing buyer exists for a restricted family partnership interest |

### The arithmetic most people get wrong

Discounts are applied in sequence, not added together.

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Pro rata share of underlying assets | $1,000,000 |
| After a 20% lack of control discount | $800,000 |
| After a 30% lack of marketability discount | $560,000 |
| **Combined effective discount** | **44%, not 50%** |

### What causes discounts to be disallowed

Section 2036 lets the IRS pull assets back into the estate at full value when the person who transferred them retained possession, enjoyment, income rights, or the right to decide who benefits. Courts have looked at the same handful of facts for twenty years.

| Red flag | What it looks like in practice |
|---|---|
| Formation late in life | Transfers made within months of death |
| No purpose other than tax | Nothing the partnership does that could not be done without it |
| Commingled funds | Personal expenses paid from partnership accounts |
| Distributions on demand | Payments timed to the donor's personal needs |
| Too little retained outside | Donor cannot fund bequests, expenses, or the estate tax bill |
| Ignored formalities | No meetings, no records, no separate books |

In the 2024 decision, the estate had retained some assets outside the partnership, but not enough to cover roughly $1.45 million of cash bequests, a specific stock gift, and the anticipated estate tax. The partnership had to fund them. That fact pattern did the damage.

### What it costs to do properly

| Item | Typical range |
|---|---|
| Formation, drafting, and initial appraisal | $25,000 to $75,000 |
| Annual valuation, tax filings, and administration | Recurring |

A qualified appraisal must generally be prepared no earlier than 60 days before the transfer and no later than the return due date.

### What a well-run family entity gives you

- Transfer of economic value at a discount, stretching every dollar of exemption
- Retained control of operations, investments, and distribution timing
- Consolidated management of assets that would otherwise fragment across heirs
- Charging order protection in many states, limiting what a beneficiary's creditor can reach
- A framework for bringing the next generation into ownership gradually
- Restrictions that keep interests inside the family

### Why this matters now

With the exemption permanent at $30 million for a couple, fewer families face a federal estate tax deadline. That makes the decision more interesting, not less. A discount stretches exemption, but a gifted interest carries over your basis rather than receiving an adjustment at death. For a family comfortably under the exemption, holding for that basis adjustment often produces a better result than discounting. For a family well above it, the discount plus decades of growth outside the estate is difficult to beat. The right answer depends on which side of that line you are on, and on how much growth is still ahead.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite family holds $20 million of rental real estate and closely held business interests. Their estate already exceeds the couple exemption. They want to move value to a dynasty trust for their children without giving up management.

### The cost of no action

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Assets held directly today | $20,000,000 |
| Value at year 20, growing at 6% | $64,140,000 |
| Amount inside the taxable estate | All of it |
| Control | Full, and never transferred |

### The benefit of acting now

They contribute the assets to a family LLC, retain the managing interest, and gift 40% of the non-managing interests to a dynasty trust.

| Step | Value |
|---|---|
| Pro rata value of the 40% interest | $8,000,000 |
| After a 20% lack of control discount | $6,400,000 |
| After a 30% lack of marketability discount | $4,480,000 |
| Combined effective discount | 44% |
| Exemption consumed by the gift | $4,480,000 |
| Exemption preserved versus an undiscounted gift | $3,520,000 |

### Side by side

| Measure | Hold directly | Family LLC with discounted gift |
|---|---|---|
| Value of the 40% interest at year 20 | $25.66M | $25.66M |
| Location of that value | Inside the estate | Outside the estate |
| Exemption consumed today | $0 | $4.48M |
| Growth sheltered from estate tax | $0 | $17.66M |
| Estate tax on that growth at 40% | $7.06M | $0 |
| Additional exemption preserved by the discount | $0 | $3.52M |
| Estate tax sheltered by that preserved exemption | $0 | $1.41M |
| Operational control | Full | Full, retained as manager |
| **Federal estate tax difference** | | **$8.47M** |

### What the table does not show

The gifted interests carry over the family's original basis rather than receiving an adjustment at death. If those assets are eventually sold, the capital gains tax on that carried-over basis offsets part of the estate tax saved. For this family, sitting well above the exemption with two decades of growth ahead, the trade favors the gift. For a family near or below $30 million, it often does not.

That is the actual decision, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.

*Assumptions: illustrative 20% lack of control and 30% lack of marketability discounts supported by a qualified appraisal, 6% annual growth, a 40% federal estate tax rate, a twenty-year horizon, and an estate already exceeding the combined exemption. Formation and ongoing administration costs are excluded and typically run $25,000 to $75,000 initially with recurring annual expense. Discounts are not guaranteed and depend entirely on the appraisal, the agreement terms, the entity's purpose, and how it is operated. No state estate or income tax is modeled.*

## Summary

| What we covered | The number |
|---|---|
| Typical lack of control discount | 15% to 30% |
| Typical lack of marketability discount | 10% to 35% |
| Combined effect of a 20% and a 30% discount | 44% |
| Typical formation and appraisal cost | $25,000 to $75,000 |
| Illustration: exemption preserved by the discount | $3.52 million |
| Illustration: federal estate tax difference | $8.47 million |

Three points carry the piece. A family entity separates control from economics, which is the only clean way to transfer value while still running the asset. Discounts are multiplicative rather than additive, so the combined figure is always smaller than the two numbers suggest. And the structure survives or fails on operation rather than on drafting, because every case the IRS has won turned on how the partnership actually behaved.
