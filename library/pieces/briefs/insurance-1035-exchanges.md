# Intelligent Wealth Series: 1035 Exchanges — Moving Policies Without a Taxable Event

## Tax-Free Does Not Mean Friction-Free

**For households reviewing an existing annuity or life insurance policy and wondering whether a tax-free exchange into a better contract is worth pursuing.**

Section 1035 can allow certain life insurance, endowment, and annuity contracts to be exchanged for a different contract without current income tax recognition if the rules are met. That is powerful, but it does not guarantee that the exchange is economically wise. Fees, surrender charges, new contractual periods, underwriting, and product economics still matter.

## What a 1035 Exchange Can and Cannot Fix

### What it may improve

A replacement contract may offer lower costs, stronger riders, a better crediting structure, improved guarantees, or more suitable long-term design. For some households, those improvements justify the work.

### What it does not erase

A tax-free exchange does not cancel surrender charges, undo a poor funding decision, or guarantee that the new policy is better. Replacement analysis is still required.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative replacement review

| Measure | Existing annuity | Proposed annuity |
|---|---|---|
| Account value | $400,000 | $400,000 transferred |
| Surrender charge | 4% ($16,000) | New surrender schedule begins |
| Rider fee | 1.40% | 0.95% |
| Crediting structure | Cap 4.5% | Participation 45%, no fee rider |
| Tax due on exchange | $0 if properly structured | $0 if properly structured |

### The key planning question

The right test is whether the economic improvement outweighs the cost of leaving the current contract and entering the new one. Tax deferral alone is not enough reason to switch.
