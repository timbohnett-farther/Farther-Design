# Intelligent Wealth Series: Annuities 101 — What They Are and What They Are Not

## A Contract, Not a Shortcut

**For households evaluating whether an annuity belongs in a retirement income plan and what kind of risk it is actually designed to solve.**

Annuities are often sold with language that sounds simpler than the contract really is. At their core, annuities are insurance contracts designed to address one or more of three concerns: protected principal, tax deferral, or predictable income. The planning decision is whether the specific contract solves a real problem at a reasonable cost.

## The Main Categories

### Immediate and deferred

Immediate annuities exchange a lump sum for income that starts right away. Deferred annuities postpone future income and may also include an accumulation phase.

### Fixed, indexed, and variable

A fixed annuity credits interest subject to the contract terms. An indexed annuity links growth in part to a market index subject to caps, spreads, or participation rates. A variable annuity invests in subaccounts and places more investment risk on the owner, often with higher fee complexity.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative comparison

| Contract type | Primary objective | Liquidity | Typical complexity |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fixed annuity | Principal stability / tax deferral | Limited | Lower |
| Indexed annuity | Buffered market-linked growth | Limited | Moderate |
| Variable annuity | Tax deferral with investment menu | Limited | Higher |
| Immediate income annuity | Lifetime or period-certain income | Low after annuitization | Moderate |

### The real decision

An annuity is not automatically good or bad. It may be useful when the planning problem is specific enough: guaranteed income, longevity risk, or a disciplined floor under part of the retirement plan.
