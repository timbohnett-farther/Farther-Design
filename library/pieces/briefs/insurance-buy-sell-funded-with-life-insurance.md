# Intelligent Wealth Series: Buy-Sell Agreements Funded With Life Insurance

## The Document and the Dollars Have to Match

**For business owners who have a buy-sell agreement, need one, or want to confirm that the funding actually supports the paper.**

A buy-sell agreement without funding is often just a promise written during calm years and tested during the worst possible moment. Life insurance is one of the most common ways to fund that obligation, but the usefulness of the policy depends on whether the valuation, ownership structure, tax treatment, and triggering events all align with the agreement.

## What the Funding Is Supposed to Solve

### Liquidity at the wrong time

When an owner dies, becomes disabled, or exits unexpectedly, the business or the remaining owners may need immediate liquidity. Insurance can provide that liquidity, but only if the amount, owner, beneficiary, and contractual triggers are coordinated correctly.

### The common mismatch

A business valued at $12 million three years ago may now be worth $20 million, while the policies still reflect the older figure. That creates a false sense of readiness.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative shortfall

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Current business value | $20,000,000 |
| Deceased owner's share | 50% |
| Buyout need | $10,000,000 |
| Insurance in force | $6,000,000 |
| Funding shortfall | $4,000,000 |

### The consequence

The agreement may still require the buyout, but the balance has to be financed, paid over time, or negotiated under stress. That is exactly what the funding was supposed to prevent.
