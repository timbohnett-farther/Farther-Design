# Intelligent Wealth Series: How Much Life Insurance Do You Actually Need

## Start With the Liability, Not the Rule of Thumb

**For households that want a clearer way to size life insurance based on real obligations, not generic multiples of income.**

Coverage decisions often get reduced to a slogan: ten times income, twelve times income, or some other round number. Real planning is more precise. The right amount of life insurance depends on who relies on the income, what liabilities would remain, what assets already exist, and how long the income replacement problem is expected to last.

## A Practical Coverage Formula

### Start with obligations

List what has to be funded if one spouse dies: income replacement, mortgage payoff, tuition, childcare, elder care, and final expenses. Then subtract assets already dedicated to those needs, such as cash reserves, survivor benefits, or vested retirement balances.

### Separate temporary from permanent needs

A family with young children may need high coverage for 15 to 20 years and much less after that. A business owner with estate tax or buy-sell exposure may have a separate permanent need that survives far beyond the income replacement years.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative calculation

| Need category | Amount |
|---|---|
| Income replacement for 15 years | $1,500,000 |
| Mortgage payoff | $650,000 |
| Future college funding | $300,000 |
| Childcare / transition costs | $150,000 |
| Final expenses and contingency | $100,000 |
| **Gross need** | **$2,700,000** |
| Less dedicated liquid assets | $400,000 |
| Less existing employer life insurance | $300,000 |
| **Estimated additional coverage need** | **$2,000,000** |

### Why the math matters

A rule of thumb based on income alone may have produced too little or too much coverage. The right answer sits in the household liabilities, not in the slogan.
