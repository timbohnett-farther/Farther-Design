# Intelligent Wealth Series: Premium Financing for Large Life Insurance Policies

## Leverage Can Expand Capacity — and Complexity

**For ultra-high-net-worth households considering whether borrowed capital should be used to fund a large life insurance strategy.**

Premium financing can help a household preserve liquidity while implementing a sizable life insurance policy. It can also add an entirely new layer of risk to what many families assume is a conservative protection tool. The strategy only works when policy design, collateral capacity, interest-rate assumptions, and lender behavior all remain inside a range the household can tolerate.

## What Is Being Financed

### The basic structure

A lender advances funds used to pay policy premiums. The client generally posts collateral, pays interest, and expects the policy or other assets to support eventual repayment depending on the design.

### The embedded risks

If borrowing costs rise, collateral demands increase, or policy performance lags assumptions, the household may have to contribute additional cash or unwind the arrangement on unfavorable terms.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative financing structure

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual premium | $500,000 |
| Financing period | 10 years |
| Total premiums advanced | $5,000,000 |
| Assumed borrowing rate at inception | 5.5% |
| Collateral posted | $2,000,000 |
| Annual interest carry in year one | $275,000 |

### Stress case

If rates rise from 5.5% to 8.0%, annual interest carry increases from $275,000 to $400,000 on the same financed premium balance before any other moving parts are considered. The strategy remains viable only if the household can comfortably absorb that change.
