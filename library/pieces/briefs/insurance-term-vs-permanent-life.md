# Intelligent Wealth Series: Term vs. Permanent Life Insurance

## Buy the Right Tool for the Right Job

**For households deciding whether they need inexpensive temporary protection, permanent coverage with long-duration guarantees, or a coordinated combination of both.**

The mistake is rarely buying too little terminology. The mistake is using a product designed for one job to solve a completely different one. Term insurance is built primarily for temporary income replacement and liability protection. Permanent insurance is built for a longer-duration problem: estate liquidity, business planning, legacy goals, or long-term coverage that is expected to remain in force for life.

## What Each Policy Actually Does

### Term insurance

Term insurance provides a death benefit for a defined period, such as 10, 20, or 30 years. It is usually the most efficient way to protect earned income, cover a mortgage, or fund the years when children are financially dependent.

### Permanent insurance

Permanent insurance is designed to stay in force for life as long as premiums and contract requirements are met. Depending on the product, it may also build cash value, support estate liquidity planning, or create funding for buy-sell, charitable, or trust-based planning.

### The decision question

The right comparison is not "which policy is better." It is which risk you are actually trying to insure. Most families have a temporary income problem first. Some also have a permanent liquidity or transfer problem later.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

### Illustrative premium comparison

| Coverage need | 20-year term | Permanent policy |
|---|---|---|
| Death benefit | $2,000,000 | $2,000,000 |
| Illustrative annual premium | $1,800 to $3,500 | $18,000 to $32,000 |
| Primary purpose | Income replacement | Lifetime coverage / liquidity / estate planning |
| Typical duration | Fixed term only | Intended for life |

### The cost of mismatch

A 38-year-old couple with two children may need $2 million of protection for the next 20 years, not necessarily for life. Using a permanent policy for a temporary problem may commit an extra $15,000 to $28,000 per year of cash flow that could have gone to retirement savings, debt reduction, or college funding.

### The benefit of matching policy to purpose

When temporary protection is solved with term and permanent coverage is reserved only for a true long-horizon need, the balance sheet stays more flexible. Insurance becomes targeted rather than oversized.
