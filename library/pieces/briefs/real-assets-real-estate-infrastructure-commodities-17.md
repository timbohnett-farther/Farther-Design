**For business owners and families who want income, inflation linkage, and the depreciation benefits that only physical assets provide.**

On July 4, 2025, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act permanently restored 100% bonus depreciation. Under prior law it was scheduled to fall to 20% in 2026 and disappear entirely in 2027. The IRS issued implementation guidance in January 2026. Opportunity Zones became permanent in the same bill.

At the same time, the largest infrastructure build in a generation is underway. Goldman Sachs projects $7.6 trillion of capital deployed across compute, data centers, and power between 2026 and 2031. Real assets are the one category where the tax code, the income, and the demand picture currently point the same direction. That does not make them easy. It makes them worth understanding properly.

## Three Categories, Three Different Jobs

### What each one actually does

| | Real estate | Infrastructure | Commodities |
|---|---|---|---|
| What you own | Land and buildings | Essential physical systems | Physical goods or futures |
| Return source | Rent plus appreciation | Contracted or regulated cash flow | Price movement only |
| Income from year one | Yes | Typically yes | No |
| Inflation linkage | Rents reset, often with a lag | Frequently contractual | Direct but volatile |
| Tax treatment | Depreciation, exchanges, basis step-up | Varies by structure | Often unfavorable |
| Primary risk | Leverage, vacancy, rates | Regulatory and construction | Volatility with no yield |

Commodities are the outlier. They produce no cash flow, which means the entire return depends on selling higher than you bought. They can hedge inflation. They cannot compound.

### What changed in the tax code

| Provision | Before | Now |
|---|---|---|
| Bonus depreciation | 40% in 2025, 20% in 2026, zero in 2027 | 100%, permanent, for qualifying property placed in service after January 19, 2025 |
| Qualified improvement property | 15-year recovery period | 15-year recovery, eligible for 100% bonus |
| Opportunity Zones | Winding down | Permanent, with a new program launching in 2027 |
| Low-income housing credit allocations | Temporary increase | Permanent increase, effective January 1, 2026 |

Qualifying property generally means assets with a useful life of 20 years or less, including land improvements and certain building components. A cost segregation study is how those components get identified.

Demand this large attracts capital faster than it attracts discipline.

### What a considered real asset allocation gives you

- Income that arrives from year one rather than after a decade of lockup
- Inflation linkage through rents and contracted escalators rather than through a price bet
- Depreciation that shelters current income, now permanently at 100% for qualifying property
- Capital gain deferral through exchanges and Opportunity Zone investment
- Exposure to the power and digital infrastructure build without concentrating in a single operator
- Collateral value that supports borrowing at rates below unsecured alternatives

### Why this matters now

Three things aligned. The depreciation environment is the most favorable it has been since 2017 and is no longer scheduled to expire, which removes the deadline and replaces it with a planning decision. Opportunity Zones became permanent with a new program arriving in 2027, creating a gap period that rewards careful sequencing. And the infrastructure build is absorbing capital at a scale that will define asset pricing in the category for years. Households that act on structure rather than on headlines will keep more of what these assets produce.

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite household acquires a $10 million industrial property using $3 million of equity and $7 million of debt. Land is $2 million and the building is $8 million. The only question is how the building gets depreciated.

### The cost of no action

Standard treatment spreads the building over 39 years.

| Year one | Standard depreciation |
|---|---|
| Depreciable basis | $8,000,000 |
| Year one deduction | $205,000 |
| Tax deferred at 40.8% | $84,000 |

### The benefit of acting now

A cost segregation study identifies components with shorter recovery periods. Those components qualify for 100% bonus depreciation in year one.

| Year one | With cost segregation and bonus depreciation |
|---|---|
| Components reclassified to 5, 7, and 15-year property | $2,000,000 |
| Bonus depreciation on those components | $2,000,000 |
| Remaining building depreciation | $154,000 |
| **Total year one deduction** | **$2,154,000** |
| **Tax deferred at 40.8%** | **$879,000** |
| Cost of the study | Roughly $15,000 |

### Side by side

| Measure | Standard | Cost segregation with bonus |
|---|---|---|
| Year one deduction | $205,000 | $2,154,000 |
| Year one tax deferred | $84,000 | $879,000 |
| Additional cash retained in year one | — | $795,000 |
| Return on the study cost | — | Roughly 53 to 1 |
| Deductions available in later years | Higher | Lower |
| Recapture exposure on sale | Lower | Higher |

The last two rows matter. This is acceleration, not elimination. The deduction is pulled forward, which means later years carry less of it, and depreciation is recaptured on sale unless the property is exchanged or held until death.

The $795,000 is not a windfall. It is the family's own capital, returned early enough to do something with. In this illustration it funds roughly a quarter of the next acquisition.

*Assumptions: illustrative purchase, an assumed 25% component reclassification that varies substantially by property type, a 40.8% combined federal ordinary rate, and 39-year nonresidential recovery. Deductions of this type are subject to passive activity loss limitations and are generally usable only against passive income unless the taxpayer qualifies as a real estate professional or the property is otherwise excepted. Many investors will not be able to use the full deduction in year one. Depreciation recapture applies on disposition. State tax is excluded. Consult your tax advisor before relying on any figure shown.*
