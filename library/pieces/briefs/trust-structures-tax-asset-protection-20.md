**For families who want their wealth held in a structure that reduces tax, deters claims, and directs outcomes across generations.**

The estate exemption is now permanent at $15 million per person and $30 million for a married couple. That removed a deadline and replaced it with a question: if there is no rush, what is the actual reason to put assets in a trust?

There are three reasons, and only one of them is the estate tax. A properly structured trust can move decades of future growth outside your estate, place assets beyond the reach of claims that do not yet exist, and direct how the next generation receives what you built. Each of those is a separate decision. Most families make all three at once, by accident, when they sign a document someone handed them.

## Three Jobs, One Document

### What a trust is actually for

| Job | What it accomplishes | What it does not do |
|---|---|---|
| Transfer tax reduction | Moves assets and all future growth outside the taxable estate | Reduce income tax on its own |
| Asset protection | Places assets beyond the reach of future claims | Defeat claims that already exist |
| Control and direction | Governs how and when beneficiaries receive | Guarantee family agreement |

### The distinction that matters most

| | Grantor trust | Non-grantor trust |
|---|---|---|
| Who pays income tax on trust earnings | You do, personally | The trust does |
| Effect of that payment | An additional transfer to beneficiaries, not treated as a gift | None |
| Assets included in your estate | Often no, depending on structure | Often no |
| Applicable income tax brackets | Yours | Compressed trust brackets |
| Best suited to | Growth assets you want out of the estate | Income assets in a favorable situs |

A grantor trust lets you pay the trust's income tax from your own pocket. Every dollar you pay reduces your estate and increases what the trust keeps, without using any exemption. It is one of the few places in the tax code where paying more tax personally is the better outcome.

### Why trust brackets punish accumulation

| Taxpayer | Taxable income reaching the top 37% federal rate |
|---|---|
| Non-grantor trust or estate, 2026 | $16,250 |
| Individual taxpayer | Many multiples higher |

Add the 3.8% net investment income tax and a non-grantor trust pays a combined 40.8% on income above roughly sixteen thousand dollars. Trusts that accumulate income rather than distribute it pay for the privilege.

### Situs is a decision, not a default

A trust does not have to live where you live. States compete for this business, and the differences are substantial.

| Jurisdiction | State income tax on trust income | Maximum trust duration | Notable feature |
|---|---|---|---|
| South Dakota | None | Perpetual | Trust litigation records permanently sealed by statute |
| Nevada | None | 365 years | Two-year creditor lookback, very few exception creditors |
| Delaware | Generally none for non-resident beneficiaries | Perpetual | Deepest body of trust case law |
| Alaska | None | Perpetual | Early asset protection statute |
| Wyoming | None | 1,000 years | Low cost, flexible directed trust rules |

*State taxation depends on beneficiary residence and the trust's connections to the state. The Supreme Court's Kaestner decision limits a state's ability to tax undistributed income of a properly sitused trust. Insurance premium tax also varies widely, from roughly 8 basis points in South Dakota to 350 in Nevada, which matters when a trust owns life insurance.*

### Which technique the rate environment favors

| Section 7520 rate condition | Techniques that improve |
|---|---|
| Higher rates, currently 5.20% in July 2026 versus 4.6% in January | Qualified personal residence trusts, charitable remainder trusts, charitable lead annuity trusts |
| Lower rates | Grantor retained annuity trusts, intrafamily loans, installment sales to grantor trusts |

The rate resets monthly. A technique that was in season in January may not be in August, and the rate locked at funding governs for the life of the structure.

### What a well-built trust structure gives you

- Future appreciation removed from your taxable estate, which is where the real money is
- Income tax paid by you rather than the trust, transferring value without using exemption
- A situs chosen for tax treatment, duration, and privacy rather than by geography
- Protection against future claims, established well before any claim exists
- Direction over how and when beneficiaries receive, across more than one generation
- Flexibility through decanting and directed trust provisions when circumstances change

### Why this matters now

Permanence changed the calculus. When the exemption was scheduled to fall, planning was about using it before it disappeared. Now it is about what the structure does over the next thirty years. The rate environment favors a different set of techniques than it did five years ago. And protection only works when it is put in place before there is anything to protect against, which makes the quiet years the right ones to act in.

*Trust law, tax rates, and state statutes described reflect law as of the publication date and change frequently. This is general education and not a recommendation for any particular structure. Work with a qualified estate planning attorney licensed in the relevant jurisdiction.*

## What the Numbers Look Like in Practice

A composite family holds a $6 million income-producing portfolio in their own names, in a state that taxes income at 6%. Their total estate already exceeds the couple exemption. They have twenty years of life expectancy.

### The cost of no action

| Measure | Amount |
|---|---|
| Annual portfolio income at 5% | $300,000 |
| Federal tax at 40.8% | $122,400 |
| State tax at 6% | $18,000 |
| Net annual income | $159,600 |
| Portfolio value at year 20, growing at 6% | $19,240,000 |
| Position at death | Entirely inside the taxable estate |

### The benefit of acting now

The family gifts the portfolio to a grantor trust sitused in a state with no trust income tax. The gift uses $6 million of their $30 million exemption.

| Measure | Effect |
|---|---|
| Assets removed from the estate | $6,000,000 plus all future growth |
| Growth outside the estate by year 20 | $13,240,000 |
| State income tax on trust income | Eliminated |
| Federal income tax on trust earnings | Paid personally by the grantor |
| Character of those payments | Additional transfers to beneficiaries, not gifts |
| Value moved out of the estate through tax payments over 20 years | $2,448,000 |

### Side by side

| Measure | No trust, home state | Grantor trust, favorable situs |
|---|---|---|
| Portfolio value at year 20 | $19.24M | $19.24M |
| Location of that value | Inside the estate | Outside the estate |
| Exemption consumed | $6.0M at death | $6.0M at funding |
| Growth sheltered from estate tax | $0 | $13.24M |
| Estate tax on that growth at 40% | $5.30M | $0 |
| Income tax payments moved out of the estate | $0 | $2.45M |
| Estate tax on those payments at 40% | $979,000 | $0 |
| Annual state income tax on the income | $18,000 | $0 |
| **Federal estate tax difference** | **$6.28M** | **—** |

The family did not give away anything they were spending. They moved an asset they intended to leave behind anyway, twenty years earlier than the estate would have moved it, and paid the income tax from a different pocket.

One caution belongs alongside the arithmetic. A favorable situs governs the trust, not every asset it holds. A 2026 federal decision held that the law where real property physically sits controls, which meant a trust formed in a protective state could not shield property located in a state that voids self-settled protection. Structure follows the asset, not the letterhead.

*Assumptions: $6 million funded, 5% income yield, 6% total annual growth, 40.8% combined federal ordinary rate, 6% state income tax, 40% federal estate tax rate, a twenty-year horizon, and an estate already exceeding the combined exemption. No valuation discounts are modeled. Grantor trust status must be structured to achieve the intended income and estate tax treatment, and the two do not follow automatically from each other. Asset protection depends on state law, the timing of funding relative to any claim, and applicable fraudulent transfer rules.*

Three points carry the piece. A trust does three separate jobs and you should know which one you are buying. Grantor status lets you pay the trust's income tax personally, which moves value to your beneficiaries without touching your exemption and is the most efficient transfer available. And situs is a decision worth making deliberately, because duration, privacy, creditor rules, and state income tax all turn on it.
