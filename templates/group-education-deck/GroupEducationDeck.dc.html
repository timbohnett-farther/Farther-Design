Group Education Deck
A broad-audience presentation advisors deliver to groups (seminars, webinars, employee sessions, business-owner roundtables, family education) with no personal financial data. Each education piece gets one. Example topic: turning savings into retirement income. Every deck follows the same ten-step arc, then the disclosure slide.
Positioning. Lead with the question people are trying to solve. Farther is a source of clarity and coordination, not the subject. Primary idea: financial decisions are connected; better outcomes start with the complete picture and the right order.
Group rules. Speak to "you", households, families or business owners, never a named client. No account-level recommendations, allocations, scorecards or personal action plans. End with a self-assessment, not a decision agenda.
Engagement. At least three per deck: opening poll, myth or reality, reflection prompt, case discussion, decision card, closing self-assessment. Tagged on each slide in bronze.
Layout. 16:9, 10 teaching slides plus the disclosure slide. 45–60 visible words, three visual zones maximum, 24px minimum type, direct chart labels, alt text on diagrams. Works on a room screen and a laptop webinar.
Data. Current, verifiable figures only, with a dated source line in the footer. Ranges where uncertainty matters. No simulated performance or planning probabilities. Cases are qualitative and labeled illustrative.
Compliance. Labeled general education on every teaching slide. Discuss categories, criteria and tradeoffs; never recommend a security, strategy, product, trust or tax position. Personal questions go to a private follow-up.
