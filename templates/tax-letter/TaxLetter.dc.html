What your preparer needs to know
01
You gave shares to a donor-advised fund
This counts as a charitable gift. Because the shares were held more than a year, the gift is generally deductible at market value and the gain on them is never taxed. Noncash gifts over $500 are reported on Form 8283.
Nov 19, 2025 · 150 shares of NVDA at $145.00, bought Oct 24, 2022$21,750
Bring: the fund's gift acknowledgment letter.
02
Retirement money was redeposited within 60 days
Your 1099-R will show this as a withdrawal. Custodians don't confirm whether a rollover met the 60-day rule, so it won't be marked tax-free. Your preparer reports it as a rollover so it isn't taxed.
May 24, 2025 · Marcus · Vanguard 401(k) to Schwab IRA$60,000
Bring: the Vanguard 1099-R and Schwab's deposit confirmation showing the date.
03
You converted IRA money to Roth
Conversions are reported on Form 8606. If either IRA ever held after-tax money, part of the conversion is tax-free. That share depends on all your traditional IRA balances on December 31.
Jul 5, 2025 · Marcus · Fidelity IRA to Fidelity Roth IRA$15,000
Aug 19, 2025 · Elena · Fidelity IRA to Fidelity Roth IRA$25,000
Total$40,000
Bring: both 1099-Rs, December 31 balances for every traditional IRA, and any past Form 8606.
04
You contributed to health savings accounts
If you were eligible, these are deductible on Form 8889 whether or not you itemize. Money taken out of a paycheck through an employer plan is handled differently, so your preparer needs to know these came from you directly.
Dec 1, 2025 · Marcus · catch-up contribution$1,000
Dec 1, 2025 · Elena · family contribution and catch-up$9,300
Total$10,300
Bring: HSA contribution statements. Form 5498-SA follows in May.
