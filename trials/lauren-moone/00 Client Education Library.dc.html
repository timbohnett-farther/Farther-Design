Twenty one-page guides to the ideas that matter most for long-term investors — what markets have actually done, how behavior shapes outcomes, and the arithmetic behind a durable plan.
Market literacy
01 The best days won’t wait for you Missing the ten best days cut a $192,167 outcome to $85,490. 02 The drop is normal. So is the recovery The average intra-year decline is 14.2% — yet 35 of the last 46 years finished higher. 03 Bulls outlast bears — by years Bears average about a year and -33%; bulls more than five years and +177%. 04 Markets outlast presidents Returns were positive under presidents of both parties in most years since 1948. 05 No market leads forever The longest U.S. leadership run ever — 204 months — ended in 2024; 2025 flipped the script. 06 What patience paid: $10,000 to $256,696 $10,000 invested in 1995 grew to $256,696 — through three major crashes. 07 Dividends, the quiet engine of wealth $7.58M with dividends reinvested since 1960, versus $1.14M on price alone. 08 Why last year’s winner rarely repeats Five different asset classes finished first in 15 years; the diversified mix never won or lost. 09 Rate hikes haven’t stopped the market The S&P 500 rose during all seven Fed hiking campaigns since 1983. 10 Recessions end. Markets come back Recession bears averaged -35.8% and a ~3.8-year round trip — every one ended in a new high.
Behavioral finance
11 Time tames risk A single year ranges -37% to +52%; every rolling 20-year period was positive. 12 The most expensive habit: chasing the market The average equity fund investor lagged the S&P 500 by 2.84 points a year for 30 years. 13 Your emotions peak at exactly the wrong time Record bearishness came four days before the 2009 bottom — then +68.6% in a year. 14 Waiting costs more than imperfect timing Perfect timing beat day-one investing by $15,522; never investing cost $123,198. 15 Invest it now, or ease it in? Lump-sum investing beat easing in about two-thirds of the time across seven markets. 16 What good advice is actually worth An estimated 4.92% of annual value — nearly half of it behavioral coaching.
Planning and portfolio
17 How money doubles: the rule of 72 72 ÷ your return = years to double; the S&P 500’s long-run rate doubles money every ~7.2 years. 18 Real diversification moves differently International stocks correlate 0.87 with the U.S.; gold just 0.07. 19 Cash never loses — except to inflation $10,000 in cash since 2000 buys $5,349 of goods today. 20 Same returns, opposite retirements Identical 5% average returns: one retiree ends with $1.59M, another with $0.
