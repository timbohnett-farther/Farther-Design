Equity compensation
Trading concentrated stock into pooled exposure, subject to restrictions and risks
An exchange fund allows a holder of concentrated stock to contribute shares to a pooled fund alongside other concentrated holders, receiving diversified exposure in return without triggering an immediate taxable sale.
How it works
By pooling concentrated positions from multiple investors, an exchange fund achieves diversification for each participant without any individual sale occurring, deferring the capital gains tax that would otherwise be due, though participants must meet minimum position size requirements and accept a multi-year lock-up period.
Step 1
Confirm the concentrated position meets the exchange fund's minimum size and eligibility requirements.
Step 2
Contribute the concentrated shares to the pooled fund.
Step 3
Receive an interest in the diversified pool, achieving immediate diversification without a taxable sale.
Step 4
Remain subject to the fund's lock-up period before redemption is available.

Exchange Funds: Diversify Without an Immediate Sale · fit and reference
Who this is designed for
Holders of a large, low-basis concentrated position seeking diversification without a sale
Investors comfortable with a multi-year lock-up period
Anyone whose position size meets the exchange fund's minimum eligibility requirements
Key benefits at a glance
Achieves diversification without an immediate taxable event
Defers the capital gains tax on the contributed position
Provides exposure to a broader, pooled portfolio immediately
Can complement other concentration-reduction strategies over time
Important considerations
Lock-up surprise
Underestimating the length and illiquidity of the fund's lock-up period.
Deferral misunderstanding
Assuming the gain is eliminated rather than merely deferred.
Fee blind spot
Failing to compare exchange fund fees and terms against alternative concentration-reduction strategies.
Quick reference
Typical minimum position size
$500,000 to $1M+
Typical lock-up period
~7 years
Tax treatment

Best suited for

How Farther approaches this
Farther evaluates exchange funds alongside other concentration-reduction alternatives, such as staged sales and collars, comparing terms and lock-up periods to determine the best fit for a specific position and household.
