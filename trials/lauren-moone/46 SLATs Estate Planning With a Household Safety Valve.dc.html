Advanced trust structures
Spousal Lifetime Access Trusts; completed gifts with indirect access through a spouse, and reciprocal-trust and divorce/death risk considerations
A SLAT allows a married household to make a completed gift out of the donor spouse's estate while preserving a safety valve of indirect access through the non-donor spouse, though reciprocal-trust doctrine, divorce, and mortality risks all require careful structuring.
How it works
A SLAT provides a middle ground for households uncertain about permanently and completely giving up access to gifted assets, by naming the non-donor spouse as a lifetime beneficiary, the household retains a plausible path back to those funds through spousal distributions, while still removing the assets from the donor's taxable estate.
Step 1
The donor spouse establishes an irrevocable trust naming the other spouse as a lifetime beneficiary.
Step 2
The donor spouse gifts assets into the trust, using available gift tax exemption.
Step 3
The trustee may make discretionary distributions to the non-donor spouse during their lifetime.
Step 4
The household carefully avoids creating mirror-image SLATs that could trigger reciprocal trust doctrine concerns.

SLATs: Estate Planning With a Household Safety Valve · fit and reference
Who this is designed for
Married UHNW households wanting to use gift tax exemption without total loss of access
Couples comfortable with the structural risks tied to divorce or the non-donor spouse's death
Households seeking a completed gift strategy that still allows for family flexibility
Key benefits at a glance
Removes assets and future appreciation from the donor spouse's taxable estate
Preserves a plausible indirect path back to trust assets for the household
Can be combined with grantor trust status for additional wealth transfer benefit
Offers flexibility relative to fully irrevocable trusts with no spousal access provision
Important considerations
Mirror-image trusts
Creating reciprocal SLATs for each other with substantially similar terms, risking unwinding under reciprocal trust doctrine.
Divorce planning gap
Failing to address what happens to indirect household access if the couple later divorces.
Overreliance on access
Treating the SLAT's indirect access as guaranteed, rather than genuinely discretionary and at risk.

Questions worth asking your advisor
01
How much estate tax exposure would a SLAT structure help remove from our household's estate?
02
What happens to our indirect access to SLAT assets in the event of divorce or the death of my spouse?
03
How is this SLAT being differentiated from any prior or contemplated trusts to avoid reciprocal trust doctrine concerns?
