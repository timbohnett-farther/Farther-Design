Founder liquidity and partial diversification through a recapitalization; compares common equity, preferred equity, control, downside, and future upside
A preferred equity recapitalization allows a founder to receive partial liquidity from their business today, often while retaining significant control and future upside, by restructuring some ownership into preferred equity with a defined return preference rather than selling the business outright.
How it works
Rather than selling the entire business, or continuing to hold 100% illiquid ownership, a preferred equity recapitalization creates a middle path, a portion of the founder's equity is exchanged for cash or converted into preferred equity carrying a defined return preference, while common equity ownership, and often day-to-day control, is substantially retained for continued participation in the business's future growth.
Step 1
Evaluate the business's current valuation and the founder's desired liquidity amount.
Step 2
Structure a portion of ownership as preferred equity, carrying a defined return preference senior to common equity.
Step 3
The founder receives cash proceeds from the recapitalization while retaining common equity ownership and typically operational control.
Step 4
Future growth and eventual sale proceeds are shared between preferred and common equity according to the negotiated structure.

Who this is designed for
Founders wanting partial liquidity and diversification without fully exiting their business
Business owners seeking to de-risk personal net worth while continuing to grow the company
Anyone evaluating recapitalization as a middle path between full illiquidity and a complete sale
Key benefits at a glance
Provides meaningful liquidity without requiring a full exit from the business
Allows the founder to retain substantial ownership, and often operational control
Diversifies a founder's personal net worth away from a single concentrated business asset
Preserves future upside participation in the business's continued growth
Important considerations
Underestimating preference terms
Failing to fully understand how the preferred equity's return preference affects the founder's eventual proceeds in various future sale scenarios.
Control dilution surprise
Assuming operational control is fully preserved without carefully negotiating governance terms.
Comparing only headline numbers
Evaluating the recapitalization solely on immediate liquidity without modeling long-term proceeds under different future outcomes.
