Tax planning
A tax-free exchange can still be a bad trade
Section 1035 can allow certain life insurance, endowment, and annuity contracts to be exchanged for a different contract without current income tax recognition if the rules are met. That is powerful, but it does not guarantee the exchange is economically wise.
How it works
Fees, surrender charges, new contractual periods, underwriting, and product economics still matter. The right test is whether the economic improvement outweighs the cost of leaving the current contract and entering the new one.
Step 1
Price the exit: surrender charge, market value adjustment, and lost guarantees.
Step 2
Compare the ongoing economics, rider fee against rider fee.
Step 3
Read the new surrender schedule, which starts over on day one.
Step 4
Confirm carrier strength and that the contract fits the objective better.

What it does not erase

1035 exchanges · fit and reference
What an exchange may improve
Lower ongoing costs, including rider and contract fees
Stronger riders or improved guarantees
A better crediting structure for the same market exposure
A design more suited to the actual long-term objective
A financially stronger carrier standing behind the guarantees
What the exchange costs you
The surrender charge on the existing contract, paid out of account value
A new surrender schedule, restarting the illiquid period
New underwriting for life policies, where health may have changed
Loss of any favorable guarantees embedded in the old contract
New contestability and exclusion periods on a replacement life policy
Three outcomes of an honest review
Keep
The existing contract's guarantees or remaining surrender schedule outweigh the savings available.
Exchange
The ongoing economics improve enough to justify the exit cost and the new holding period.
Surrender
Neither contract serves the objective, and the proceeds belong somewhere else entirely.
