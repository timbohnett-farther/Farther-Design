Retirement planning
The match is compensation you leave on the table if you skip it
A 401(k) lets you contribute part of your paycheck, often pre-tax, and many employers match a portion of what you put in. Limits are far higher than an IRA's, and the match is an immediate, dollar-for-dollar increase in your savings.
How it works
Employer matching is compensation you only receive if you contribute enough to capture it. Beyond the elective deferral limit, many plans also allow additional after-tax contributions up to a much higher combined limit.
Step 1
Confirm the match formula in your plan document, including the percentage matched and the pay cap.
Step 2
Set your deferral high enough to capture the full match before directing money anywhere else.
Step 3
Check your vesting schedule so a job change does not leave employer contributions behind.
Step 4
Decide between pre-tax and Roth deferrals, and ask whether after-tax contributions are allowed.

Employer plans · fit and reference
What understanding your plan buys you
Match capture is an immediate, dollar-for-dollar increase in your savings if you're vested
Pre-tax contributions reduce your current taxable income
Automatic payroll deduction makes saving consistent and effortless
Some plans offer a Roth 401(k), allowing after-tax contributions with tax-free growth
Worth checking before you set and forget
Vesting schedules can return unvested employer contributions to the plan if you leave early
A match capped at a percentage of pay means front-loading deferrals can cut what you receive
Plan investment menus vary widely in cost and quality
After-tax contributions are only possible if your specific plan permits them
Three layers of the same limit
Employee deferral
What you elect from your own paycheck, pre-tax or Roth, subject to the annual elective limit.
Employer contributions
Match and profit sharing, which sit outside your elective limit but count against the combined limit.
After-tax contributions
Available only if your plan allows, filling the space between the first two layers and the combined limit.
