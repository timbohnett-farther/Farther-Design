Education funding
The federal benefit is identical. The state benefit is not
A 529 plan gives tax-free growth and tax-free withdrawals for qualified education costs, including K–12 tuition, college, and certain apprenticeship programs. Contributions are never deductible federally, so the entire difference between plans is state treatment.
How it works
Because state tax benefits differ so widely, confirm your own state's rules before assuming a particular plan is the best choice. The best plan for a Texas resident is not the best plan for a New York resident.
Step 1
Confirm whether your state offers a deduction or credit, and what it is capped at.
Step 2
Check whether your state has tax parity, allowing any state's plan to qualify.
Step 3
Size the annual contribution to the cap where a cap applies.
Step 4
Plan for leftover funds: a family-member beneficiary change, or a Roth IRA rollover within limits.

529 plans · fit and reference
What a 529 gives you
Tax-free growth and tax-free qualified withdrawals at the federal level
A potential state income tax deduction or credit, depending on residence
High contribution limits compared with other education savings vehicles
Use for K–12 tuition and certain apprenticeship costs, not just college
Unused funds can move to another family member or, within limits, roll into a Roth IRA for the beneficiary
Where the rules diverge
Nine states have no income tax, so no deduction is available regardless of plan
New Mexico, South Carolina, and West Virginia allow the entire contribution to be deducted, uncapped
California, Hawaii, Kentucky, and North Carolina tax income but offer no 529 deduction
Most states require the in-state plan; tax-parity states do not
Three state categories to place yourself in
No income tax
Nine states including Texas, Florida, and Nevada. Plan choice is driven by cost and investment menu alone.
Tax parity
Arizona, Kansas, Minnesota, Missouri, Montana, Ohio, and Pennsylvania among others. Any state's plan earns the benefit.
In-state required
Most remaining states. The deduction or credit applies only to contributions made to the home-state plan.
