Portfolio construction
The mix matters more than the picks
Asset allocation is how you divide a portfolio among stocks, bonds, cash, and sometimes alternatives, based on goals, timeline, and risk tolerance. Research on institutional portfolios (Brinson, Hood, and Beebower, "Determinants of Portfolio Performance," Financial Analysts Journal, 1986) consistently finds that the mix drives long-term return and volatility more than which funds sit inside each category.
How it works
Your ideal allocation is not static. It should evolve as goals, timeline, income needs, and risk tolerance change over a lifetime.
Step 1
Define the goal and the date the money is needed.
Step 2
Separate willingness to accept volatility from the capacity to absorb it.
Step 3
Set the target mix, then let it govern rebalancing and new contributions.
Step 4
Revisit at each life stage: career growth, retirement, legacy planning.

Asset allocation · fit and reference
What a deliberate allocation gives you
Portfolio risk aligned to your actual ability and willingness to handle volatility
A disciplined framework that reduces reactive, emotion-driven decisions
Growth balanced against near-term cash flow and liquidity needs
A foundation that guides rebalancing and future contributions
A structure that adapts naturally as life stages change
The cost of being too conservative too early
Five years too conservative: roughly 8%–12% lower ending wealth
Ten years: roughly 18%–25% lower
Twenty years: roughly 35%–50% lower
Holding a near-retirement mix decades early is a decision, not a neutral default
What sets the mix
Time horizon
How long until the money is spent, which determines how much recovery time a drawdown has.
Risk tolerance
What level of volatility you can hold through without abandoning the plan.
Cash flow needs
What must be liquid and near-term, and therefore cannot carry equity risk.
