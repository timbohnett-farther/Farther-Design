Tax planning
Same holdings, same risk, a different tax bill
Asset location places each investment in the account type where it is taxed most favorably: tax-inefficient holdings such as bonds in tax-deferred accounts, tax-efficient holdings such as broad index funds in taxable accounts.
How it works
Allocation is about what you own. Location is about where you hold it. The two work together, and location is often overlooked because it does not change the investment mix, only its tax efficiency.
Step 1
Map every holding across taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts.
Step 2
Rank holdings by how heavily they are taxed each year.
Step 3
Move the most heavily taxed into sheltered space, without changing the overall mix.
Step 4
Re-check as balances, contributions, and holdings shift over time.
Seeing it in action

Asset location · fit and reference
What thoughtful location gives you
Lower total tax drag without changing your allocation
Better use of scarce tax-deferred and tax-free account space
Meaningfully higher after-tax returns over long horizons
A complement to tax-loss harvesting and Roth conversions
The most value for households holding taxable, tax-deferred, and Roth accounts together
What tends to be misplaced
Taxable bonds and bond funds held in a taxable account, taxed annually at up to 32%–37%
REITs paying non-qualified dividends, taxed at ordinary income rates
High-turnover active funds generating short-term gains
Broad index funds and tax-efficient ETFs sitting in sheltered space they do not need
Where each account type earns its keep
Taxable
Best for tax-efficient equity exposure, where gains can qualify for long-term rates and losses can be harvested.
Tax-deferred
Best for the heaviest annual tax burdens: taxable bonds, REITs, and high-turnover strategies.
Roth
Best for the highest expected growth, since appreciation is never taxed again.
