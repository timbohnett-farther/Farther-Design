How advice works · client education guide
How advice works
Suitable and best interest are not the same standard
A brokerage account is typically transaction-based, executing individual trades for a per-trade or embedded commission. An advisory account is an ongoing relationship managing your investments for an asset-based fee, typically under a fiduciary standard.
How it works
Advisors acting in a fiduciary capacity are legally required to act in your best interest at all times. Brokers operate under Regulation Best Interest, which requires a recommendation be in your best interest at the time it is made, but does not carry the fiduciary's ongoing duty of care and loyalty across the full relationship.
Step 1
Identify which type of account and relationship you actually have.
Step 2
Ask what standard of care applies to the advice you receive.
Step 3
Establish whether fees are per-transaction or an ongoing advisory fee.
Step 4
Confirm whether ongoing monitoring is included, rather than assumed.

Three points of comparison
Standard of care
Regulation Best Interest asks whether a recommendation was in your interest when made. A fiduciary standard asks whether it is the best available for you.
Compensation
Commissions are paid per transaction. An advisory fee is a share of assets, paid whether or not anything trades.
Disclosure
Advisory relationships require full disclosure of conflicts. Brokerage disclosure is more limited.
