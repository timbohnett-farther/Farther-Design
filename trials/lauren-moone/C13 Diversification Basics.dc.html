Risk management
No single holding should decide your year
Diversification spreads money across asset classes, sectors, geographies, and individual securities so no one investment can significantly harm the whole. Different holdings react differently to the same event, which smooths the overall experience.
How it works
Diversification does not eliminate risk or guarantee against loss. It reduces the impact any single investment, sector, or event can have on your total wealth.
Step 1
Look through every account at once, since concentration hides across them.
Step 2
Check for overlap between funds holding the same underlying names.
Step 3
Count employer stock, options, and deferred comp as part of the exposure.
Step 4
Rebalance back toward the target as markets move the weights.

Diversification · fit and reference
What diversification gives you
Less impact from a single stock or sector underperforming
Smoother portfolio volatility over time
Exposure to a broader set of growth opportunities across the economy
A guard against concentration risk, especially with significant employer stock
More consistent long-term outcomes
Where concentration hides
Employer stock held across a 401(k), an ESPP, and vested equity at once
Several funds holding the same largest names
A single sector reached through multiple products
Home-country bias that leaves geographic exposure narrower than it looks
Three axes to diversify along
Asset class
Stocks, bonds, and cash respond differently to the same economic conditions.
Sector
Concentration in one industry means one industry's cycle sets your outcome.
Geography
Domestic-only exposure ties results to a single economy and currency.
