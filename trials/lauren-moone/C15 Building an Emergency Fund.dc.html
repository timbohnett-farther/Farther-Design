Financial foundations
A reserve exists so a surprise never forces a bad decision
An emergency fund is cash set aside for the unexpected: job loss, medical bills, urgent home or car repairs. It stays in a safe, liquid account rather than in the market.
How it works
Without one, an unexpected expense can force a sale of investments at the worst time or push a balance onto a high-interest card, undoing years of progress.
Step 1
Total your essential monthly expenses, not your full budget.
Step 2
Choose the multiple that matches your income stability.
Step 3
Hold it somewhere safe and liquid, where return is not the objective.
Step 4
Rebuild it first after any use, before resuming other goals.

Emergency reserves · fit and reference
What a funded reserve prevents
Forced selling of investments during a market downturn
Reliance on high-interest credit cards or loans during a crisis
Anxiety that undermines confidence in a long-term strategy
Cash-flow disruption during job loss or reduced business revenue
Poor decisions made under stress and time pressure
Sizing it honestly
Essential expenses, not total spending, set the monthly figure
Variable or commission income argues for the higher multiple
A single household income concentrates the risk of any disruption
Business owners often need the largest buffer of all
Three tests for where to hold it
Safe
Principal should not fluctuate with markets. Return is not the objective here.
Liquid
Available within days without penalty, since emergencies do not wait for settlement.
Separate
Held apart from everyday spending, so the balance is not quietly consumed.
