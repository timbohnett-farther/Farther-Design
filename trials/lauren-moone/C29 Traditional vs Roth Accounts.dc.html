Retirement planning
The choice is a bet on your future tax rate
Traditional accounts are funded with pre-tax dollars, reducing taxable income today, with withdrawals taxed as ordinary income later. Roth accounts take after-tax dollars, with no deduction now, and qualified withdrawals are entirely tax-free.
How it works
There is no universally better choice. The answer depends on your current bracket, your expected bracket in retirement, and how many years the money has to grow. Many households benefit from holding both.
Step 1
Compare your current marginal rate against the rate you expect in retirement.
Step 2
Weight toward Traditional in high-earning years, and Roth in lower ones.
Step 3
Hold both, so taxable income can be managed in retirement.
Step 4
Revisit as income changes, and consider conversions in low-income years.

Traditional and Roth · fit and reference
What holding both gives you
Tax diversification, and flexibility to manage taxable income in retirement
Tax-free growth in the Roth, most valuable over long horizons
A lower current tax bill from Traditional contributions during high-earning years
No required distributions from a Roth IRA during the owner's lifetime
A stronger position for Roth conversions later in life
What can shift the answer
A future bracket higher than today's, which favors Roth
A future bracket lower than today's, which favors Traditional
Roth IRA eligibility phasing out at higher income levels
Years of growth remaining, since tax-free compounding rewards time
Three inputs to the decision
Current bracket
What a deduction is worth to you this year.
Expected bracket
What the eventual withdrawal is likely to cost, including required distributions.
Time to grow
How many years the balance compounds, which magnifies the value of tax-free growth.
