Insurance planning
The right amount is discovered, not guessed
Coverage decisions often get reduced to a slogan: ten times income, twelve times income, or some other round number. The right amount depends on who relies on the income, what liabilities would remain, what assets already exist, and how long the income replacement problem is expected to last.
How it works
Step 1
Total the obligations: income replacement, mortgage, tuition, childcare, final expenses.
Step 2
Subtract dedicated liquid assets, survivor benefits, and existing employer coverage.
Step 3
Separate the temporary need from any permanent one, and date each.
Step 4
Revisit as debt falls, assets grow, and children age out of dependency.

Temporary and permanent are different problems
A family with young children may need high coverage for 15 to 20 years and much less after that. A business owner may have a separate permanent need that survives the income replacement years.

Sizing coverage · fit and reference
What belongs in the calculation
Income replacement for the years dependents actually rely on it
Mortgage and other debt that would remain outstanding
Education funding still ahead of you
Childcare, elder care, and transition costs a surviving spouse would face
Final expenses and a contingency reserve
What reduces the number
Cash reserves and taxable assets already earmarked for these needs
Vested retirement balances available to the survivor
Survivor benefits from Social Security or a pension
Employer group coverage, though it is rarely portable
Three household shapes, three answers
Young family
High temporary need. Income replacement and education dominate; permanent coverage is usually premature.
Dual income, no children
Lower need. Each earner is largely self-supporting, so debt payoff and final expenses drive the figure.
Business owner with an estate need
Two separate needs. A temporary income layer plus a permanent liquidity layer sized to the estate or buy-sell obligation.

How Farther approaches this
Farther models insurance need as part of a broader financial plan rather than as an isolated quote exercise. That makes it easier to identify which needs are temporary, which are permanent, and where existing assets already reduce the required coverage amount.
