Home ownership · benefits, costs, and timing
What are the benefits and costs of home ownership?
Forced savings on one side, stretched budgets on the other
Benefits
Building equity, a form of forced savings
Improvements add value
Houses are appreciating assets, in the long run
Protection against rising rent
Tax breaks for some interest payments and capital gains, including a state and local tax (SALT) deduction cap raised to $40,000 from $10,000 through 2029 under the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, and mortgage insurance premiums once again counting as deductible mortgage interest
Emotional connection to one's home
Costs
Monthly payments tend to stretch budgets once property tax, insurance, homeowners association (HOA), and maintenance fees are included
Maintenance is your responsibility
Reduced flexibility to move, since it is expensive to sell
A big upfront payment over-weights real estate in your overall portfolio
When should you buy a house?
You should buy when you are ready.
You are in a stable place and can commit to a house for at least 5 years
You have determined where you want to live, with a commute and school district that works for you
You know your budget for a house, and you are sticking to it
You have a down payment ready for 20% of the home price, plus closing costs
You are comfortable with the trade-offs of renting versus buying in your market, from a financial and psychological perspective
Do not try to time the market. Prices will fluctuate up and down, but if you are ready to commit, you will be fine in the long run

How much house can you afford?
Make sure this is the right question
Most people opt for the upper end of their affordability range, but there is no rule requiring you to get there
Any savings you secure from buying a less expensive house allows you to save for and spend on other goals, including vacations, hobbies, second homes, college, and retirement, so keep that in mind as you conduct your search
Be aware of your own financial situation
When you do think about the amount you can afford to spend on a house, you usually want to consider how easily you can afford the monthly mortgage payment, insurance, property taxes, and homeowners association (HOA) fees.
