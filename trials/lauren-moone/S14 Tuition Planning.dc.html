Tuition planning · 529 basics and superfunding
What is a 529?
A state-sponsored plan for education expenses, in tax-advantaged accounts
Investments grow tax-free and earnings can be withdrawn tax-free if used to pay for qualified educational expenses, usually college and grad school. Some states provide an extra bonus by allowing you to deduct contributions from your income to reduce state taxes as well. For each child, each parent can contribute up to $19,000 annually without any tax consequences.
Advantages of 529 plans
Tax-advantaged, with no capital gains, dividend, or interest taxes
Some states offer additional tax breaks as incentives to participate
Can be used for higher and lower educational expenses, including up to $20,000 per year for K-12 tuition and expenses, doubled from $10,000 starting in 2026, and now also covering curriculum materials, tutoring, standardized test fees, dual-enrollment courses, and educational therapies for students with disabilities
Contributions can be withdrawn at any time without tax consequences
The owner of the account retains control
What is superfunding?
Superfunding allows you to make five years worth of contributions in year one without triggering taxes.

A complementary option: Trump Accounts
Starting July 4, 2026, families can also open a Trump Account, a new tax-advantaged savings account for children under 18, allowing contributions of up to $5,000 per year. Children born between 2025 and 2028 receive a one-time $1,000 federal seed contribution if their parents opt into the pilot program.
