SMAs · structure and comparison
What is an SMA?
An account you own, holding individual positions
While most advisors and their clients are familiar with mutual funds and exchange traded funds (ETFs), separately managed accounts (SMAs) can be a useful asset allocation tool. SMAs have many of the same characteristics as mutual funds and ETFs with one primary difference: SMAs are not a pooled investment vehicle, but an account owned by an investor with individual positions held in the account.
This means that an SMA investor owns the assets in the account directly, as opposed to owning shares of a mutual fund or ETF with underlying ownership. That structure allows for more flexibility, transparency, and tax efficiency for the SMA investor.
