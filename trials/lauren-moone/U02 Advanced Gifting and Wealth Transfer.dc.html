Estate planning
Move the growth, not just the money
For nearly a decade estate planning ran against a clock. The One Big Beautiful Bill Act ended that: the exemption is now $15 million per person, $30 million per couple, permanent and indexed for inflation from 2027.
The deadline is gone. The arithmetic is not. A dollar gifted today takes all of its future growth out of your estate with it, and at 40% that growth is the entire game. The families who do best from here use the transfer capacity that does not consume exemption at all, then spend exemption where it buys the most.

What the table does not show
Gifted assets carry over basis rather than receiving an adjustment at death. For a family well above the exemption with two decades of compounding ahead, the transfer tax saved dwarfs the capital gains cost. Near the threshold, the calculation can reverse. Gifting also means the assets are gone, which is a decision about your own security before it is a decision about tax.

Three points carry the piece
Substantial transfer capacity exists before exemption is touched, and direct tuition and medical payments are the most overlooked provisions in the code. The generation-skipping exemption is separate, is not portable, and is not automatically allocated to most trusts, which makes it the easiest exemption to waste. And what you transfer is not the asset but its future growth, which is why timing matters more than size.
How Farther approaches this
