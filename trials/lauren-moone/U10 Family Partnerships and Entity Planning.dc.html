Estate planning
Give away the economics, keep the control
A family partnership separates control from economics, which lets the senior generation hand down the value of an asset while continuing to run it. It also produces a valuation discount, because an interest that carries no control and cannot be sold is worth less than its share of the underlying assets.

Section 2036 lets the IRS pull assets back into the estate at full value when the transferor retained possession, enjoyment, income rights, or the right to decide who benefits. In Estate of Anne Milner Fields v. Commissioner, T.C. Memo 2024-90, the Tax Court disallowed discounts entirely on a partnership holding roughly $10.8 million of reported value: the estate had retained assets outside the partnership, but not enough to cover $1.45 million of cash bequests, a specific stock gift, and the anticipated estate tax.
What a well-run family entity gives you
Transfer of economic value at a discount, stretching every dollar of exemption
Retained control of operations, investments, and distribution timing
Consolidated management of assets that would otherwise fragment across heirs
Charging order protection in many states, limiting what a beneficiary's creditor can reach
A framework for bringing the next generation into ownership gradually

What the table does not show
The gifted interests carry over the family's original basis rather than receiving an adjustment at death. If those assets are eventually sold, the capital gains tax on that carried-over basis offsets part of the estate tax saved. For a family well above the exemption with two decades of growth ahead, the trade favors the gift. For a family near or below $30 million, it often does not. That is the actual decision, and it is arithmetic rather than opinion.

Three points carry the piece
A family entity separates control from economics, which is the only clean way to transfer value while still running the asset. Discounts are multiplicative rather than additive, so the combined figure is always smaller than the two numbers suggest. And the structure survives or fails on operation rather than on drafting, because every case the IRS has won turned on how the partnership actually behaved.
How Farther approaches this
Family entities are drafted by attorneys, valued by appraisers, and reported by accountants, and they fail in the years between those events when nobody is watching how the entity operates. Farther stays in the coordinating seat rather than handing off, and models the thirty-year outcome, including the basis trade-off, before anyone drafts a document.
What that looks like in practice
Modeling whether a discount actually beats holding for a basis adjustment, given your estate and horizon
Confirming enough liquidity stays outside the entity to fund bequests, expenses, and any estate tax
Coordinating appraisal timing so the qualified appraisal supports the transfer as filed
Tracking annual gifting against the exclusion and remaining exemption across every recipient
Watching for the operating patterns that have cost other families their discounts
