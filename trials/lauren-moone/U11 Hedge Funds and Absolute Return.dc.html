Alternative investments
The return that matters is the one you keep
Per HFRI (Hedge Fund Research) index data, hedge funds are having their best run in fifteen years, passing $5 trillion in assets with back-to-back double-digit years. That record deserves a fair hearing, and one question that rarely gets asked in the meeting: what does this need to earn, before fees and before ordinary income tax, to beat what you already own?
Traditional managers are measured against an index. Absolute return managers aim to make money regardless of market direction, which means the product is not return. It is return that behaves differently from everything else you own. Judged as a return engine, these strategies usually disappoint. Judged as a shock absorber, some of them earn their place.

Three points carry the piece
Absolute return strategies sell behavior, not performance, so the correlation number matters more than the return number. Headline fees and actual fees are different things, sometimes by a factor of three. And in a taxable account, ordinary income treatment sets a hurdle so high that where you hold the position matters more than which manager you pick.
How Farther approaches this
Firms that distribute hedge funds are paid to place them. Farther is fee-based with open architecture, so there is no revenue reason to recommend one and no reason to present a headline fee without the pass-through expenses behind it. Location, which for this category determines the outcome, is handled first.
What that looks like in practice
Calculating the required gross return before a strategy is recommended, using your actual marginal rates
Placing tax-inefficient strategies in tax-advantaged capacity first, then sizing the taxable remainder
Requesting complete fee disclosure including pass-through operating expenses, then negotiating on the total
Measuring a manager's correlation to what you already own rather than accepting the category label
Assigning every position a defined role and reviewing it against that role, not against the index
