Portfolio construction
The math behind your portfolio, and where it breaks
Modern Portfolio Theory answers a real question well: for a given level of risk, which mix produces the most return. The catch is that the answer is only as good as three inputs, and for a household holding private funds, an operating business, and a taxable account, at least two of those arrive distorted.

Three points carry the piece
The efficient frontier is a conclusion drawn from three estimates, so the quality of the estimates determines the quality of the portfolio. Private market statistics arrive understated by construction, which biases models toward holding more of them. And the model was never built to see taxes, illiquidity, or the business that funds your life, which means those have to be added by the people running it.
How Farther approaches this
Most portfolio recommendations arrive as an output with the inputs hidden. There is no revenue reason to prefer one asset class over another, and no reason to accept a flattering volatility number at face value. Construction happens against the whole balance sheet, including the business and assets Farther does not manage.
What that looks like in practice
Correcting private market volatility and correlation inputs before position sizes are set
Setting an explicit illiquidity budget tied to liquidity needs, capital call schedule, and horizon
Modeling the tax cost of every rebalance and sequencing trades against harvested losses
Stress-testing correlation assumptions for the scenario where diversifiers stop diversifying
Treating the operating business and future earnings as positions on the frontier rather than footnotes
