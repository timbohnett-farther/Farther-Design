In public markets, picking the wrong manager costs a rounding error. In private markets it can cost the entire premium you came for. Per Cambridge Associates US buyout benchmark data, top-quartile managers delivered roughly 22% IRR over the past decade against a median near 13%, gross of fees and before adjusting for survivorship bias.

What a disciplined allocation gives you
Exposure to companies that never reach public markets, projected at 10.2% annually for private equity
Contracted income from private credit that floats with rates rather than depending on an exit
Fee reduction through co-investment, which typically carries reduced or no management fee and carry
Vintage diversification, so you are not betting on one year's entry prices
A capital call schedule you can plan around rather than react to

Three points carry the piece
Private markets are not one asset class but three distinct exposures with different cash flow shapes and different risks. Manager selection matters here by an order of magnitude more than in public markets, which makes access quality the central question. And how you pace and structure a commitment can change the fee load by hundreds of thousands of dollars without changing a single investment decision.
How Farther approaches this
Many firms offer private markets through a proprietary platform, which means the menu is shaped by distribution economics rather than manager quality. Farther has open-architecture access, so there is no revenue reason to prefer one manager and no additional access layer between you and the fund.
What that looks like in practice
Building a commitment pacing plan across multiple vintage years rather than deploying in one
Evaluating managers on realized distributions and consistency across cycles, not reported paper value
Sourcing co-investment where the fee reduction is real and the concentration is acceptable
Modeling capital calls against liquidity, tax events, and other commitments before committing
Reading the disagreement in private credit default data honestly rather than quoting whichever number sells
