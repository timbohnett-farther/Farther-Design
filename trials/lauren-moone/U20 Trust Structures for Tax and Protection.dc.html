Estate planning
Where your assets live changes what they cost you
The exemption is now permanent at $15 million per person. That removed a deadline and replaced it with a question: if there is no rush, what is the actual reason to put assets in a trust? There are three, and only one of them is the estate tax.

Every dollar of trust income tax you pay personally reduces your estate and increases what the trust keeps, without using any exemption. It is one of the few places where paying more tax personally is the better outcome. A non-grantor trust pays 40.8% on income above roughly sixteen thousand dollars.

One caution alongside the arithmetic
A favorable situs governs the trust, not every asset it holds. As a general matter, the law of the state where real property physically sits controls its protection, so a trust formed in a favorable jurisdiction cannot shield property located in a state that voids self-settled protection. Structure follows the asset, not the letterhead.

Three points carry the piece
A trust does three separate jobs and you should know which one you are buying. Grantor status lets you pay the trust's income tax personally, which moves value to your beneficiaries without touching your exemption and is the most efficient transfer available. And situs is a decision worth making deliberately, because duration, privacy, creditor rules, and state income tax all turn on it.
How Farther approaches this
Trusts are drafted by attorneys and funded by advisors, and most failures happen in the gap between those two. Farther works directly alongside your estate attorney and CPA rather than handing off, models the thirty-year outcome before documents are signed, and manages what sits inside the trust once it is funded.
